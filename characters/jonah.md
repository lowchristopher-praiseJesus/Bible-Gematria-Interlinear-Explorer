## Who Jonah is

Jonah son of Amittai appears in 2 Kings 14:25 as a prophet under Jeroboam II, and is the central figure of the four-chapter book that carries his name (37 of his 42 links). The other four are in Matthew 12 and Luke 11 where Jesus calls Jonah's story "the sign of the prophet Jonas".

## What the verses tell us

**Who he was.** "He restored the coast of Israel from the entering of Hamath unto the sea of the plain, according to the word of the LORD God of Israel, which he spake by the hand of his servant Jonah, the son of Amittai, the prophet, which was of Gathhepher" (2 Kings 14:25).

**Running away.** "the word of the LORD came unto Jonah the son of Amittai, saying, Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and cry against it; for their wickedness is come up before me. But Jonah rose up to flee unto Tarshish from the presence of the LORD" (Jonah 1:1–3). He goes down to Joppa, pays the fare and boards a ship. In the storm "the mariners were afraid, and cried every man unto his god… But Jonah was gone down into the sides of the ship; and he lay, and was fast asleep" (1:5). The shipmaster wakes him: "What meanest thou, O sleeper? arise, call upon thy God" (1:6). The crew cast lots and "the lot fell upon Jonah" (1:7). He tells them "I am an Hebrew; and I fear the LORD, the God of heaven, which hath made the sea and the dry land" (1:9). "Then were the men exceedingly afraid, and said unto him, Why hast thou done this?" (1:10). Jonah's answer: "Take me up, and cast me forth into the sea; so shall the sea be calm unto you" (1:12). The sailors row hard first (1:13), then cry to the LORD "let us not perish for this man's life, and lay not upon us innocent blood" (1:14) and throw him overboard; "the sea ceased from her raging" (1:15).

**The fish.** "The LORD had prepared a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights" (1:17). His prayer: "I cried by reason of mine affliction unto the LORD, and he heard me; out of the belly of hell cried I" (2:2), "the waters compassed me about, even to the soul… the weeds were wrapped about my head" (2:5), "yet hast thou brought up my life from corruption, O LORD my God" (2:6), and "Salvation is of the LORD" (2:9). "The LORD spake unto the fish, and it vomited out Jonah upon the dry land" (2:10).

**Nineveh.** "The word of the LORD came unto Jonah the second time… Arise, go unto Nineveh" (3:1–2). "Jonah arose, and went… Now Nineveh was an exceeding great city of three days' journey" (3:3). He enters a day's journey and cries "Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown" (3:4). (The repentance of the city, 3:5–10, is about its people and king, and not linked to Jonah.)

**Anger and the gourd.** "But it displeased Jonah exceedingly, and he was very angry" (4:1). His prayer explains the flight: "I fled before unto Tarshish: for I knew that thou art a gracious God, and merciful, slow to anger, and of great kindness, and repentest thee of the evil" (4:2). "Take, I beseech thee, my life from me; for it is better for me to die than to live" (4:3). The LORD: "Doest thou well to be angry?" (4:4). Jonah builds a booth east of the city and sits in its shade (4:5). God "prepared a gourd" to shade him and Jonah "was exceeding glad of the gourd" (4:6); the next morning a worm kills it (4:7) and a hot east wind and the sun make him faint: "It is better for me to die than to live" (4:8). God: "Doest thou well to be angry for the gourd?" He said "I do well to be angry, even unto death" (4:9). The book ends: "Thou hast had pity on the gourd… And should not I spare Nineveh, that great city, wherein are more than sixscore thousand persons that cannot discern between their right hand and their left hand; and also much cattle?" (4:10–11).

**Jesus and the sign of Jonah.** "An evil and adulterous generation seeketh after a sign; and there shall no sign be given to it, but the sign of the prophet Jonas: For as Jonas was three days and three nights in the whale's belly; so shall the Son of man be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth" (Matthew 12:39–40; also Luke 11:29–30). "The men of Nineveh shall rise in judgment with this generation, and shall condemn it: because they repented at the preaching of Jonas; and, behold, a greater than Jonas is here" (Matthew 12:41; Luke 11:32).

## Relationships

- **Amittai**: father, of Gath-hepher (2 Kings 14:25). No wife or children are recorded.
- **The mariners** and **shipmaster**, and the **people and king of Nineveh**, are the other actors.
- **[Jesus](jesus.md)** (4 shared verses): the "greater than Jonas".
