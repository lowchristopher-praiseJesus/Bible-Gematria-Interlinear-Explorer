## Who Jeremiah is

This profile is **Jeremiah son of Hilkiah, the prophet**. 381 of his 434 links are in the book of Jeremiah and 48 in Lamentations. In both the "I" of the text is the prophet, which is how most of the links are made. Three verses are in 2 Chronicles and two in Matthew ("Jeremy the prophet"). Other men named Jeremiah (a priest in Nehemiah 10 and 12; 1 Chronicles 12; Jeremiah of Libnah, Hamutal's father in Jeremiah 52:1, which scored 0.19) have been kept out.

## What the verses tell us

**The call.** "The words of Jeremiah the son of Hilkiah, of the priests that were in Anathoth in the land of Benjamin: To whom the word of the LORD came in the days of Josiah the son of Amon king of Judah, in the thirteenth year of his reign" (Jeremiah 1:1–2). God: "Before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee; and before thou camest forth out of the womb I sanctified thee, and I ordained thee a prophet unto the nations" (1:5). Jeremiah objects "Ah, Lord GOD! behold, I cannot speak: for I am a child" (1:6). God: "Say not, I am a child… Be not afraid of their faces: for I am with thee to deliver thee" (1:7–8). "The LORD put forth his hand, and touched my mouth. And the LORD said unto me, Behold, I have put my words in thy mouth… I have this day set thee over the nations and over the kingdoms, to root out, and to pull down, and to destroy, and to throw down, to build, and to plant" (1:9–10). The first vision is "a rod of an almond tree" (1:11).

**The message and the heart behind it.** He speaks through the reigns of Josiah, Jehoiakim and Zedekiah (3:6; 27:1). His speeches call Judah "backsliding Israel" who "hath played the harlot" (3:6) and say "their ear is uncircumcised, and they cannot hearken" (6:10). Inside the messages the prophet's own voice breaks through: "Ah, Lord GOD! surely thou hast greatly deceived this people" (4:10); "Is there no balm in Gilead; is there no physician there?" (8:22); "Oh that my head were waters, and mine eyes a fountain of tears, that I might weep day and night for the slain of the daughter of my people!" (9:1). He is forbidden marriage: "Thou shalt not take thee a wife, neither shalt thou have sons or daughters in this place" (16:2). "Woe is me, my mother, that thou hast borne me a man of strife and a man of contention to the whole earth" (15:10). He complains: "Righteous art thou, O LORD… wherefore doth the way of the wicked prosper?" (12:1), and "O LORD, thou hast deceived me, and I was deceived… I am in derision daily" (20:7). He tried silence: "I will not make mention of him, nor speak any more in his name. But his word was in mine heart as a burning fire shut up in my bones, and I was weary with forbearing, and I could not stay" (20:9).

**Object lessons.** God tells him "Go and get thee a linen girdle" (13:1), "Arise, and go down to the potter's house" (18:2), and shows him two baskets of figs: "the good figs, very good; and the evil, very evil, that cannot be eaten" (24:3). At the temple Pashur "smote Jeremiah the prophet, and put him in the stocks" (20:2). After the temple sermon, the priests, prophets and people seized him: "Thou shalt surely die" (26:8). He stands up to the prophet Hananiah (28:5), writes a letter to the exiles in Babylon "whom Nebuchadnezzar had carried away captive" (29:1), and commends the Rechabites who kept their father's command (35:18).

**The scroll.** "Jeremiah called Baruch the son of Neriah, and Baruch wrote from the mouth of Jeremiah all the words of the LORD, which he had spoken unto him, upon a roll of a book" (36:4). (When King Jehoiakim burns the roll, 36:23, the verse is not linked to Jeremiah.) Baruch is given a word of his own in chapter 45.

**Prison and siege.** "The king of Babylon's army besieged Jerusalem: and Jeremiah the prophet was shut up in the court of the prison" (32:2), where "the word of the LORD came unto Jeremiah the second time" (33:1). In prison he buys his cousin Hanameel's field at Anathoth for seventeen shekels of silver (32:7–9) as a sign that land will again be bought. He speaks to King Zedekiah (34:6; 21:3) who, though he sends "Pray now unto the LORD our God for us" (37:3), lets the princes have their way: "let this man be put to death: for thus he weakeneth the hands of the men of war" (38:4–5). "Then took they Jeremiah, and cast him into the dungeon of Malchiah… And in the dungeon there was no water, but mire: so Jeremiah sunk in the mire" (38:6).

**After the fall.** Nebuchadnezzar "gave charge concerning Jeremiah to Nebuzaradan the captain of the guard, saying, Take him, and look well to him, and do him no harm" (39:11–12), and he is committed to Gedaliah (39:14). After the assassination of Gedaliah the remnant asks him to pray, then ignores the answer and goes to Egypt, taking "Jeremiah the prophet, and Baruch" with them (43:6–7). His last word to the Jews in Egypt is in 44:20. The book ends with the note that "Jeremiah wrote in a book all the evil that should come upon Babylon" (51:60).

**Lamentations.** The 48 links in Lamentations are the poem's "I": "Behold, O LORD; for I am in distress… there is none to comfort me" (1:20–21), "Mine eye runneth down with rivers of water for the destruction of the daughter of my people" (3:48–49). The traditional attribution to Jeremiah is not in the text itself. "The LORD's mercies… are new every morning" (3:22–23) is not linked (0.41 and 0.23).

**Outside his book.** "Zedekiah… humbled not himself before Jeremiah the prophet speaking from the mouth of the LORD" (2 Chronicles 36:12). Matthew 2:17 and 27:9 say "Then was fulfilled that which was spoken by Jeremy the prophet".

## Relationships

- **Hilkiah**: father, a priest of Anathoth.
- **Baruch son of Neriah**: his scribe. **Zedekiah**, **Jehoiakim** and **Josiah**: the kings. **Pashur**, **Hananiah**: opponents. **Gedaliah**: the governor after the fall; **Nebuzaradan** the Babylonian officer who treated him kindly.
- **[Nebuchadnezzar](nebuchadnezzar.md)** shares 18 verses (the siege, the letter to exiles, the release). **[Jesus](jesus.md)** and **[Judas Iscariot](judas_iscariot.md)** each share one verse with him, Matthew 27:9 (the thirty pieces of silver "spoken by Jeremy the prophet").

## Reading the data

- The best-known "comfort" passages (Jeremiah 29:10–11 at 0.29 and 0.16; the "new covenant" 31:31 at 0.15) are not linked to him. Only verses where he speaks or acts are.
- Lamentations is attributed here by tradition, and Jeremiah 48:32 (0.63) is one of the oracle verses where the speaker "I" may not be him.
- Misses: 2 Chronicles 36:22, Ezra 1:1 (both "by the mouth of Jeremiah"), Matthew 16:14, Jeremiah 36:10 and Daniel 9:2.
