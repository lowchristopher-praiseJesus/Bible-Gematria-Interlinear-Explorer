## Who Aaron is

Aaron is linked in 447 verses, almost all in Exodus (150), Leviticus (145) and Numbers (118). He is the human face of the priesthood: the person the sacrificial laws are addressed to and the one who wears the holy garments. Later books speak of "the house of Aaron" or "the sons of Aaron" as the priestly line.

## What the verses tell us

**Spokesman.** When Moses pleads that he is slow of speech, God says "Is not Aaron the Levite thy brother? I know that he can speak well… he shall be thy spokesman unto the people" (Exodus 4:14, 4:16). He meets Moses in the mount of God and kisses him (4:27). In Egypt "Aaron thy brother shall speak unto Pharaoh" (7:2). He stretches out his rod to bring the frogs (8:5). He and Moses go in to Pharaoh together (5:1, 10:3, 11:10), and "Moses and Aaron did all these wonders before Pharaoh" (11:10). Pharaoh calls for them saying "I have sinned this time" (9:27).

**Supporting Moses.** When Israel fights Amalek, "Aaron and Hur stayed up his hands, the one on the one side, and the other on the other side; and his hands were steady until the going down of the sun" (17:12). He goes up Sinai with Moses, Nadab, Abihu and seventy elders to worship "afar off" (24:1).

**Chosen as priest.** "Take thou unto thee Aaron thy brother, and his sons with him… that he may minister unto me in the priest's office, even Aaron, Nadab and Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar" (28:1). The holy garments ("a breastplate, and an ephod, and a robe, and a broidered coat, a mitre, and a girdle") are made "for glory and for beauty" (28:2–4). Aaron and his sons are washed at the tabernacle door (29:4, 40:12), Aaron burns sweet incense every morning (30:7) and keeps the lamps burning (27:21). Leviticus 8–9 records the consecration and his first offerings: "Take thee a young calf for a sin offering, and a ram for a burnt offering" (9:2). Much of the sacrificial law says that portions are "Aaron's and his sons'" (2:10, 7:31).

**The golden calf.** While Moses is on the mountain the people say to Aaron "Up, make us gods, which shall go before us" (32:1). He tells them to break off their gold earrings, fashions it "after he had made it a molten calf", and proclaims a feast (32:2–5). Confronted, he says "Let not the anger of my lord wax hot: thou knowest the people, that they are set on mischief… I cast it into the fire, and there came out this calf" (32:22–24). Deuteronomy 9:20: "the LORD was very angry with Aaron to have destroyed him: and I prayed for Aaron also the same time".

**Nadab and Abihu.** His two eldest sons "offered strange fire before the LORD, which he commanded them not", and "fire from the LORD… devoured them" (Leviticus 10:1–2). Moses says "I will be sanctified in them that come nigh me… And Aaron held his peace" (10:3).

**Rebellion.** Miriam and Aaron "spake against Moses because of the Ethiopian woman whom he had married" and ask "Hath the LORD indeed spoken only by Moses?" (Numbers 12:1–2). God summons the three and Miriam becomes leprous; Aaron confesses "we have done foolishly… we have sinned" and asks Moses to intervene (12:10–13, see [Miriam](miriam.md)). In Korah's revolt the challenge is "Ye take too much upon you… wherefore then lift ye up yourselves above the congregation" (16:3). Aaron then runs with the censer to stop a plague: he "stood between the dead and the living; and the plague was stayed" (16:47–48). The next day his rod alone "was budded… and yielded almonds" (17:8), the sign of God's choice.

**Excluded from the land, and death.** He shares Moses' sentence at the rock (Numbers 20:12, see [Moses](moses.md)). "Aaron the priest went up into mount Hor at the commandment of the LORD, and died there, in the fortieth year… And Aaron was an hundred and twenty and three years old when he died" (33:38–39). Deuteronomy 10:6: "there Aaron died, and there he was buried; and Eleazar his son ministered in the priest's office in his stead".

**The priestly line.** Joshua 21:4 gives "the children of Aaron the priest" thirteen cities; 1 Chronicles 23:13 says "Aaron was separated, that he should sanctify the most holy things, he and his sons for ever, to burn incense before the LORD". 2 Chronicles 13:9 calls the priests "the sons of Aaron". Psalms 115:12, 118:3, 135:19 speak of "the house of Aaron" praising God. Psalm 106:16: "Aaron the saint of the LORD". Micah 6:4: "I sent before thee Moses, Aaron, and Miriam." In Hebrews, Aaron is the pattern of a priest "called of God" (5:4), and Jesus is a priest "after the order of Melchisedec, and not… after the order of Aaron" (7:11).

## Relationships

- **[Moses](moses.md)**: younger brother, and 227 of Aaron's 447 verses are shared with him. **[Miriam](miriam.md)**: sister. Both parents are **Amram** and **Jochebed**.
- **Elisheba**: wife (Exodus 6:23). **Nadab and Abihu** died for offering unauthorised fire; **Eleazar** succeeded as high priest; **Ithamar** served too (Leviticus 10; Numbers 7:8).
- **Hur**: helped hold up Moses' arms; **Korah**: led the challenge to Aaron's priesthood.
- **[Joshua](joshua.md)** (2 shared verses) and **[Samuel](samuel.md)** (1 Samuel 12:8) appear alongside him as figures from Israel's past.

## Reading the data

- Many Leviticus links (0.5–0.7) are priestly instructions addressed to "the priest" or "the sons of Aaron" (Leviticus 1:7, Numbers 5:9, 7:8, 10:8); read them as the priesthood he founded, not scenes with Aaron himself. This is one of the known soft spots of the classification.
- Named but not linked (below 0.5): Exodus 15:20 (Miriam "the sister of Aaron"), Numbers 3:1, 16:40, Psalm 133:2 ("Aaron's beard"), Hebrews 7:11.
