## Who Joseph is

This profile is **Joseph son of Jacob**, not Joseph the husband of Mary (see [Joseph (Husband of Mary)](joseph_husband.md)). Genesis 30–50 holds 296 of his 345 links, and the Genesis 37–50 narrative is the longest single life story in the Bible after Moses. From Exodus on, "Joseph" is often a way of saying the tribes of Ephraim and Manasseh, "the house of Joseph".

## What the verses tell us

**Birth.** Rachel, long barren, "called his name Joseph; and said, The LORD shall add to me another son" (Genesis 30:24). In Jacob's march to meet Esau, Rachel and Joseph are put "hindermost" as the most protected (33:2).

**Favourite and dreamer.** At seventeen he feeds the flock with his brothers and "brought unto his father their evil report" (37:2). "Israel loved Joseph more than all his children, because he was the son of his old age: and he made him a coat of many colours" (37:3). His brothers "hated him, and could not speak peaceably unto him" (37:4), and the dreams of sheaves and stars bowing to him make them hate him "yet the more" (37:5).

**Sold.** Sent by his father to check on the flocks at Shechem (37:13), he is thrown into a pit and "sold Joseph to the Ishmeelites for twenty pieces of silver" and taken to Egypt (37:28). Later, brothers admit "we saw the anguish of his soul, when he besought us, and we would not hear" (42:21).

**Potiphar and prison.** Potiphar, "an officer of Pharaoh, captain of the guard", buys him (39:1). "The LORD was with Joseph, and he was a prosperous man" (39:2). Potiphar's wife says "Lie with me" (39:7); Joseph refuses: "how then can I do this great wickedness, and sin against God?" (39:9). He is put in the king's prison, and "the LORD was with Joseph, and shewed him mercy" (39:20–21). There he interprets the dreams of Pharaoh's butler and baker: "Do not interpretations belong to God?" (40:8).

**Before Pharaoh.** Called "hastily out of the dungeon" (41:14) he tells Pharaoh "It is not in me: God shall give Pharaoh an answer of peace" (41:16). His interpretation of the dreams is seven years of plenty and seven of famine (41:29–30). Pharaoh: "there is none so discreet and wise as thou art… See, I have set thee over all the land of Egypt" (41:39–41); he gives Joseph a ring, fine linen, a gold chain and the second chariot (41:42–43), the name Zaphnathpaaneah, and Asenath "daughter of Potipherah priest of On" as wife (41:45). "Joseph was thirty years old when he stood before Pharaoh" (41:46). He stores grain "as the sand of the sea" (41:49), and when the famine comes Pharaoh says "Go unto Joseph; what he saith to you, do" (41:55).

**His brothers.** They come to buy food, "bowed down themselves before him with their faces to the earth"; "Joseph knew his brethren, but they knew not him" (42:6–8). He tests them, above all with Benjamin (43:16, 44:4–15), and weeps privately (43:30). When Judah offers himself, "Joseph could not refrain himself" and clears the room (45:1): "I am Joseph; doth my father yet live?… I am Joseph your brother, whom ye sold into Egypt" (45:3–4). His reading of it: "God did send me before you to preserve life… it was not you that sent me hither, but God" (45:5–8).

**Family in Egypt.** He goes up in his chariot to Goshen and "fell on his neck, and wept on his neck a good while" (46:29). He presents his father and brothers to Pharaoh (47:1, 47:7). He brings his sons Ephraim and Manasseh to be blessed (48:9–13). Jacob's blessing of him: "Joseph is a fruitful bough, even a fruitful bough by a well" and "the archers have sorely grieved him" (49:22–23); and he is "separate from his brethren" (49:26).

**Death and bones.** After burying and embalming his father (50:1–2), he reassures his brothers: "ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good, to bring to pass… to save much people alive" (50:20). He dies at 110 and had them swear "ye shall carry up my bones from hence" (50:24–26). Exodus 13:19: "Moses took the bones of Joseph with him"; Joshua 24:32 buries them at Shechem "in a parcel of ground which Jacob bought".

**The tribes and later references.**
- Exodus 1:5–6: "Joseph was in Egypt already… And Joseph died, and all his brethren, and all that generation".
- Deuteronomy 33:13–16: Moses' blessing of Joseph's land.
- Joshua 16–18, Judges 1:22: "the house of Joseph" (Ephraim and Manasseh) takes its lot and fights at Bethel.
- Ezekiel 37:16–19: the "stick of Joseph, which is in the hand of Ephraim" is joined to Judah's; Ezekiel 47:13: "Joseph shall have two portions".
- Psalm 105:17–19: "He sent a man before them, even Joseph, who was sold for a servant".
- Amos 5:6, 5:15, Obadiah 1:18, Zechariah 10:6: "the house of Joseph" as the northern kingdom.
- Acts 7:9–14: "the patriarchs, moved with envy, sold Joseph into Egypt: but God was with him".
- Hebrews 11:22: "By faith Joseph, when he died, made mention of the departing of the children of Israel; and gave commandment concerning his bones."
- Revelation 7:8: "Of the tribe of Joseph were sealed twelve thousand."

## Relationships

- **[Jacob](jacob.md)** (Israel): father, with 101 shared verses. **Rachel**: mother; she dies bearing his brother **Benjamin** (35:18–19).
- **Brothers**: Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Dan, Naphtali, Gad, Asher, Issachar, Zebulun (half-brothers), Benjamin (full brother), and sister Dinah. Judah and Reuben are the two who speak up for him in the pit and before him in Egypt.
- **Asenath**: his Egyptian wife; sons **Manasseh** and **Ephraim**.
- **Potiphar** and his wife; the butler and baker; **Pharaoh**.
- **[Moses](moses.md)** and **[Joshua](joshua.md)** share a few verses: the bones (Exodus 13:19) and the tribal land (Joshua 17).

## Reading the data

Genesis 37–50 is very clean. Outside Genesis, roughly a third of the links (Joshua, Judges, 1 Chronicles, Psalms, Ezekiel, Amos, Obadiah, Zechariah) refer to "Joseph" as the tribes of Ephraim and Manasseh, with scores of 0.5–0.7. TIPNR lists 26 named verses the classifier missed (mainly "sons of Joseph" tribal counts in Numbers and Joshua). The two "Josephs" are kept clearly apart by the classifier: this profile has no Gospel links.
