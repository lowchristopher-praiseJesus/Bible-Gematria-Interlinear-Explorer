## Who Mary Magdalene is

This profile is **Mary called Magdalene**. Her verses are in the passion and resurrection accounts of all four Gospels (Matthew 12, Mark 13, Luke 21, John 11 links), plus one earlier mention in Luke 8:2. Of the Bible's several Marys, she is kept apart from Mary the mother of Jesus and from Mary the mother of James and Joses (the "other Mary"). The links also include some scenes where an *unnamed* woman anoints Jesus; see "Reading the data".

## What the verses tell us

**Healed.** Luke names "certain women, which had been healed of evil spirits and infirmities, Mary called Magdalene, out of whom went seven devils" (Luke 8:2). Mark repeats "Mary Magdalene, out of whom he had cast seven devils" when he introduces her as the first witness (Mark 16:9). Mark 15:41 (linked at 0.62) says of the women at the cross that "when he was in Galilee, followed him, and ministered unto him".

**At the cross.** "Among which was Mary Magdalene, and Mary the mother of James and Joses, and the mother of Zebedee's children" (Matthew 27:56); "women looking on afar off: among whom was Mary Magdalene, and Mary the mother of James the less and of Joses, and Salome" (Mark 15:40). John: "Now there stood by the cross of Jesus his mother, and his mother's sister, Mary the wife of Cleophas, and Mary Magdalene" (19:25). Luke has "all his acquaintance, and the women that followed him from Galilee, stood afar off, beholding these things" (23:49).

**At the tomb (burial).** "Mary Magdalene, and the other Mary, sitting over against the sepulchre" (Matthew 27:61); "Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of Joses beheld where he was laid" (Mark 15:47); "the women also, which came with him from Galilee, followed after, and beheld the sepulchre, and how his body was laid. And they returned, and prepared spices and ointments; and rested the sabbath day according to the commandment" (Luke 23:55–56).

**Easter morning.** In Matthew, "in the end of the sabbath, as it began to dawn toward the first day of the week, came Mary Magdalene and the other Mary to see the sepulchre" (28:1). The angel tells the women "Fear not ye: for I know that ye seek Jesus, which was crucified. He is not here: for he is risen, as he said. Come, see the place where the Lord lay. And go quickly, and tell his disciples that he is risen from the dead" (28:5–7). They run "with fear and great joy" and "behold, Jesus met them, saying, All hail. And they came and held him by the feet, and worshipped him. Then said Jesus unto them, Be not afraid: go tell my brethren that they go into Galilee" (28:8–10). In Mark, "Mary Magdalene, and Mary the mother of James, and Salome, had bought sweet spices, that they might come and anoint him" (16:1); on the way they ask "Who shall roll us away the stone from the door of the sepulchre?" (16:3) and find it rolled away (16:4). Mark's longer ending: "he appeared first to Mary Magdalene, out of whom he had cast seven devils. And she went and told them that had been with him, as they mourned and wept. And they, when they had heard that he was alive, and had been seen of her, believed not" (16:9–11). In Luke the women find the stone rolled away and "two men stood by them in shining garments": "Why seek ye the living among the dead?" (24:2–5). "It was Mary Magdalene, and Joanna, and Mary the mother of James, and other women that were with them, which told these things unto the apostles. And their words seemed to them as idle tales, and they believed them not" (24:10–11).

**In John.** "The first day of the week cometh Mary Magdalene early, when it was yet dark, unto the sepulchre, and seeth the stone taken away" (20:1). She runs "to Simon Peter, and to the other disciple, whom Jesus loved" with "They have taken away the LORD out of the sepulchre, and we know not where they have laid him" (20:2). After they leave she stays, "weeping", and looks in: two angels ask "Woman, why weepest thou?" and she answers "Because they have taken away my LORD, and I know not where they have laid him" (20:11–13). She turns and sees Jesus "and knew not that it was Jesus. Jesus saith unto her, Woman, why weepest thou? whom seekest thou? She, supposing him to be the gardener, saith unto him, Sir, if thou have borne him hence, tell me where thou hast laid him" (20:14–15). "Jesus saith unto her, Mary. She turned herself, and saith unto him, Rabboni; which is to say, Master" (20:16). Jesus: "Touch me not; for I am not yet ascended to my Father: but go to my brethren, and say unto them, I ascend unto my Father, and your Father; and to my God, and your God" (20:17). "Mary Magdalene came and told the disciples that she had seen the LORD, and that he had spoken these things unto her" (20:18).

## Reading the data

- **Two kinds of link are open to question.** (1) *The woman who anoints Jesus.* Matthew 26:10–13, Mark 14:6–9 (0.53–0.59) and Luke 7:36–50 (the sinful woman at Simon's house, 0.5–0.7) are linked to her, although those verses do not name the woman. John 12:3 names the anointing woman as Mary of Bethany (the sister of Lazarus), and none of the Gospels identifies the Luke 7 woman as Mary Magdalene; that identification is a later tradition, not stated in the text. (2) *Group scenes.* Matthew 28:6–10, Mark 16:4–5 and Luke 23:49–24:11 are linked (0.5–0.7) because Mary Magdalene is among "the women" there.
- The core of the profile is the named verses: Luke 8:2, the cross, the burial and John 20. All 14 verses TIPNR marks with her name are captured.
- **Namesake note.** After the description was extended to say she is not Mary of Bethany, John 12:3 (Mary anointing Jesus' feet) dropped to 0.09 and is no longer linked to her.
