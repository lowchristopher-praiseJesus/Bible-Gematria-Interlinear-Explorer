## Who Sarah is

Sarah is the first matriarch of Israel. Her story is Genesis 11–25 (63 of her 72 linked verses); the New Testament remembers her as a woman of faith (Hebrews 11:11), an example of obedient wives (1 Peter 3:6) and the mother of the child of promise (Romans 9:9, Galatians 4).

## What the verses tell us

**Sarai, barren.** "The name of Abram's wife was Sarai" and "Sarai was barren; she had no child" (Genesis 11:29–30). She leaves Ur with Terah, Abram and Lot, and goes on to Canaan (11:31; 12:5).

**Taken twice, called "sister".** Entering Egypt, Abram tells her "Say, I pray thee, thou art my sister: that it may be well with me for thy sake" (12:13). Pharaoh's princes praise her and "the woman was taken into Pharaoh's house" (12:15); the LORD "plagued Pharaoh and his house with great plagues because of Sarai" and she is returned (12:17–20). The pattern is repeated at Gerar: Abraham says "She is my sister… and Abimelech king of Gerar sent, and took Sarah" (20:2). God warns Abimelech in a dream, "she is a man's wife" (20:3). Abraham's later explanation is that "she is the daughter of my father, but not the daughter of my mother; and she became my wife" (20:12): she was his half-sister. Abimelech gives Abraham sheep, oxen, servants and a thousand pieces of silver "to thy brother", and "the LORD had fast closed up all the wombs of the house of Abimelech, because of Sarah" (20:14–18).

**Hagar and Ishmael.** Because "the LORD hath restrained me from bearing", Sarai gives her Egyptian maid Hagar to Abram (16:1–3). When Hagar conceives and "her mistress was despised in her eyes" (16:4), Sarai blames Abram; he answers "thy maid is in thy hand", and Sarai "dealt hardly with her" so that Hagar fled (16:5–6, 16:8–9).

**The name change and the promise.** God says "thou shalt not call her name Sarai, but Sarah shall her name be. And I will bless her… she shall be a mother of nations; kings of people shall be of her" (17:15–16). Abraham falls on his face and laughs at the thought of a birth at ninety (17:17); the covenant will be with Isaac, "which Sarah shall bear unto thee at this set time in the next year" (17:19, 17:21).

**The laugh at the tent door.** Three visitors ask "Where is Sarah thy wife?" and one says "Sarah thy wife shall have a son. And Sarah heard it in the tent door" (18:9–10). "It ceased to be with Sarah after the manner of women", so "Sarah laughed within herself" (18:11–12). The LORD asks "Is any thing too hard for the LORD?" (18:14), and Sarah denies laughing "for she was afraid" (18:15).

**Isaac.** "The LORD visited Sarah as he had said" (21:1). She conceived and bore "in his old age" (21:2). Sarah says "God hath made me to laugh, so that all that hear will laugh with me. … Who would have said unto Abraham, that Sarah should have given children suck?" (21:6–7). Then she sees Hagar's son "mocking" and demands "Cast out this bondwoman and her son: for the son of this bondwoman shall not be heir with my son, even with Isaac" (21:9–10). God tells Abraham "in all that Sarah hath said unto thee, hearken unto her voice: for in Isaac shall thy seed be called" (21:12).

**Death and burial.** "Sarah was an hundred and seven and twenty years old" (23:1). She "died in Kirjatharba; the same is Hebron", and Abraham "came to mourn for Sarah, and to weep for her" (23:2). He buries her in the cave of Machpelah before Mamre (23:19); Abraham himself is buried there "and Sarah his wife" (25:10), and later Isaac and Rebekah and Leah (49:31).

**In the rest of the Bible.**
- *Isaiah 51:2:* "Look unto Abraham your father, and unto Sarah that bare you".
- *Romans 4:19; 9:9:* "the deadness of Sarah's womb"; "At this time will I come, and Sarah shall have a son".
- *Galatians 4:22–30:* Paul's allegory of the two sons, "the one by a bondmaid, the other by a freewoman", ending "Cast out the bondwoman and her son".
- *Hebrews 11:11:* "Through faith also Sara herself received strength to conceive seed… because she judged him faithful who had promised."
- *1 Peter 3:6:* "Even as Sara obeyed Abraham, calling him lord: whose daughters ye are".

## Relationships

- **[Abraham](abraham.md)**: husband and half-brother (Genesis 20:12). They share 59 of Sarah's 72 linked verses.
- **[Isaac](isaac.md)**: her only child. **Ishmael** is Abraham's son by her maid **Hagar**; the two women are the "bondwoman" and "freewoman" of Galatians 4.
- **[Lot](lot.md)**: Abraham's nephew who travelled with them (Genesis 11:31, 12:5).
- **Terah** is her father (TIPNR); **Nahor** and **Haran** are her brothers.
- **Pharaoh** and **Abimelech** are the two kings who took her.
