## Who Lazarus is

All 26 links are in John 11–12. This is **Lazarus of Bethany**. The Lazarus of Jesus' parable in Luke 16 (the beggar "full of sores" at the rich man's gate) is a different person and is not linked here (Luke 16:20 scored 0.00; the fix for that namesake removed two verses). Lazarus himself speaks no recorded words.

## What the verses tell us

**Sick.** "Now a certain man was sick, named Lazarus, of Bethany, the town of Mary and her sister Martha. (It was that Mary which anointed the Lord with ointment, and wiped his feet with her hair, whose brother Lazarus was sick.)" (John 11:1–2). The sisters send: "Lord, behold, he whom thou lovest is sick" (11:3). "Now Jesus loved Martha, and her sister, and Lazarus" (11:5). Yet "when he had heard therefore that he was sick, he abode two days still in the same place where he was" (11:6).

**Dead.** "Our friend Lazarus sleepeth; but I go, that I may awake him out of sleep" (11:11). The disciples answer "Lord, if he sleep, he shall do well" (11:12), so "Then said Jesus unto them plainly, Lazarus is dead. And I am glad for your sakes that I was not there, to the intent ye may believe; nevertheless let us go unto him" (11:14–15). Thomas says "Let us also go, that we may die with him" (11:16). "When Jesus came, he found that he had lain in the grave four days already" (11:17).

**The sisters.** Martha meets him: "Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died" (11:21). Jesus: "Thy brother shall rise again" (11:23), and Martha, "I know that he shall rise again in the resurrection at the last day" (11:24). Mary, when she comes, falls at his feet with the same words (11:32). "Jesus wept. Then said the Jews, Behold how he loved him!" (11:35–36). Some ask "Could not this man, which opened the eyes of the blind, have caused that even this man should not have died?" (11:37).

**The tomb.** "Jesus therefore again groaning in himself cometh to the grave. It was a cave, and a stone lay upon it" (11:38). "Take ye away the stone." Martha protests "Lord, by this time he stinketh: for he hath been dead four days" (11:39). They remove the stone and Jesus thanks the Father (11:41). "He cried with a loud voice, Lazarus, come forth. And he that was dead came forth, bound hand and foot with graveclothes: and his face was bound about with a napkin. Jesus saith unto them, Loose him, and let him go" (11:43–44).

**The supper at Bethany.** "Jesus six days before the passover came to Bethany, where Lazarus was, which had been dead, whom he raised from the dead. There they made him a supper; and Martha served: but Lazarus was one of them that sat at the table with him" (12:1–2). "Much people of the Jews therefore knew that he was there: and they came not for Jesus' sake only, but that they might see Lazarus also, whom he had raised from the dead" (12:9). "But the chief priests consulted that they might put Lazarus also to death; because that by reason of him many of the Jews went away, and believed on Jesus" (12:10–11). At the entry to Jerusalem, "the people therefore that was with him when he called Lazarus out of his grave, and raised him from the dead, bare record" (12:17).

## Relationships

- **[Jesus](jesus.md)**: the friend who raises him (24 shared verses). **Martha** and **Mary** (of Bethany): his sisters, and the home where Jesus stays (Luke 10:38–42 gives their first appearance, not linked to him here). Mary is the one who anoints Jesus' feet (John 12:3, not Mary Magdalene).
- **Thomas** and the disciples are with Jesus when he hears the news; **the chief priests** plan his death.
- No wife or children are recorded. TIPNR notes an unnamed father.

## Reading the data

- Very clean. 26 of 26 are John 11–12. Weaker links (0.5–0.7): John 11:6, 11:12, 11:15, 11:32, 11:37 and 11:41, verses where the text is about Jesus or the mourners around the tomb.
- **Not linked:** the Sanhedrin's plotting in John 11:47–53 and the crowd verses (11:45–46, 12:12).
- The beggar Lazarus of Luke 16:20–25 is correctly excluded, and is the reason the description says "not the beggar Lazarus".
