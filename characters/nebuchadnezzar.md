## Who Nebuchadnezzar is

Nebuchadnezzar II (also spelled Nebuchadrezzar in the KJV, especially in Jeremiah and Ezekiel) reigned over Babylon from 605 to 562 BC. He appears in 302 verses: 111 in Daniel, 100 in Jeremiah, 33 in Ezekiel, 20 in 2 Kings, and a few in Chronicles, Ezra, Isaiah and Habakkuk. In Kings, Jeremiah and Ezekiel he is a conqueror described from Judah's side; in Daniel he is a character with dreams and speeches.

## What the verses tell us

**First invasion.** "In his days Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came up, and Jehoiakim became his servant three years: then he turned and rebelled against him" (2 Kings 24:1). "In the third year of the reign of Jehoiakim king of Judah came Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon unto Jerusalem, and besieged it" (Daniel 1:1). Against Jehoiakim "came up Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, and bound him in fetters, to carry him to Babylon" (2 Chronicles 36:6); "Nebuchadnezzar also carried of the vessels of the house of the LORD to Babylon, and put them in his temple at Babylon" (36:7).

**Jehoiachin and Zedekiah.** His servants besiege Jerusalem; "Jehoiachin the king of Judah went out to the king of Babylon… and the king of Babylon took him in the eighth year of his reign" (2 Kings 24:10–12). He carries away "all Jerusalem, and all the princes, and all the mighty men of valour, even ten thousand captives, and all the craftsmen and smiths: none remained, save the poorest sort" (24:14), takes the king, his mother, wives and officers (24:15), and "made Mattaniah his father's brother king in his stead, and changed his name to Zedekiah" (24:17; 2 Chronicles 36:10). Zedekiah "rebelled against king Nebuchadnezzar, who had made him swear by God: but he stiffened his neck, and hardened his heart" (36:13).

**The fall of Jerusalem.** "In the ninth year of his reign, in the tenth month, in the tenth day of the month… Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came, he, and all his host, against Jerusalem, and pitched against it; and they built forts against it round about" (2 Kings 25:1; Jeremiah 52:4; 39:1). Later texts remember it: "he gave them into the hand of Nebuchadnezzar the king of Babylon, the Chaldean, who destroyed this house, and carried the people away into Babylon" (Ezra 5:12); "Jehozadak went into captivity, when the LORD carried away Judah and Jerusalem by the hand of Nebuchadnezzar" (1 Chronicles 6:15). Cyrus later brings out "the vessels of the house of the LORD, which Nebuchadnezzar had brought forth out of Jerusalem" (Ezra 1:7).

**As God's "servant" in the prophets.** Jeremiah says God will "take all the families of the north… and Nebuchadrezzar the king of Babylon, my servant" (Jeremiah 25:9), and "And now have I given all these lands into the hand of Nebuchadnezzar the king of Babylon, my servant" (27:6). "I have put a yoke of iron upon the neck of all these nations, that they may serve Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon" (28:14). He is to punish false prophets (29:21), take Jerusalem (32:28), Zedekiah's household (38:23) and Egypt (43:10, 46:26), and threatens Hazor (49:30). Jeremiah 50:17 and 51:34 give the sufferer's view: "this Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon hath broken his bones", "hath devoured me, he hath crushed me, he hath made me an empty vessel". Jeremiah 39:11–12 records that he "gave charge concerning Jeremiah… Take him, and look well to him, and do him no harm". Ezekiel sees him coming against Tyre, "a king of kings, from the north" (26:7), and Ezekiel 21:21 pictures him at the fork in the road using arrows, images and liver-divination. Ezekiel 29:18–20: Tyre gave him no wages, "therefore… I will give the land of Egypt unto Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon". Isaiah 14:4 ("How hath the oppressor ceased!") and Habakkuk 1:11 and 2:6 are the taunts linked to him by the classifier; they are about "the king of Babylon" in general.

**The dream of the image.** "In the second year of the reign of Nebuchadnezzar Nebuchadnezzar dreamed dreams, wherewith his spirit was troubled" (Daniel 2:1). He calls his magicians, astrologers, sorcerers and Chaldeans (2:2). Daniel tells him "Thou, O king, art a king of kings: for the God of heaven hath given thee a kingdom, power, and strength, and glory… Thou art this head of gold" (2:37–38). The king "fell upon his face, and worshipped Daniel" (2:46).

**The golden image and the furnace.** "Nebuchadnezzar the king made an image of gold, whose height was threescore cubits, and the breadth thereof six cubits: he set it up in the plain of Dura" (3:1). Everyone must fall down when the music sounds (3:5). "Then Nebuchadnezzar in his rage and fury commanded to bring Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego" (3:13); "Is it true… do not ye serve my gods, nor worship the golden image which I have set up?" (3:14). "Full of fury" he has the furnace heated seven times hotter (3:19). Then "Nebuchadnezzar the king was astonied, and rose up in haste… Lo, I see four men loose, walking in the midst of the fire, and they have no hurt; and the form of the fourth is like the Son of God" (3:24–25). He blesses "the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, who hath sent his angel, and delivered his servants that trusted in him" (3:28) and decrees death for anyone who speaks against that God (3:29).

**The tree, the madness, the return.** He writes to "all people, nations, and languages… I thought it good to shew the signs and wonders that the high God hath wrought toward me" (4:1–2). He tells Daniel his dream of a great tree and asks "declare the interpretation" because the spirit of the holy gods is in Belteshazzar (4:8–9, 4:18). A year later, walking in the palace, he says "Is not this great Babylon, that I have built for the house of the kingdom by the might of my power, and for the honour of my majesty?" (4:29–30). "While the word was in the king's mouth, there fell a voice from heaven… The kingdom is departed from thee" (4:31); "the same hour was the thing fulfilled upon Nebuchadnezzar: and he was driven from men, and did eat grass as oxen, and his body was wet with the dew of heaven, till his hairs were grown like eagles' feathers, and his nails like birds' claws" (4:33). Daniel later reminds Belshazzar: "the most high God gave Nebuchadnezzar thy father a kingdom… when his heart was lifted up, and his mind hardened in pride, he was deposed from his kingly throne" (5:18–21). The king's restoration and praise (4:34–37: "I Nebuchadnezzar praise and extol and honour the King of heaven") are not in the linked set.

## Relationships

- **Belshazzar**: named as his "son" in Daniel 5 (the text calls Nebuchadnezzar his "father"). TIPNR lists him as his only child.
- **[Daniel](daniel.md)** (35 shared verses), **Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego**, **Arioch**: the men who serve or face him in Daniel.
- **[Jeremiah](jeremiah.md)** (18 shared verses): the prophet who calls him "my servant" and who is spared by his order. **Jehoiakim, Jehoiachin, Zedekiah**: the Judean kings he deals with. **Nebuzaradan**: his captain of the guard (2 Kings 25:8, Jeremiah 39:11).
- **Cyrus** later returns the temple vessels he took.

## Reading the data

- Daniel chapter 4's ending (4:34–37) and 1:1–2 fall below 0.5, along with several verses on the destruction (2 Kings 25:7–10, 0.10–0.25: the actors are "the Chaldees").
- Ezekiel 31:11 (0.66), Isaiah 37:25 (0.52), Isaiah 14:4 and Habakkuk 1:11, 2:6 are links to unnamed "king of Babylon" figures that are only *read* as him.
- Of the 88 verses that name him in TIPNR, eight fall below 0.5 (e.g. 2 Kings 25:8, Esther 2:6, Jeremiah 52:12, 52:30, Daniel 5:2).
