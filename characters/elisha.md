# Elisha

*Elijah's successor: the farmer called by a cloak, who asked for a double portion of Elijah's spirit and worked miracles of provision, healing and resurrection over about fifty years of Israel's kings.*

## Who Elisha is

Elisha's story is 2 Kings 2–13 (136 of his 143 links), with his call in 1 Kings 19 and a brief look back in Luke 4:27. He is the only tracked character whose whole biography sits inside 2 Kings. Most of what he does is small and local: a widow's oil, a Shunammite's son, a lost axe-head.

## What the verses tell us

**Called by a mantle.** God tells Elijah to anoint "Elisha the son of Shaphat of Abelmeholah… to be prophet in thy room" (1 Kings 19:16). Elijah "found Elisha the son of Shaphat, who was plowing with twelve yoke of oxen before him, and he with the twelfth: and Elijah passed by him, and cast his mantle upon him" (19:19). Elisha "left the oxen, and ran after Elijah, and said, Let me, I pray thee, kiss my father and my mother, and then I will follow thee" (19:20).

**Double portion.** He stays with Elijah on the last journey: "As the LORD liveth, and as thy soul liveth, I will not leave thee" (2 Kings 2:2, 2:4). The sons of the prophets tell him "Knowest thou that the LORD will take away thy master from thy head to day?" and he answers "Yea, I know it; hold ye your peace" (2:3, 2:5). He asks "let a double portion of thy spirit be upon me" (2:9). He sees the chariot of fire (2:11–12), picks up Elijah's mantle, strikes the Jordan and says "Where is the LORD God of Elijah?", and the waters part (2:13–14). "The spirit of Elijah doth rest on Elisha," say the prophets at Jericho (2:15).

**Bethel.** At Bethel "little children out of the city… mocked him, and said unto him, Go up, thou bald head" and he "cursed them in the name of the LORD. And there came forth two she bears out of the wood, and tare forty and two children of them" (2:23–24).

**Kings and war.** Jehoshaphat asks "Is there not here a prophet of the LORD?" and a servant says "Here is Elisha the son of Shaphat, which poured water on the hands of Elijah" (3:11). Elisha tells the king of Israel "What have I to do with thee? get thee to the prophets of thy father, and to the prophets of thy mother", but because of Jehoshaphat asks for a minstrel, "the hand of the LORD came upon him" (3:13–15). In 6:17–20 he prays that his servant's eyes be opened so he sees "the mountain… full of horses and chariots of fire round about Elisha", has the Syrian raiders struck with blindness, leads them to Samaria and has their sight restored (6:18–20). He predicts an end to the siege: "Thus saith the LORD, To morrow about this time shall a measure of fine flour be sold for a shekel… in the gate of Samaria" (7:1); the doubting royal officer "shalt see it with thine eyes, but shalt not eat thereof" (7:2), and is trampled at the gate (7:17–19). He tells the Shunammite woman to leave for seven years because of the famine (8:1). At Damascus, Benhadad is sick; Elisha tells Hazael "Thou mayest certainly recover: howbeit the LORD hath shewed me that he shall surely die" and that "thou shalt be king over Syria" (8:7–13). He sends a young prophet with a box of oil to anoint Jehu (9:1–6).

**Small miracles.** A prophet's widow with only "a pot of oil" is told what to do to pay a creditor who would take her two sons (4:1–2). A wealthy Shunammite woman feeds him whenever he passes, and builds him a chamber (4:8, 4:10–11). He promises her a son "about this season, according to the time of life" and she says "do not lie unto thine handmaid" (4:16); she conceives (4:17). When the child dies, Elisha "shut the door upon them twain, and prayed unto the LORD", stretches himself upon the boy and "the child sneezed seven times, and the child opened his eyes" (4:33–35), and tells her "Take up thy son" (4:36). In a famine he has the pottage of the prophets made safe (4:38). Naaman, a leprous Syrian commander, is told "Go and wash in Jordan seven times, and thy flesh shall come again to thee, and thou shalt be clean" (5:10); he does so and "his flesh came again like unto the flesh of a little child" (5:14) and he says "there is no God in all the earth, but in Israel" (5:15). Elisha's servant Gehazi is caught taking gifts: "Whence comest thou, Gehazi?… Is it a time to receive money?" (5:25–26). An axe-head lost in the Jordan floats when he throws in a stick: "the iron did swim" (6:5–7).

**Death.** "Elisha was fallen sick of his sickness whereof he died. And Joash the king of Israel came down unto him, and wept over his face, and said, O my father, my father, the chariot of Israel" (13:14), echoing Elisha's own cry for Elijah. From his sickbed Elisha has the king shoot an arrow east: "The arrow of the LORD's deliverance… thou shalt smite the Syrians in Aphek" (13:15–17). "Elisha died, and they buried him" (13:20). Later a dead man is thrown into his tomb, "and when the man was let down, and touched the bones of Elisha, he revived, and stood up on his feet" (13:21).

**Luke 4:27.** Jesus: "many lepers were in Israel in the time of Eliseus the prophet; and none of them was cleansed, saving Naaman the Syrian."
