## Who Daniel is

Daniel appears almost entirely in the book that carries his name (143 of 146 links). He is also named by Ezekiel, alongside Noah and Job, as a model of righteousness (Ezekiel 14:14, 14:20). The book has two halves: court stories in Babylon (Daniel 1–6), told in the third person, and visions (7–12), told in the first person by "I Daniel".

## What the verses tell us

**Taken to Babylon.** Nebuchadnezzar orders "certain of the children of Israel, and of the king's seed" who are without blemish and quick to learn to be trained for three years (Daniel 1:3–5). "Now among these were of the children of Judah, Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah" (1:6). The chief eunuch renames them: "he gave unto Daniel the name of Belteshazzar; and to Hananiah, of Shadrach; and to Mishael, of Meshach; and to Azariah, of Abednego" (1:7).

**The test of diet.** "Daniel purposed in his heart that he would not defile himself with the portion of the king's meat, nor with the wine which he drank" (1:8). The official fears for his head if they look worse (1:10), so Daniel asks their overseer Melzar: "Prove thy servants, I beseech thee, ten days; and let them give us pulse to eat, and water to drink" (1:12). After ten days "their countenances appeared fairer and fatter in flesh than all the children which did eat the portion of the king's meat" (1:15). "God gave them knowledge and skill in all learning and wisdom: and Daniel had understanding in all visions and dreams" (1:17). The king finds "none like Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah… ten times better than all the magicians and astrologers" (1:19–20). "Daniel continued even unto the first year of king Cyrus" (1:21).

**The dream of the image.** When Nebuchadnezzar demands that his wise men tell him both his dream and its meaning, Daniel "answered with counsel and wisdom to Arioch the captain of the king's guard, which was gone forth to slay the wise men of Babylon" (2:14). He asks for time (2:16), tells his three companions (2:17), and "Then was the secret revealed unto Daniel in a night vision. Then Daniel blessed the God of heaven" (2:19). He tells the king "there is a God in heaven that revealeth secrets" (2:28), describes the great image with a head of gold, and says of Nebuchadnezzar "Thou art this head of gold" (2:31–38). The king "fell upon his face, and worshipped Daniel" and says "your God is a God of gods, and a Lord of kings, and a revealer of secrets" (2:46–47). Daniel is made "ruler over the whole province of Babylon, and chief of the governors over all the wise men", and asks that Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego be given the provincial affairs while he sits in the gate (2:48–49).

**The fiery furnace.** Chapter 3 (Nebuchadnezzar's gold image and the three friends in the furnace) does not mention Daniel and is not linked to him. He is absent from the story.

**The second dream.** Nebuchadnezzar says "Daniel came in before me, whose name was Belteshazzar… O Belteshazzar, master of the magicians… tell me the visions of my dream" (4:8–9). "Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, was astonied for one hour" and answered "My lord, the dream be to them that hate thee, and the interpretation thereof to thine enemies" (4:19).

**The writing on the wall.** At Belshazzar's feast, the queen recalls Daniel: "an excellent spirit, and knowledge, and understanding, interpreting of dreams, and shewing of hard sentences, and dissolving of doubts, were found in the same Daniel, whom the king named Belteshazzar" (5:12). Belshazzar asks "Art thou that Daniel, which art of the children of the captivity of Judah?" (5:13). Daniel: "Let thy gifts be to thyself, and give thy rewards to another; yet I will read the writing unto the king" (5:17). He tells the king what Nebuchadnezzar's pride cost him (5:18–21) and interprets: "MENE; God hath numbered thy kingdom, and finished it" (5:26). Belshazzar clothes him in scarlet, gives him a gold chain and makes him "the third ruler in the kingdom" (5:29).

**The lions' den.** Under Darius, "this Daniel was preferred above the presidents and princes, because an excellent spirit was in him" (6:3), and his rivals "could find none occasion nor fault; forasmuch as he was faithful" (6:4). They persuade the king to sign a ban on prayer. "Now when Daniel knew that the writing was signed, he went into his house; and his windows being open in his chamber toward Jerusalem, he kneeled upon his knees three times a day, and prayed, and gave thanks before his God, as he did aforetime" (6:10). He is found praying (6:11) and thrown into the den: "Thy God whom thou servest continually, he will deliver thee" (6:16). A stone seals the mouth (6:17). At dawn the king calls out "O Daniel, servant of the living God, is thy God… able to deliver thee from the lions?" (6:20). Daniel: "O king, live for ever. My God hath sent his angel, and hath shut the lions' mouths, that they have not hurt me: forasmuch as before him innocency was found in me" (6:21–22). "No manner of hurt was found upon him, because he believed in his God" (6:23). Darius decrees "men tremble and fear before the God of Daniel: for he is the living God" (6:26). "So this Daniel prospered in the reign of Darius, and in the reign of Cyrus the Persian" (6:28).

**Visions.** "In the first year of Belshazzar king of Babylon Daniel had a dream and visions of his head upon his bed: then he wrote the dream" (7:1). "I saw in my vision by night, and, behold, the four winds of the heaven strove upon the great sea" (7:2). He is troubled: "I Daniel was grieved in my spirit… my cogitations much troubled me" (7:15, 7:28). In chapter 8 he sees a vision at Shushan by the river Ulai and hears a voice tell Gabriel "make this man to understand the vision" (8:2, 8:16); afterwards "I Daniel fainted, and was sick certain days" (8:27). In the first year of Darius he reads in the books "the number of the years, whereof the word of the LORD came to Jeremiah the prophet, that he would accomplish seventy years in the desolations of Jerusalem" and sets his face to prayer with "fasting, and sackcloth, and ashes" (9:2–3). His prayer opens "O Lord, the great and dreadful God, keeping the covenant and mercy" (9:4) and confesses "my sin and the sin of my people Israel" (9:20). Gabriel touches him "about the time of the evening oblation" with "O Daniel, I am now come forth to give thee skill and understanding… thou art greatly beloved" (9:21–23). In chapter 10 he mourns "three full weeks", and "I Daniel alone saw the vision" as those with him flee (10:2–3, 10:7). The messenger: "O Daniel, a man greatly beloved, understand the words that I speak unto thee… Fear not, Daniel: for from the first day that thou didst set thine heart to understand… thy words were heard" (10:11–12). The last instruction: "thou, O Daniel, shut up the words, and seal the book, even to the time of the end" (12:4), and "Go thy way, Daniel: for the words are closed up and sealed till the time of the end" (12:9).

**Outside the book.** Ezekiel 14:14: "Though these three men, Noah, Daniel, and Job, were in it, they should deliver but their own souls by their righteousness" (repeated in 14:20). Hebrews 11:33's "stopped the mouths of lions" (0.57) alludes to him, and Jesus cites "the abomination of desolation, spoken of by Daniel the prophet" (Matthew 24:15, Mark 13:14), which are not linked here.

## Relationships

- **Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego** (**Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah**): his three friends, who join him in Daniel 1–2 and 3.
- **[Nebuchadnezzar](nebuchadnezzar.md)** (35 shared verses), his son **Belshazzar**, **Darius the Mede** and **Cyrus**: the kings he served. **Arioch** and **Melzar/Ashpenaz** are court officials.
- **Gabriel** and the "man clothed in linen": the angels who explain his visions (8:16, 9:21).
- **[Noah](noah.md)** and **[Job](job.md)** (2 shared verses each): the other two men in Ezekiel 14:14 and 14:20. **[Jesus](jesus.md)** shares two verses (Daniel 9:24–25, Gabriel's message about the "Messiah the Prince"). The "Son of man" vision in Daniel 7:13–14 is not linked to Daniel at 0.26 and 0.14.

## Reading the data

- **Missing scenes.** Some passages where Daniel is central fall below the threshold because the actor is someone else: the writing on the wall itself (5:5–6, 5:24–25), Belshazzar's death (5:30) and the vision of the Ancient of Days and the Son of man (7:9–14 at 0.14–0.34). The three friends' furnace chapter (3) is correctly absent.
- Daniel 1:1–3 (the siege and the selection of captives) is only 0.10–0.36 for the same reason.
- **Chapter 11** is the angel's long speech about the kings of Persia and Greece; only Daniel 11:2 (0.56) is linked and most of the chapter is not.
- "Belteshazzar" is accepted as Daniel's second name (TIPNR), which is why the 4:8–19 verses link.
