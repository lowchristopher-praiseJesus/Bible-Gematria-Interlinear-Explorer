## Who Samson is

Samson is the last great judge in the book of Judges, and his whole story (chapters 13–16) is included here: 74 of his 76 links. The other two are Hebrews 11:32–33, which lists him among the men of faith. His story is one of great gifts and repeated poor judgement.

## What the verses tell us

**Promised birth.** "A certain man of Zorah, of the family of the Danites, whose name was Manoah; and his wife was barren" (Judges 13:2). An angel says she will conceive and the son will be "a Nazarite unto God from the womb", no razor on his head, and "he shall begin to deliver Israel out of the hand of the Philistines" (13:5). She is to avoid wine and unclean food during the pregnancy (13:4, 13:7). "The woman bare a son, and called his name Samson: and the child grew, and the LORD blessed him. And the Spirit of the LORD began to move him at times in the camp of Dan" (13:24–25).

**The Philistine wife.** He sees a woman in Timnath "of the daughters of the Philistines" and demands her; his parents object, but "his father and his mother knew not that it was of the LORD, that he sought an occasion against the Philistines" (14:1–4). On the way, "a young lion roared against him. And the Spirit of the LORD came mightily upon him, and he rent him as he would have rent a kid" (14:5–6). Later he finds "a swarm of bees and honey in the carcase of the lion" and eats it (14:8–9). At the wedding feast he sets a riddle: "Out of the eater came forth meat, and out of the strong came forth sweetness" (14:14), with thirty sheets and garments at stake (14:12–13). The Philistines threaten his wife and she weeps until he tells her (14:15–17). His retort: "If ye had not plowed with my heifer, ye had not found out my riddle" (14:18). He kills thirty men at Ashkelon to pay the debt (14:19), and his wife is given to his companion (14:20).

**Revenge.** He returns with a kid, is refused, and answers "Now shall I be more blameless than the Philistines, though I do them a displeasure" (15:1–3). He ties torches to three hundred foxes and burns the grain, vineyards and olives (15:4–5); the Philistines burn his wife and her father (15:6). He smites them "hip and thigh with a great slaughter" (15:8). Three thousand men of Judah come to bind him and hand him over, and he lets them (15:10–13). At Lehi "the Spirit of the LORD came mightily upon him, and the cords… became as flax that was burnt with fire" (15:14) and with "a new jawbone of an ass" he kills a thousand men (15:15–16). Thirsty, he prays and God opens a spring in the jaw (15:18–19). "He judged Israel in the days of the Philistines twenty years" (15:20).

**Gaza.** He goes in to a harlot at Gaza; the men wait to kill him at dawn, but he rises at midnight, takes the city gate doors and posts "bar and all", and carries them up a hill before Hebron (16:1–3).

**Delilah.** "He loved a woman in the valley of Sorek, whose name was Delilah" (16:4). The lords of the Philistines offer her eleven hundred pieces of silver each to find the source of his strength (16:5). He gives her three false answers: seven green withs, new ropes, and the seven locks woven into a web, and each time "The Philistines be upon thee, Samson" and he breaks free (16:6–14). She argues "How canst thou say, I love thee, when thine heart is not with me?" and presses him daily "so that his soul was vexed unto death" (16:15–16). He tells her: "There hath not come a razor upon mine head; for I have been a Nazarite unto God from my mother's womb" (16:17). She lulls him to sleep on her knees, a man shaves the seven locks, and "his strength went from him" (16:19). He wakes intending to shake himself free "and he wist not that the LORD was departed from him" (16:20).

**Captivity and death.** "The Philistines took him, and put out his eyes, and brought him down to Gaza, and bound him with fetters of brass; and he did grind in the prison house" (16:21). "The hair of his head began to grow again" (16:22). At a great feast to Dagon they call for him "that he may make us sport" and set him between the pillars (16:25). He asks the lad holding his hand to let him feel the pillars, then prays "O Lord God, remember me… only this once… that I may be at once avenged of the Philistines for my two eyes" (16:28). "Let me die with the Philistines" — he pushes down the two middle pillars, and "the dead which he slew at his death were more than they which he slew in his life" (16:30). His family bury him "between Zorah and Eshtaol in the buryingplace of Manoah his father. And he judged Israel twenty years" (16:31).

**Hebrews.** He is one of those named in Hebrews 11:32, "the time would fail me to tell of Gedeon, and of Barak, and of Samson, and of Jephthae; of David also, and Samuel", among those who "out of weakness were made strong" (11:34).

## Relationships

- **Manoah** and his wife (unnamed): parents, of the family of the Danites at Zorah.
- **[Delilah](delilah.md)**: the woman of Sorek he loved; they share 17 verses (Judges 16:4–20). TIPNR calls her his wife, but the text says only that he loved her.
- **His Timnite wife** (unnamed) and her father: the Philistine marriage of Judges 14–15.
- **Philistine lords**, **Judah**'s three thousand men, and the Gazites are the parties he faces.
- **[Gideon](gideon.md)**, **[Samuel](samuel.md)** and **[David](david.md)** appear next to him in Hebrews 11:32 (each shares 2 verses).

## Reading the data

- Judges 14–15 verses about "his wife", "her father" and "the woman" are linked to Samson because he is the actor; the wife is not Delilah, whom the classifier correctly keeps to chapter 16.
- Judges 13:5 and 13:7 refer to him as an unborn child; both are included (13:7 at 0.51).
- No named verse was missed; TIPNR's 38 verses are all in the 76.
