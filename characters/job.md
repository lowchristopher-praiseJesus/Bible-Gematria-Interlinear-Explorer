## Who Job is

Job appears in the book of Job (604 of 607 links); the other three are Ezekiel 14:14 and 14:20 (with Noah and Daniel) and Psalm 89:46. About two-thirds of the book is speeches, and the classifier links a verse to Job both when he speaks and when a friend addresses him as "thou". He is called "Job of Uz" with no genealogy, and the book gives no date; TIPNR places him "at the time of the Patriarchs".

## What the verses tell us

**The man.** "There was a man in the land of Uz, whose name was Job; and that man was perfect and upright, and one that feared God, and eschewed evil. And there were born unto him seven sons and three daughters. His substance also was seven thousand sheep, and three thousand camels, and five hundred yoke of oxen, and five hundred she asses, and a very great household; so that this man was the greatest of all the men of the east" (Job 1:1–3). After his sons' feasts he "sent and sanctified them… offered burnt offerings… for Job said, It may be that my sons have sinned, and cursed God in their hearts. Thus did Job continually" (1:5).

**The wager.** The LORD asks Satan "Hast thou considered my servant Job, that there is none like him in the earth, a perfect and an upright man, one that feareth God, and escheweth evil?" (1:8); Satan may take all he has but not touch Job himself (1:12). Messengers arrive in succession, each ending "I only am escaped alone to tell thee": Sabeans took the oxen and asses and killed the servants (1:14–15), "the fire of God" burnt the sheep (1:16), Chaldeans took the camels (1:17), and a great wind destroyed the house where his sons and daughters were feasting (1:18–19). "Then Job arose, and rent his mantle, and shaved his head, and fell down upon the ground, and worshipped, and said, Naked came I out of my mother's womb, and naked shall I return thither: the LORD gave, and the LORD hath taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD. In all this Job sinned not, nor charged God foolishly" (1:20–22).

**The boils.** The LORD says Job "still… holdeth fast his integrity, although thou movedst me against him, to destroy him without cause" (2:3). Satan "smote Job with sore boils from the sole of his foot unto his crown" and Job "took him a potsherd to scrape himself withal; and he sat down among the ashes" (2:7–8). His wife: "Dost thou still retain thine integrity? curse God, and die" (2:9). Job: "Thou speakest as one of the foolish women speaketh. What? shall we receive good at the hand of God, and shall we not receive evil? In all this did not Job sin with his lips" (2:10). Three friends come, Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite and Zophar the Naamathite, weep, tear their robes, and sit with him "seven days and seven nights, and none spake a word unto him: for they saw that his grief was very great" (2:11–13).

**The complaint (chapters 3–31).** "After this opened Job his mouth, and cursed his day" (3:1). What follows is a debate. Job speaks of his misery ("When I lie down, I say, When shall I arise, and the night be gone?" 7:4; "My soul is weary of my life… I will say unto God, Do not condemn me; shew me wherefore thou contendest with me" 10:1–2). The friends say his suffering must be punishment: Eliphaz ("Is not this thy fear, thy confidence, thy hope, and the uprightness of thy ways?" 4:6), Bildad ("If thy children have sinned against him… If thou wouldest seek unto God betimes" 8:4–5) and Zophar ("Know therefore that God exacteth of thee less than thine iniquity deserveth" 11:6). Job pushes back: "I have understanding as well as you; I am not inferior to you" (12:3) and "how should man be just with God?" (9:2). He holds on through the darkness: "Though he slay me, yet will I trust in him: but I will maintain mine own ways before him" (13:15); "If a man die, shall he live again?" (14:14); "For I know that my redeemer liveth, and that he shall stand at the latter day upon the earth: and though after my skin worms destroy this body, yet in my flesh shall I see God" (19:25–26); "But he knoweth the way that I take: when he hath tried me, I shall come forth as gold" (23:10). He recalls his former life ("I put on righteousness, and it clothed me: my judgment was as a robe and a diadem", 29:14), mourns his reversal ("now am I their song, yea, I am their byword", 30:9), and swears his innocence: "I made a covenant with mine eyes; why then should I think upon a maid?" (31:1), "Let me be weighed in an even balance that God may know mine integrity" (31:6), "Oh that one would hear me!… that the Almighty would answer me" (31:35).

**Elihu.** When "these three men ceased to answer Job, because he was righteous in his own eyes", the younger Elihu son of Barachel is angry with Job "because he justified himself rather than God" (32:1–2). He addresses him directly: "Wherefore, Job, I pray thee, hear my speeches" (33:1), "Job hath spoken without knowledge, and his words were without wisdom" (34:35), "Hearken unto this, O Job: stand still, and consider the wondrous works of God" (37:14).

**God answers.** "Then the LORD answered Job out of the whirlwind" (38:1): "Gird up now thy loins like a man; for I will demand of thee, and answer thou me. Where wast thou when I laid the foundations of the earth? declare, if thou hast understanding" (38:3–4). The questions run through the stars, the sea, the wild goats and the eagle (39:1, 39:27) and end with Leviathan: "Canst thou draw out leviathan with an hook?" (41:1–4). Job answers: "Behold, I am vile; what shall I answer thee? I will lay mine hand upon my mouth" (40:4), and finally: "I have heard of thee by the hearing of the ear: but now mine eye seeth thee. Wherefore I abhor myself, and repent in dust and ashes" (42:5–6).

**Restored.** The LORD tells Eliphaz "My wrath is kindled against thee, and against thy two friends: for ye have not spoken of me the thing that is right, as my servant Job hath" (42:7), and tells them to offer seven bullocks and seven rams so that "my servant Job shall pray for you" (42:8). "The LORD turned the captivity of Job, when he prayed for his friends: also the LORD gave Job twice as much as he had before" (42:10). His family and acquaintances come to comfort him and give him gifts (42:11). "So the LORD blessed the latter end of Job more than his beginning: for he had fourteen thousand sheep, and six thousand camels, and a thousand yoke of oxen, and a thousand she asses. He had also seven sons and three daughters" (42:12–13). The daughters are Jemima, Kezia and Kerenhappuch, "and in all the land were no women found so fair as the daughters of Job: and their father gave them inheritance among their brethren" (42:14–15). "After this lived Job an hundred and forty years… So Job died, being old and full of days" (42:16–17).

**Elsewhere.** Ezekiel 14:14: "Though these three men, Noah, Daniel, and Job, were in it, they should deliver but their own souls by their righteousness" (also 14:20; these are the two verses shared with [Noah](noah.md) and [Daniel](daniel.md)). James 5:11 ("Ye have heard of the patience of Job") was named by TIPNR but scored 0.45.

## Relationships

- **His wife** (unnamed) and his **seven sons and three daughters** before and after (Jemima, Kezia and Kerenhappuch after).
- **Eliphaz, Bildad, Zophar** and **Elihu**: the four men who argue with him. **Satan** and the LORD are the other voices.
- **[Noah](noah.md)** and **[Daniel](daniel.md)**: the other two "righteous men" in Ezekiel 14.
- No tracked figure appears in his story.

## Reading the data

- Much of the linking depends on the second-person "thou" and "thee" in the friends' speeches: verses such as Job 4:2, 5:20, 8:2 and 11:5 are addressed to him. Verses spoken by the friends about their own view or about God in general are separate and not linked.
- The heavenly scene in Job 1:6–7 (0.07 and 0.00) is not linked; 2:1–6 follows the same pattern.
- Psalm 89:46 (0.50) is a misfit. It is a psalm lament and is only linked because the speaker's suffering resembles Job's.
