## Who Lot is

Lot's story runs through Genesis 11–19 (46 of 53 linked verses). Later writers use him in three ways: as the reason Israel must not attack Moab and Ammon (Deuteronomy 2, Psalm 83), as a warning that judgement comes suddenly (Luke 17), and as a "just" man among the wicked (2 Peter 2).

## What the verses tell us

**Family.** "Terah begat Abram, Nahor, and Haran; and Haran begat Lot" (Genesis 11:27). Terah takes "Lot the son of Haran his son's son" from Ur towards Canaan (11:31); Abram later takes "Lot his brother's son" with him (12:4–5).

**The parting.** Both men are so rich that "the land was not able to bear them" and "there was a strife between the herdmen of Abram's cattle and the herdmen of Lot's cattle" (13:6–7). Abram offers Lot the first choice: "Let there be no strife, I pray thee, between me and thee… for we be brethren" (13:8–9). Lot "beheld all the plain of Jordan, that it was well watered every where" and "chose him all the plain of Jordan" (13:10–11). He "dwelled in the cities of the plain, and pitched his tent toward Sodom" (13:12).

**Captured and rescued.** In the war of the kings "they took Lot, Abram's brother's son, who dwelt in Sodom, and his goods" (14:12). Abram arms 318 trained servants (14:14) and "brought again his brother Lot, and his goods, and the women also" (14:16).

**Sodom.** Two angels arrive as Lot "sat in the gate of Sodom"; he bows, presses them to stay and feeds them (19:1–3). The men of the city demand the visitors (19:5). Lot shuts the door behind him, says "do not so wickedly", and offers his two daughters instead (19:6–8), a choice the text reports without comment. The men of Sodom nearly break in, calling him a man who "came in to sojourn, and he will needs be a judge" (19:9); the angels pull him inside (19:10). His sons-in-law think he "seemed as one that mocked" when he warns them (19:14). At dawn the angels seize the hands of Lot, his wife and two daughters, "the LORD being merciful unto him" (19:15–16). He begs to flee to the small city of Zoar instead of the mountain (19:18–22).

**Destruction and the wife.** "His wife looked back from behind him, and she became a pillar of salt" (19:26). "God remembered Abraham, and sent Lot out of the midst of the overthrow" (19:29): the rescue is credited to Abraham.

**The cave.** Afraid to stay in Zoar, Lot lives in a cave with his daughters (19:30). Believing no man is left "to come in unto us", the daughters make him drunk two nights running and sleep with him, "that we may preserve seed of our father" (19:31–36). The text says twice "he perceived not when she lay down, nor when she arose" (19:33, 19:35). Their sons are Moab and Ben-ammi (Genesis 19:37–38, not linked here).

**Later references.**
- *Deuteronomy 2:9, 2:19:* God tells Moses not to distress Moab or Ammon, "because I have given Ar unto the children of Lot".
- *Psalm 83:8:* "they have holpen the children of Lot".
- *Luke 17:28–29:* Jesus: "as it was in the days of Lot; they did eat, they drank, they bought, they sold… the same day that Lot went out of Sodom it rained fire and brimstone from heaven". Verse 32 adds "Remember Lot's wife".
- *2 Peter 2:7–8:* God "delivered just Lot, vexed with the filthy conversation of the wicked… that righteous man dwelling among them… vexed his righteous soul from day to day".

## Relationships

- **[Abraham](abraham.md)**: uncle; they travel together, separate, and Abraham rescues him and intercedes for Sodom. Fifteen linked verses are shared. **Haran**, Lot's father, was Abraham's brother; **Milcah** and **Iscah** are his sisters (TIPNR).
- **[Sarah](sarah.md)**: Abraham's wife and Lot's aunt (Genesis 11:31, 12:5).
- **His wife** (unnamed, the pillar of salt) and **two unnamed daughters** (19:30–36), who are also his partners in the cave episode by TIPNR's counting.
- **Moab and Ben-ammi (Ammon)**: sons by his daughters, ancestors of the Moabites and Ammonites, who are the "children of Lot" in Deuteronomy 2.
