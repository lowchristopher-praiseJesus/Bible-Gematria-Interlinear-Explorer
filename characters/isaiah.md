## Who Isaiah is

Isaiah son of Amoz was active in Judah in the eighth century BC. In this index he is linked in three kinds of place: narrative about him in 2 Kings 19–20 and 2 Chronicles (20 verses), passages in the book of Isaiah where the prophet is named or speaks as "I" (76), and New Testament quotations introduced as "Esaias the prophet" (12). His most famous oracles (Isaiah 7:14, 9:6, 53) are *not* linked to him: the classifier attaches him to who is speaking or named, not to the content of what is prophesied.

## What the verses tell us

**The vision and the commission.** The book opens "The vision of Isaiah the son of Amoz, which he saw concerning Judah and Jerusalem in the days of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah, kings of Judah" (Isaiah 1:1; 2:1). In the year King Uzziah died "I saw also the LORD sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up, and his train filled the temple" (6:1). His response: "Woe is me! for I am undone; because I am a man of unclean lips, and I dwell in the midst of a people of unclean lips: for mine eyes have seen the King, the LORD of hosts" (6:5). A seraph touches his lips with a live coal: "thine iniquity is taken away, and thy sin purged" (6:6–7). Then "I heard the voice of the Lord, saying, Whom shall I send, and who will go for us? Then said I, Here am I; send me" (6:8), followed by the commission to tell the people "Hear ye indeed, but understand not" (6:9).

**Ahaz and the family as signs.** The LORD tells him "Go forth now to meet Ahaz, thou, and Shearjashub thy son… Take heed, and be quiet; fear not, neither be fainthearted for the two tails of these smoking firebrands" (7:3–4). He is told to "take thee a great roll, and write in it… concerning Mahershalalhashbaz" and to call his son by that name; he takes "faithful witnesses to record, Uriah the priest, and Zechariah the son of Jeberechiah", and "went unto the prophetess; and she conceived, and bare a son" (8:1–3). "Behold, I and the children whom the LORD hath given me are for signs and for wonders in Israel from the LORD of hosts" (8:18). For three years he walks "naked and barefoot" as "a sign and wonder upon Egypt and upon Ethiopia" (20:2–3).

**Oracles and his own anguish.** Isaiah's speeches about the nations include his own reactions: "my bowels shall sound like an harp for Moab" (16:11), "Therefore are my loins filled with pain" at the vision of Babylon's fall (21:3), "Look away from me; I will weep bitterly… because of the spoiling of the daughter of my people" (22:4), "For thus hath the LORD said unto me, Go, set a watchman" (21:6), and "My leanness, my leanness, woe unto me!" (24:16). The LORD tells him "Now go, write it before them in a table, and note it in a book, that it may be for the time to come for ever and ever" (30:8). 2 Chronicles 26:22 says he wrote the acts of Uzziah, and 32:32 that Hezekiah's acts "are written in the vision of Isaiah the prophet".

**Hezekiah and Sennacherib.** When the Assyrian envoys blaspheme, Isaiah says "Thus shall ye say to your master, Thus saith the LORD, Be not afraid of the words which thou hast heard, with which the servants of the king of Assyria have blasphemed me" (2 Kings 19:6; Isaiah 37:6). "Isaiah the son of Amoz sent to Hezekiah, saying, Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, That which thou hast prayed to me against Sennacherib king of Assyria I have heard" (2 Kings 19:20; Isaiah 37:21). "Hezekiah the king, and the prophet Isaiah the son of Amoz, prayed and cried to heaven" (2 Chronicles 32:20).

**Hezekiah's illness.** "In those days was Hezekiah sick unto death. And the prophet Isaiah the son of Amoz came to him, and said unto him, Thus saith the LORD, Set thine house in order; for thou shalt die, and not live" (2 Kings 20:1; Isaiah 38:1). Before he left the middle court the word came to turn back with "I will heal thee" and fifteen more years (20:4–6; Isaiah 38:5). "Isaiah said, Take a lump of figs. And they took and laid it on the boil, and he recovered" (20:7). At the king's request "Isaiah the prophet cried unto the LORD: and he brought the shadow ten degrees backward, by which it had gone down in the dial of Ahaz" (20:11).

**The Babylonian envoys.** He asks the king "What said these men? and from whence came they unto thee?" and Hezekiah answers "from a far country… from Babylon" (Isaiah 39:3). Isaiah then says "Hear the word of the LORD of hosts" (39:5) and announces that everything in the king's house will be carried to Babylon (39:6).

**The voice.** "The voice said, Cry. And he said, What shall I cry? All flesh is grass" (40:6). Passages of the Servant in chapters 49–50 ("Thou art my servant, O Israel, in whom I will be glorified", 49:3; "The Lord GOD hath opened mine ear, and I was not rebellious", 50:5) are also linked to him at 0.5–0.6; whether that voice is Isaiah, Israel or the coming Servant is a question the text does not answer within these verses.

**In the Gospels and Paul.** Matthew introduces two quotations "which was spoken by Esaias the prophet" (4:14; 12:17). Luke 3:4: "As it is written in the book of the words of Esaias the prophet, saying, The voice of one crying in the wilderness, Prepare ye the way of the Lord". John 12:38–41: "Lord, who hath believed our report?… These things said Esaias, when he saw his glory, and spake of him." Romans quotes him repeatedly: "a remnant shall be saved" (9:27), "Lord, who hath believed our report?" (10:16), "I was found of them that sought me not" (10:20), "There shall be a root of Jesse, and he that shall rise to reign over the Gentiles; in him shall the Gentiles trust" (15:12).

## Relationships

- **Amoz**: father. **Shear-jashub** and **Maher-shalal-hash-baz**: sons (Isaiah 7:3, 8:3). His wife is called "the prophetess" (8:3) and is unnamed.
- **Hezekiah** and **Ahaz**: the kings he advised. **Sennacherib** and the Assyrian envoys are the enemy. **Uriah the priest** and **Zechariah son of Jeberechiah** witnessed the roll (8:2).
- **[Jesus](jesus.md)** (7 shared verses): Isaiah 49:5 and 50:5 (the Servant passages), Matthew 4:14, 12:17, Luke 3:4, John 12:41 and Romans 15:12, where the Gospels and Paul read Isaiah's words as about him. **[John the Baptist](john_baptist.md)** shares Luke 3:4.
- **[Jacob](jacob.md)** appears in one shared verse (Isaiah 49:5, "to bring Jacob again to him").

## Reading the data

- **What is not linked.** The great messianic passages (7:14 at 0.25, 9:6 at 0.09, 53:5 at 0.07) and the angel destroying Sennacherib's army (37:36) are not attached to him. His name appears in Matthew 3:3, 8:17, 13:14, 15:7, Mark 1:2, 7:6, Luke 4:17, John 1:23 and Acts 8:28–30 (11 verses) but they scored 0.20–0.47.
- **Oracle "I" verses.** Links in Isaiah 16, 18, 21, 22, 24, 28, 30, 32, 44, 49, 50, 51, 58 and 59 at 0.5–0.65 depend on reading the "I" as the prophet; 49:3 and 49:5 may be the Servant.
- The 2 Kings 19–20 narrative is fully covered and is the best-documented part of his life.
