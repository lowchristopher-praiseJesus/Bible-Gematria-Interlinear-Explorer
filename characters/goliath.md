## Who Goliath is

Goliath appears in a single chapter, 1 Samuel 17, plus one later reference. All 31 links are in 1 Samuel (17:4–57 and 19:5). He is the Bible's standard example of the overwhelming enemy and the way David came to public notice.

## What the verses tell us

**The champion.** "There went out a champion out of the camp of the Philistines, named Goliath, of Gath, whose height was six cubits and a span" (1 Samuel 17:4). His gear is listed: a brass helmet, a coat of mail weighing "five thousand shekels of brass", brass greaves and a brass target, and a spear with a shaft "like a weaver's beam" and a head weighing "six hundred shekels of iron", with a shield-bearer going before him (17:5–7).

**The challenge.** He shouts at the armies of Israel: "Why are ye come out to set your battle in array? am not I a Philistine, and ye servants to Saul? choose you a man for you, and let him come down to me. If he be able to fight with me, and to kill me, then will we be your servants: but if I prevail against him, and kill him, then shall ye be our servants" (17:8–9). "I defy the armies of Israel this day; give me a man, that we may fight together" (17:10). Saul and all Israel "were dismayed, and greatly afraid" (17:11). The Philistine "drew near morning and evening, and presented himself forty days" (17:16).

**David hears.** When David is bringing food to his brothers, Goliath comes up again "and spake according to the same words: and David heard them" (17:23). The Israelites flee from him "sore afraid" (17:24) and say the king will give great riches, his daughter, and freedom from taxes to the man who kills him (17:25). David asks "who is this uncircumcised Philistine, that he should defy the armies of the living God?" (17:26).

**Before Saul.** David: "Let no man's heart fail because of him; thy servant will go and fight with this Philistine" (17:32). Saul objects that David is "but a youth, and he a man of war from his youth" (17:33). David answers that he has killed a lion and a bear: "The LORD that delivered me out of the paw of the lion, and out of the paw of the bear, he will deliver me out of the hand of this Philistine" (17:36–37). Saul: "Go, and the LORD be with thee."

**The fight.** David picks "five smooth stones out of the brook" with his sling (17:40). Goliath "disdained him: for he was but a youth, and ruddy" (17:42), says "Am I a dog, that thou comest to me with staves?" and curses David by his gods (17:43), promising "I will give thy flesh unto the fowls of the air" (17:44). David: "Thou comest to me with a sword, and with a spear, and with a shield: but I come to thee in the name of the LORD of hosts" (17:45). He runs at the Philistine, slings a stone into his forehead, and "he fell upon his face to the earth" (17:48–49). "David prevailed over the Philistine with a sling and with a stone… but there was no sword in the hand of David" (17:50), so he draws Goliath's own sword and cuts off his head (17:51). The Philistines flee (17:51), Israel plunders their camp (17:53), and Abner brings David before Saul "with the head of the Philistine in his hand" (17:57). Jonathan later reminds Saul that David "did put his life in his hand, and slew the Philistine, and the LORD wrought a great salvation for all Israel" (19:5).

**Afterwards.** The priest Ahimelech gives David "the sword of Goliath the Philistine, whom thou slewest in the valley of Elah" (1 Samuel 21:9, not linked to him here at 0.27). 2 Samuel 21:19 and 1 Chronicles 20:5 record another battle in which Elhanan killed "the brother of Goliath the Gittite" (or Lahmi in Chronicles), whose spear staff was also "like a weaver's beam".

## Reading the data

The links cover the whole of 1 Samuel 17 apart from the parts that describe only David (the shepherding and armour scenes) and the later "sword of Goliath" references (1 Samuel 21:9, 22:10, 0.27–0.30). 2 Samuel 21:19 and 1 Chronicles 20:5 mention him only as the brother's relation and score 0.38–0.43. No other giant is confused with him.
