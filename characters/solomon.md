## Who Solomon is

Solomon is a wide-ranging profile: 669 links, of which 308 are in 1 Kings (chapters 1–11) and 155 in 2 Chronicles (1–9). The rest are from David's charge to him in 1 Chronicles, the wisdom books (Proverbs 15, Ecclesiastes 58, Song of Solomon 55, and Psalms 20) and references in the prophets and New Testament. The Ecclesiastes, Song and Proverbs links come from books that traditionally carry his name; see "Reading the data".

## What the verses tell us

**Birth and succession.** He is listed among the sons born to David in Jerusalem (2 Samuel 5:14) and is named by David when Bathsheba bears him: "he called his name Solomon: and the LORD loved him" (12:24). God's promise to David is "He shall build an house for my name, and I will stablish the throne of his kingdom for ever" (7:13). When Adonijah tries to take the throne, Zadok, Nathan and Benaiah "caused Solomon to ride upon king David's mule, and brought him to Gihon" (1 Kings 1:38); "Zadok the priest took an horn of oil out of the tabernacle, and anointed Solomon… and all the people said, God save king Solomon" (1:39). "Then sat Solomon upon the throne of David his father; and his kingdom was established greatly" (2:12). David's charge to him: "know thou the God of thy father, and serve him with a perfect heart and with a willing mind… if thou forsake him, he will cast thee off for ever" (1 Chronicles 28:9). "Solomon sat on the throne of the LORD as king instead of David his father, and prospered" (29:23).

**Wisdom.** "In Gibeon the LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream by night: and God said, Ask what I shall give thee" (1 Kings 3:5). Solomon recalls David's faithfulness and says "I am but a little child: I know not how to go out or come in… Give therefore thy servant an understanding heart to judge thy people, that I may discern between good and bad" (3:7, 3:9). God is pleased that he asked neither for long life, riches nor his enemies' lives (3:10–11) and says "I have given thee a wise and an understanding heart; so that there was none like thee before thee, neither after thee shall any arise like unto thee" (3:12). His best-known judgement: two harlots claim one baby; "Give her the living child, and in no wise slay it: she is the mother thereof", and "all Israel heard… and they feared the king: for they saw that the wisdom of God was in him" (3:16–28). "God gave Solomon wisdom and understanding exceeding much… wiser than all men" (4:29–31). "He spake three thousand proverbs: and his songs were a thousand and five. And he spake of trees, from the cedar tree that is in Lebanon even unto the hyssop that springeth out of the wall" (4:32–33). "There came of all people to hear the wisdom of Solomon, from all kings of the earth" (4:34).

**Wealth and reach.** He "reigned over all kingdoms from the river unto the land of the Philistines, and unto the border of Egypt: they brought presents" (4:21). He has "forty thousand stalls of horses for his chariots, and twelve thousand horsemen" (4:26). He makes an alliance with Pharaoh and marries his daughter (3:1). "King Solomon passed all the kings of the earth in riches and wisdom. And all the kings of the earth sought the presence of Solomon, to hear his wisdom" (2 Chronicles 9:22–23). The queen of Sheba comes "with a very great train, with camels that bare spices, and very much gold, and precious stones" and "Solomon told her all her questions" (1 Kings 10:2–3); she says "the half was not told me: thy wisdom and prosperity exceedeth the fame which I heard" (10:7).

**Temple and palace.** He sends to Hiram of Tyre for cedar and fir (5:2), pays him wheat and oil year by year (5:11), and calls Hiram (the craftsman) from Tyre (7:13). "In the four hundred and eightieth year after the children of Israel were come out of the land of Egypt, in the fourth year of Solomon's reign… he began to build the house of the LORD" (6:1). It is finished in the eleventh year: "so was he seven years in building it" (6:38). His own house takes thirteen years (7:1). Chronicles adds that it was begun on Mount Moriah, in the place David prepared (2 Chronicles 3:1), with 70,000 bearers and 80,000 hewers (2:2). At the dedication the ark is brought in with sheep and oxen "that could not be told nor numbered" (1 Kings 8:1, 8:5). He says "The LORD said that he would dwell in the thick darkness. I have surely built thee an house to dwell in" (8:12–13) and asks "will God indeed dwell on the earth? behold, the heaven and heaven of heavens cannot contain thee; how much less this house that I have builded?" (8:27). He kneels on a bronze platform with hands spread toward heaven (2 Chronicles 6:13). He offers 22,000 oxen and 120,000 sheep (7:5). The LORD appears again: "I have hallowed this house, which thou hast built, to put my name there for ever" (1 Kings 9:1–3). Stephen sums it up in Acts 7:47: "But Solomon built him an house."

**Decline.** "King Solomon loved many strange women, together with the daughter of Pharaoh, women of the Moabites, Ammonites, Edomites, Zidonians, and Hittites" (1 Kings 11:1). "He had seven hundred wives, princesses, and three hundred concubines: and his wives turned away his heart" (11:3). "When Solomon was old… his wives turned away his heart after other gods: and his heart was not perfect with the LORD his God, as was the heart of David his father" (11:4), so he "went after Ashtoreth the goddess of the Zidonians, and after Milcom the abomination of the Ammonites" (11:5). "The LORD was angry with Solomon" (11:9) and said "I will surely rend the kingdom from thee… Notwithstanding in thy days I will not do it for David thy father's sake: but I will rend it out of the hand of thy son" (11:11–12), leaving him one tribe (11:13). Nehemiah 13:26 adds: "among many nations was there no king like him, who was beloved of his God… nevertheless even him did outlandish women cause to sin." He appoints the industrious young Jeroboam over the labour of "the house of Joseph" (11:28), and Jeroboam "lifted up his hand against the king" (11:26). "The time that Solomon reigned in Jerusalem over all Israel was forty years. And Solomon slept with his fathers… and Rehoboam his son reigned in his stead" (11:42–43). Rehoboam's answer to the people, "My father made your yoke heavy, and I will add to your yoke… I will chastise you with scorpions", splits the kingdom (12:14).

**Wisdom books.** The Bible's first words of Proverbs are "The proverbs of Solomon the son of David, king of Israel" (Proverbs 1:1; also 10:1, 25:1). Ecclesiastes begins "The words of the Preacher, the son of David, king in Jerusalem" (1:1), with lines such as "Vanity of vanities, saith the Preacher" (1:2) and "I gave my heart to know wisdom, and to know madness and folly" (1:17), "I made me gardens and orchards… pools of water" (2:5–6), "I applied mine heart to know, and to search, and to seek out wisdom" (7:25). The Song is "The song of songs, which is Solomon's" (1:1), with "King Solomon made himself a chariot of the wood of Lebanon" (3:9) and "Solomon had a vineyard at Baalhamon" (8:11). Psalm 72:1: "Give the king thy judgments, O God, and thy righteousness unto the king's son."

**Later references.** The temple's bronze pillars and vessels "which Solomon had made" are carried off to Babylon (2 Kings 25:16, Jeremiah 52:20) and Shishak takes his gold shields (2 Chronicles 12:9). Kings and Chronicles cite the promise "to David, and to Solomon his son" in condemning Manasseh (2 Kings 21:7, 2 Chronicles 33:7). Matthew 1:6: "David the king begat Solomon of her that had been the wife of Urias". Jesus' comparison ("a greater than Solomon is here", Matthew 12:42, Luke 11:31) is named in TIPNR but scored 0.27–0.40.

## Reading the data

- **Wisdom books.** The links in Ecclesiastes (58), Song of Solomon (55) and Proverbs (15) rest on the traditional attribution ("the Preacher, the son of David", "which is Solomon's"). Verses such as Ecclesiastes 2:6 or Song of Solomon 4:1 are Solomon *speaking* only by that tradition, or in Song 4:1 and 5:16 are the lovers speaking about each other. Proverbs is thin (15) because most proverbs have no speaker cue.
- **Preaching without the name.** Matthew 6:29 ("even Solomon in all his glory", 0.11) and Matthew 12:42 (0.27) fall below 0.5.
- The core narrative in 1 Kings 1–11 and 2 Chronicles 1–9 is dense and reliable, including the scenes with Sheba and the temple.
- The 33 TIPNR-named misses are mostly "Rehoboam the son of Solomon" and "the servant of Solomon" phrases.
