## Who Samuel is

Samuel stands at the hinge between the judges and the monarchy. 167 of his 175 links are in 1 Samuel, and 79 of them are shared with [Saul](saul.md). Later writers name him as a leader on a level with Moses and Aaron (Psalm 99:6, Jeremiah 15:1) and as the last judge before the kings (Acts 13:20).

## What the verses tell us

**Given to God.** He is born to Hannah "because I have asked him of the LORD" (1 Samuel 1:20). She waits until "the child be weaned" and then takes him, with three bullocks, flour and wine, to Eli at Shiloh (1:23–25): "For this child I prayed… Therefore also I have lent him to the LORD; as long as he liveth he shall be lent to the LORD" (1:27–28). He serves "before Eli the priest" (2:11), "being a child, girded with a linen ephod" (2:18). His mother brings him "a little coat" every year (2:19) and God gives Hannah three sons and two daughters more (2:21). "The child Samuel grew on, and was in favour both with the LORD, and also with men" (2:26).

**The call.** "The word of the LORD was precious in those days; there was no open vision" (3:1). "The LORD called Samuel: and he answered, Here am I" (3:4). He runs to Eli three times, and "Eli perceived that the LORD had called the child" (3:5–8). Verses 3:19–20: "the LORD was with him, and did let none of his words fall to the ground. And all Israel from Dan even to Beersheba knew that Samuel was established to be a prophet of the LORD."

**Judge and intercessor.** He tells Israel "If ye do return unto the LORD with all your hearts, then put away the strange gods and Ashtaroth" (7:3), gathers them to Mizpeh, "I will pray for you unto the LORD" (7:5); they pour out water and fast, and "Samuel judged the children of Israel in Mizpeh" (7:6). He offers a sucking lamb and "cried unto the LORD for Israel; and the LORD heard him" (7:9). He sets up a stone called Ebenezer: "Hitherto hath the LORD helped us" (7:12).

**The demand for a king.** When Samuel is old and his sons do not follow him, the elders say "now make us a king to judge us like all the nations" (8:5); "the thing displeased Samuel" and he prays (8:6). God: "they have not rejected thee, but they have rejected me, that I should not reign over them" (8:7). Samuel warns them what a king will take, then reports their answer to the LORD (8:10, 8:21).

**Anointing Saul.** God tells him "Behold the man whom I spake to thee of! this same shall reign" (9:17). Saul asks "where the seer's house is" and Samuel answers "I am the seer" (9:18–19), seats him in the chief place at the feast (9:22–23), pours oil on his head and kisses him: "Is it not because the LORD hath anointed thee to be captain over his inheritance?" (10:1). At Mizpeh the tribes are brought near by lot until Saul is taken (10:17–24). "Samuel told the people the manner of the kingdom, and wrote it in a book" (10:25). After the victory at Jabesh he says "let us go to Gilgal, and renew the kingdom there" (11:14). In his farewell he invites Israel to testify against him: "whose ox have I taken? or whose ass?" (12:1–3), and after a thunderstorm the people ask "Pray for thy servants unto the LORD thy God, that we die not". His answer: "Fear not… turn not aside from following the LORD, but serve the LORD with all your heart" (12:18–20).

**Saul rejected.** At Gilgal Saul gives the burnt offering himself instead of waiting for Samuel (13:8–10). Samuel: "Thou hast done foolishly: thou hast not kept the commandment of the LORD… now thy kingdom shall not continue: the LORD hath sought him a man after his own heart" (13:13–14). After Amalek, God says "It repenteth me that I have set up Saul to be king… And it grieved Samuel; and he cried unto the LORD all night" (15:11). Samuel meets Saul: "What meaneth then this bleating of the sheep in mine ears, and the lowing of the oxen?" (15:14). Then "to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams. For rebellion is as the sin of witchcraft… Because thou hast rejected the word of the LORD, he hath also rejected thee from being king" (15:22–23). "Samuel came no more to see Saul until the day of his death: nevertheless Samuel mourned for Saul" (15:35).

**Anointing David.** "How long wilt thou mourn for Saul?… fill thine horn with oil, and go, I will send thee to Jesse the Bethlehemite" (16:1). Samuel: "How can I go? if Saul hear it, he will kill me" and is told to take a heifer (16:2). The elders of Bethlehem tremble: "Comest thou peaceably?" (16:4). Looking at the first son, he is told "Look not on his countenance, or on the height of his stature… the LORD seeth not as man seeth… the LORD looketh on the heart" (16:7). Seven sons pass; "The LORD hath not chosen these" (16:10). Finally "Samuel took the horn of oil, and anointed him in the midst of his brethren: and the Spirit of the LORD came upon David from that day forward" (16:13). Later David flees to Samuel at Ramah and they live at Naioth (19:18); Saul's messengers and then Saul himself prophesy in Samuel's presence, "Is Saul also among the prophets?" (19:20–24).

**Death and the medium of Endor.** "Samuel died; and all the Israelites were gathered together, and lamented him, and buried him in his house at Ramah" (25:1). Saul, terrified of the Philistines, asks a woman with a familiar spirit to call him up (28:3–14). "Samuel said to Saul, Why hast thou disquieted me, to bring me up?" and tells him the LORD "is departed from thee, and is become thine enemy" (28:15–16). "Saul fell straightway all along on the earth, and was sore afraid" (28:20).

**Later references.** 1 Chronicles 9:22 and 26:28 list gifts and offices ordered by "David and Samuel the seer", and 29:29 says David's acts "are written in the book of Samuel the seer, and in the book of Nathan the prophet, and in the book of Gad the seer". Psalm 99:6: "Moses and Aaron among his priests, and Samuel among them that call upon his name". Jeremiah 15:1: "Though Moses and Samuel stood before me, yet my mind could not be toward this people". Acts 13:20: "he gave unto them judges… until Samuel the prophet". Hebrews 11:32: "David also, and Samuel, and of the prophets".

## Relationships

- **Elkanah and Hannah**: parents. **Eli** (priest, and Hophni and Phinehas) trains him at Shiloh. His own sons **Joel and Abijah** (1 Samuel 8:2) are the reason the elders ask for a king.
- **[Saul](saul.md)**: the king he anointed and then rejected; 79 shared verses. **[David](david.md)**: the king he anointed; 12 shared verses.
- **Jesse**, whose seven sons pass before him (16:10).
- **[Moses](moses.md)**, **[Aaron](aaron.md)**, **[Gideon](gideon.md)** and **[Samson](samson.md)**: named alongside him in Psalm 99, 1 Samuel 12:6–11 and Hebrews 11.
