## Who Esther is

Esther's story is the book that bears her name: all 57 of her links are in Esther, chiefly chapters 2 and 4–9. She has two names, Hadassah (Hebrew) and Esther. The king is Ahasuerus (Xerxes), and the events belong to the Persian period after the exile.

## What the verses tell us

**Chosen.** Mordecai "brought up Hadassah, that is, Esther, his uncle's daughter: for she had neither father nor mother, and the maid was fair and beautiful; whom Mordecai, when her father and mother were dead, took for his own daughter" (Esther 2:7). When the king's decree gathers maidens to Shushan under the keeper Hegai, "Esther was brought also unto the king's house" (2:8). "The maiden pleased him, and she obtained kindness of him", and he gave her seven maids and "the best place of the house of the women" (2:9). "Esther had not shewed her people nor her kindred: for Mordecai had charged her that she should not shew it" (2:10, again 2:20). When her turn came she "required nothing but what Hegai… appointed. And Esther obtained favour in the sight of all them that looked upon her" (2:15). "Esther was taken unto king Ahasuerus into his house royal in the tenth month… in the seventh year of his reign" and "the king loved Esther above all the women… so that he set the royal crown upon her head, and made her queen instead of Vashti" (2:16–17). When Mordecai learns of a plot by two chamberlains, he tells Esther, who "certified the king thereof in Mordecai's name" (2:22).

**The decree.** Haman, whom Ahasuerus has promoted, plans to destroy all the Jews because Mordecai will not bow to him (not linked to Esther: 3:8–9 and 3:13 have Haman and the king as actors). Mordecai puts on sackcloth. "Esther's maids and her chamberlains came and told it her. Then was the queen exceedingly grieved; and she sent raiment to clothe Mordecai… but he received it not" (4:4). She sends the chamberlain Hatach to ask why (4:5); he brings back the copy of the decree with a charge that she "go in unto the king, to make supplication unto him, and to make request before him for her people" (4:8–9). She replies that going in unbidden is against the law (4:10–11 in the story). Mordecai's answer: "Think not with thyself that thou shalt escape in the king's house, more than all the Jews" (4:13) and "who knoweth whether thou art come to the kingdom for such a time as this?" (4:14). Esther answers: "Go, gather together all the Jews that are present in Shushan, and fast ye for me, and neither eat nor drink three days, night or day: I also and my maidens will fast likewise; and so will I go in unto the king, which is not according to the law: and if I perish, I perish" (4:16).

**Before the king.** On the third day "Esther put on her royal apparel, and stood in the inner court of the king's house" and "she obtained favour in his sight: and the king held out to Esther the golden sceptre that was in his hand. So Esther drew near, and touched the top of the sceptre" (5:1–2). "What wilt thou, queen Esther? and what is thy request? it shall be even given thee to the half of the kingdom" (5:3). She asks that the king and Haman come to a banquet (5:4), and at the banquet defers her petition to the next day (5:6–8). At the second banquet: "let my life be given me at my petition, and my people at my request: For we are sold, I and my people, to be destroyed, to be slain, and to perish" (7:3–4). "Who is he, and where is he, that durst presume in his heart to do so?" "The adversary and enemy is this wicked Haman" (7:5–6). Haman begs Esther for his life (7:7).

**Reversal.** "On that day did the king Ahasuerus give the house of Haman the Jews' enemy unto Esther the queen. And Mordecai came before the king; for Esther had told what he was unto her" (8:1). The king gives Mordecai the ring taken from Haman and Esther sets him over Haman's house (8:2). She falls at the king's feet weeping, asking to "put away the mischief of Haman the Agagite" (8:3), and pleads "how can I endure to see the evil that shall come unto my people? or how can I endure to see the destruction of my kindred?" (8:6). A second decree lets the Jews defend themselves. After the first day's slaughter in Shushan she asks that it be repeated the next day and that Haman's ten sons be hanged (9:12–13). The king's earlier plan to destroy the Jews "should return upon his own head" (9:25).

**Purim.** "Esther the queen, the daughter of Abihail, and Mordecai the Jew, wrote with all authority, to confirm this second letter of Purim" (9:29), and "the decree of Esther confirmed these matters of Purim; and it was written in the book" (9:32).

## Relationships

- **[Mordecai](mordecai.md)**: cousin and guardian (21 shared verses). **Abihail**: her father (Mordecai's uncle).
- **Ahasuerus** (king) and **Vashti** (the queen she replaces; Esther 1). **Hegai**: keeper of the women. **Hatach**: her chamberlain who carries the messages.
- **Haman** the Agagite: the enemy she exposes; his wife **Zeresh** and ten sons.
- No husband besides the king or children are recorded.

## Reading the data

- **Nehemiah 2:6** ("the queen also sitting by him") was linked at 0.68 but is the wife of the king Nehemiah served (Artaxerxes), not Esther; it has been removed from the database.
- Verses that record Haman, Vashti and the king's decisions without Esther on stage (Esther 1, 3, 6, 9:26–28) are correctly not linked; Esther 1 is Vashti's story.
- Esther 2:5–6 introduces Mordecai, not her.
