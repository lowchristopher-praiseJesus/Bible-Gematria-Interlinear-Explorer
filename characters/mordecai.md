## Who Mordecai is

All 57 of Mordecai's links are in the book of Esther. TIPNR describes him as a Benjamite exile's descendant and Esther's cousin and guardian. He is one of the two heroes of the book and its most public one: he is the man at the gate, then the man the king honours, then the man who writes the letters that found Purim.

## What the verses tell us

**Background.** "In Shushan the palace there was a certain Jew, whose name was Mordecai, the son of Jair, the son of Shimei, the son of Kish, a Benjamite; who had been carried away from Jerusalem with the captivity which had been carried away with Jeconiah king of Judah, whom Nebuchadnezzar the king of Babylon had carried away" (Esther 2:5–6). He "took for his own daughter" Esther when her parents died (2:7). While she is in the women's house "Mordecai walked every day before the court of the women's house, to know how Esther did, and what should become of her" (2:11). He has told her not to reveal her people, and she "did the commandment of Mordecai, like as when she was brought up with him" (2:10, 2:20).

**The plot.** "Mordecai sat in the king's gate" when two chamberlains, Bigthan and Teresh, "sought to lay hand on the king Ahasuerus" (2:19–21). He tells Esther, who tells the king in his name (2:22).

**The refusal.** Haman is promoted, and "all the king's servants, that were in the king's gate, bowed, and reverenced Haman: for the king had so commanded concerning him. But Mordecai bowed not, nor did him reverence" (3:2). When they ask "Why transgressest thou the king's commandment?" (3:3), he has "told them that he was a Jew" (3:4). "When Haman saw that Mordecai bowed not… then was Haman full of wrath. And he thought scorn to lay hands on Mordecai alone… wherefore Haman sought to destroy all the Jews that were throughout the whole kingdom of Ahasuerus, even the people of Mordecai" (3:5–6).

**Sackcloth and the message.** "When Mordecai perceived all that was done, Mordecai rent his clothes, and put on sackcloth with ashes, and went out into the midst of the city, and cried with a loud and a bitter cry" (4:1). Esther sends clothes but "he received it not" (4:4). Through Hatach "Mordecai told him of all that had happened unto him, and of the sum of the money that Haman had promised to pay to the king's treasuries for the Jews, to destroy them" (4:6–7). His answer to her hesitation: "Think not with thyself that thou shalt escape in the king's house, more than all the Jews. For if thou altogether holdest thy peace at this time, then shall there enlargement and deliverance arise to the Jews from another place… who knoweth whether thou art come to the kingdom for such a time as this?" (4:13–14). Esther asks him to gather the Jews of Shushan to fast three days; "So Mordecai went his way, and did according to all that Esther had commanded him" (4:17).

**Haman's gallows.** Haman is "full of indignation" when Mordecai does not stand for him (5:9), and tells his household "Yet all this availeth me nothing, so long as I see Mordecai the Jew sitting at the king's gate" (5:13). His wife Zeresh and his friends propose "a gallows… of fifty cubits high, and to morrow speak thou unto the king that Mordecai may be hanged thereon" (5:14).

**The honour.** That night the king cannot sleep and the chronicles are read: "it was found written, that Mordecai had told of Bigthana and Teresh… What honour and dignity hath been done to Mordecai for this? Then said the king's servants… There is nothing done for him" (6:2–3). Haman comes in to ask to hang Mordecai (6:4), and the king asks him what should be done for the man he delights to honour. Haman, thinking it is himself, describes royal robes and a horse; the king says "do even so to Mordecai the Jew, that sitteth at the king's gate: let nothing fail" (6:10). "Then took Haman the apparel and the horse, and arrayed Mordecai, and brought him on horseback through the street of the city, and proclaimed before him, Thus shall it be done unto the man whom the king delighteth to honour" (6:11). Mordecai goes back to the gate while Haman hurries home "mourning, and having his head covered" (6:12). Haman's wise men say "If Mordecai be of the seed of the Jews… but shalt surely fall before him" (6:13). At the second banquet the chamberlain Harbonah says "the gallows fifty cubits high, which Haman had made for Mordecai, who had spoken good for the king, standeth in the house of Haman. Then the king said, Hang him thereon" (7:9). "So they hanged Haman on the gallows that he had prepared for Mordecai. Then was the king's wrath pacified" (7:10).

**Promoted.** "Mordecai came before the king; for Esther had told what he was unto her. And the king took off his ring, which he had taken from Haman, and gave it unto Mordecai. And Esther set Mordecai over the house of Haman" (8:1–2). The new decree is "written according to all that Mordecai commanded unto the Jews" in the languages of the 127 provinces (8:9). "Mordecai went out from the presence of the king in royal apparel of blue and white, and with a great crown of gold, and with a garment of fine linen and purple: and the city of Shushan rejoiced and was glad" (8:15). "The fear of Mordecai fell upon them… For Mordecai was great in the king's house, and his fame went out throughout all the provinces: for this man Mordecai waxed greater and greater" (9:3–4).

**Founding Purim.** "Mordecai wrote these things, and sent letters unto all the Jews that were in all the provinces… to stablish this among them, that they should keep the fourteenth day of the month Adar, and the fifteenth day of the same, yearly" (9:20–21); "the Jews undertook to do as they had begun, and as Mordecai had written unto them" (9:23). With Esther he "wrote with all authority, to confirm this second letter of Purim" (9:29, 9:31).

**The epilogue.** "All the acts of his power and of his might, and the declaration of the greatness of Mordecai, whereunto the king advanced him, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Media and Persia? For Mordecai the Jew was next unto king Ahasuerus, and great among the Jews, and accepted of the multitude of his brethren, seeking the wealth of his people, and speaking peace to all his seed" (10:2–3).

## Relationships

- **[Esther](esther.md)**: cousin and adopted daughter (21 shared verses). **Jair**: father (Mordecai's line goes back to Kish, a Benjamite, per Esther 2:5).
- **Haman** the Agagite and his wife **Zeresh**: his enemies. **Ahasuerus** (Xerxes): the king he serves.
- **Bigthan and Teresh**: the chamberlains whose plot he exposed. **Hatach**: Esther's messenger. **Harbonah**: the chamberlain who reveals the gallows.

## Reading the data

The Mordecai links are dense and reliable. The verses that describe Haman's scheme without him (3:1, 3:8–13, 9:1) and 7:4 are not linked, but the sitting-at-the-gate and honour scenes are all included.
