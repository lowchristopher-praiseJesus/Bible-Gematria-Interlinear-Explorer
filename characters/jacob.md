## Who Jacob is

Jacob is both a person and, from Exodus onward, a *name for the people*. About 390 of his 593 links are in Genesis, where the narrative is the man himself. The other ~200 are in the Psalms, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Hosea, Micah and the rest, where "Jacob" and "Israel" mostly stand for the nation descended from him (see "Reading the data").

The classifier's target here is the man; TIPNR files his name under **Israel** (he is called both), and this profile follows the same person.

## What the verses tell us

**Birth and character.** The twins are born when Isaac is sixty (Genesis 25:26): "Esau was a cunning hunter, a man of the field; and Jacob was a plain man, dwelling in tents" (25:27). Isaac loves Esau "because he did eat of his venison: but Rebekah loved Jacob" (25:28).

**The birthright.** Esau comes in faint, Jacob says "Sell me this day thy birthright", Esau swears it away for bread and lentil pottage: "thus Esau despised his birthright" (25:29–34).

**The blessing.** Rebekah tells Jacob what Isaac is about to do (27:6). Jacob goes in disguised and says "I am Esau thy first born" (27:19); Isaac feels the hands, says "The voice is Jacob's voice, but the hands are the hands of Esau" (27:22), and blesses him. Esau "hated Jacob because of the blessing… then will I slay my brother Jacob" (27:41). Rebekah sends him to her brother Laban in Haran (27:43), and Isaac charges him "Thou shalt not take a wife of the daughters of Canaan" (28:1).

**Bethel.** Sleeping on a stone on the way to Haran he dreams of "a ladder set up on the earth, and the top of it reached to heaven" with angels ascending and descending (28:12). The LORD says "I am the LORD God of Abraham thy father, and the God of Isaac: the land whereon thou liest, to thee will I give it… in thee and in thy seed shall all the families of the earth be blessed" and "I am with thee… I will not leave thee" (28:13–15). Jacob wakes: "Surely the LORD is in this place; and I knew it not" (28:16).

**Laban, Leah and Rachel.** "Jacob loved Rachel; and said, I will serve thee seven years for Rachel thy younger daughter" (29:18); the seven years "seemed unto him but a few days, for the love he had to her" (29:20). At the wedding Laban substitutes Leah: "in the morning, behold, it was Leah… wherefore then hast thou beguiled me?" (29:23–25). Laban's answer: "It must not be so done in our country, to give the younger before the firstborn"; Jacob serves another seven years for Rachel (29:26–28). The two wives and their maids Zilpah and Bilhah bear the sons (TIPNR; Genesis 29–30). He works on for wages and prospers; Laban's "countenance… was not toward him as before" (31:2). He "stole away unawares to Laban", fleeing with everything (31:20–21).

**Peniel.** Alone at night before meeting Esau, Jacob wrestles "a man… until the breaking of the day" (32:24). His thigh is put out of joint; he says "I will not let thee go, except thou bless me" (32:25–26). "Thy name shall be called no more Jacob, but Israel: for as a prince hast thou power with God and with men, and hast prevailed" (32:28). He calls the place Peniel: "I have seen God face to face, and my life is preserved" (32:30). Earlier he had prayed "O God of my father Abraham, and God of my father Isaac… Return unto thy country" (32:9).

**Reconciled with Esau.** "Esau ran to meet him, and embraced him, and fell on his neck, and kissed him: and they wept" (33:4). Jacob says he has "seen thy face, as though I had seen the face of God" (33:10).

**Dinah and Bethel again.** He hears that his daughter Dinah has been defiled and "held his peace until they were come" (34:5). God tells him "Arise, go up to Bethel, and dwell there" (35:1) and confirms "Israel shall be thy name" (35:10). Rachel dies giving birth to Benjamin near Ephrath (35:16–19); Isaac dies and his sons bury him (35:29).

**Joseph.** Israel "loved Joseph more than all his children, because he was the son of his old age", made him a coat of many colours (37:3), and his brothers hated him. He tears his clothes and mourns "many days" when told Joseph is dead (37:34). In the famine he sends the sons to buy corn (42:1), does not want to risk Benjamin (43:6), and when told Joseph is alive "Jacob's heart fainted, for he believed them not" (45:26). God says "fear not to go down into Egypt; for I will there make of thee a great nation" (46:3–4). He is set before Pharaoh and "Jacob blessed Pharaoh" (47:7).

**Blessing and death.** He adopts Joseph's sons Ephraim and Manasseh as his own (48:5) and, crossing his hands, puts the younger before the elder: "he also shall become a people… but truly his younger brother shall be greater than he" (48:14, 48:19–20). He calls all twelve sons to tell "that which shall befall you in the last days" (49:1); to Judah "the sceptre shall not depart from Judah… until Shiloh come" (49:10). He is embalmed in Egypt (50:2) and buried in Machpelah (49:31).

**As the nation.** Exodus 1:1 starts with "the children of Israel, which came into Egypt… with Jacob". Numbers 23:23: "there is no enchantment against Jacob, neither is there any divination against Israel". Psalm 105:23: "Israel also came into Egypt; and Jacob sojourned in the land of Ham". Prophets use "Jacob" as the covenant people: Isaiah 41:8 ("Israel, art my servant, Jacob whom I have chosen, the seed of Abraham my friend"), Isaiah 43:1, 44:2, Jeremiah 30:10, 46:27, Micah 2:12. Hosea 12:12 gives the one sharp backward look: "Jacob fled into the country of Syria, and Israel served for a wife, and for a wife he kept sheep". Hebrews 11:21: "By faith Jacob, when he was a dying, blessed both the sons of Joseph; and worshipped, leaning upon the top of his staff."

## Relationships

- **[Isaac](isaac.md)** and **Rebekah**: parents; 53 verses shared with Isaac. **Esau**: twin brother, later reconciled.
- **[Abraham](abraham.md)**: grandfather; the formula "the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob" links them (29 shared verses).
- **Laban**: uncle and father-in-law; **Leah** and **Rachel**: wives; **Zilpah** and **Bilhah**: their maids and his concubines.
- **[Joseph](joseph.md)**: favourite son, with whom Jacob shares 101 verses. The other sons are Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Dan, Naphtali, Gad, Asher, Issachar, Zebulun and Benjamin, plus a daughter, Dinah (TIPNR).
- **[Sarah](sarah.md)** appears only in a burial-place verse (Genesis 49:31).

## Reading the data

- Genesis is dense with Jacob, but "Israel" in the prophets is usually the people, not the man. TIPNR's own naming lists 342 verses using "Jacob" or "Israel" for him; the classifier gives about 590, so around a third are inferred from context (e.g. every "God of Jacob" in the Psalms). Treat Psalms, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Hosea, Micah and Lamentations links as *the nation named after him*.
- Genesis 44:25 ("our father said…") and Genesis 31:5, 31:7 concern Jacob (as "father") or Laban depending on the speaker; sampled links here are correct.
- Around 80 named verses fell just under 0.5, mostly more of the same "house of Jacob" and "God of Jacob" references.
