## Who Rahab is

Rahab is an outsider who becomes an insider. She is a "harlot" (Joshua 2:1, 6:17) in a city Israel is about to destroy, and yet she is the only person in Jericho spared. She is named in eight verses; the classifier links 27 because the whole of Joshua 2 is about her.

## What the verses tell us

**The hiding.** Joshua sends two men to spy out Jericho and "came into an harlot's house, named Rahab, and lodged there" (Joshua 2:1). The king sends "Bring forth the men that are come to thee" (2:3). She has already "brought them up to the roof of the house, and hid them with the stalks of flax" (2:6), and tells the king's men "There came men unto me, but I wist not whence they were… pursue after them quickly" (2:4–5), sending them toward the Jordan fords (2:7).

**Her confession.** Up on the roof she tells the spies "I know that the LORD hath given you the land, and that your terror is fallen upon us" (2:9). She says the city has heard how "the LORD dried up the water of the Red sea for you… and what ye did unto the two kings of the Amorites, that were on the other side Jordan, Sihon and Og" (2:10), and "our hearts did melt… for the LORD your God, he is God in heaven above, and in earth beneath" (2:11).

**The oath.** She asks: "swear unto me by the LORD, since I have shewed you kindness, that ye will also shew kindness unto my father's house, and give me a true token: And that ye will save alive my father, and my mother, and my brethren, and my sisters, and all that they have" (2:12–13). The spies: "Our life for yours, if ye utter not this our business" (2:14). Her house is "upon the town wall", so she lets them down "by a cord through the window" (2:15), and advises them to hide three days in the mountain (2:16). They give the sign: "thou shalt bind this line of scarlet thread in the window… and thou shalt bring thy father, and thy mother, and thy brethren… home unto thee" (2:18). Anyone who goes out into the street is responsible for himself (2:19). "She bound the scarlet line in the window" (2:21).

**The rescue.** When Jericho is placed under the ban: "only Rahab the harlot shall live, she and all that are with her in the house, because she hid the messengers that we sent" (6:17). Joshua tells the two spies to fetch "the woman, and all that she hath, as ye sware unto her" (6:22); they bring out Rahab, "her father, and her mother, and her brethren, and all that she had… and left them without the camp of Israel" (6:23). "Joshua saved Rahab the harlot alive, and her father's household… and she dwelleth in Israel even unto this day" (6:25).

**In the New Testament.**
- *Matthew 1:5:* the genealogy has "Salmon begat Booz of Rachab" (linked at 0.55), which is how she enters the line of Boaz, [Ruth](ruth.md), Jesse and David.
- *Hebrews 11:31:* "By faith the harlot Rahab perished not with them that believed not, when she had received the spies with peace."
- *James 2:25:* "was not Rahab the harlot justified by works, when she had received the messengers, and had sent them out another way?"

## Relationships

- **[Joshua](joshua.md)**: the leader whose spies she shelters and who spares her (6 shared verses).
- **The two spies**: unnamed in the text.
- **Her family**: father, mother, brothers and sisters, all saved (2:13, 6:23); the king of Jericho is her adversary.
- **Salmon** (husband, Matthew 1:5 via TIPNR) and **Boaz** (her son, marked uncertain in TIPNR): the link from Rahab to **[Ruth](ruth.md)** and, through them, to **[David](david.md)** and **[Jesus](jesus.md)** (Ruth shares one verse with her, Matthew 1:5).

## Reading the data

- All 24 Joshua links are inside chapters 2 and 6, and they are dependable. The New Testament references include every TIPNR-named verse, so there are no misses, and no other Rahab is confused with her here (the poetic "Rahab" for Egypt in Psalms and Isaiah is not linked).
- The Salmon–Rahab marriage is from Matthew 1:5 and is not stated in Joshua.
