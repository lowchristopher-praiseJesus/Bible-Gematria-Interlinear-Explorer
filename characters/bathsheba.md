## Who Bathsheba is

Bathsheba appears in only 12 verses by name (called "Bathshua" in 1 Chronicles 3:5), but the classifier links 41 verses: the story of the adultery in 2 Samuel 11–12 and the court scenes in 1 Kings 1–2 where she speaks as "the mother of Solomon". She is a woman with little voice in the sin of 2 Samuel 11 and a strong one at the end of David's life.

## What the verses tell us

**The roof.** In the spring when kings go to war, David stays in Jerusalem. "In an eveningtide, that David arose from off his bed, and walked upon the roof of the king's house: and from the roof he saw a woman washing herself; and the woman was very beautiful to look upon" (2 Samuel 11:2). His inquiry gets an answer: "Is not this Bathsheba, the daughter of Eliam, the wife of Uriah the Hittite?" (11:3). "David sent messengers, and took her; and she came in unto him, and he lay with her; for she was purified from her uncleanness: and she returned unto her house" (11:4). "The woman conceived, and sent and told David, and said, I am with child" (11:5). The text records her actions and words only briefly and says nothing of her consent.

**Uriah's death and marriage.** When Uriah is killed by David's order, "the wife of Uriah heard that Uriah her husband was dead, she mourned for her husband" (11:26). "When the mourning was past, David sent and fetched her to his house, and she became his wife, and bare him a son. But the thing that David had done displeased the LORD" (11:27).

**Nathan's charge.** In the prophet's words to David: "thou hast killed Uriah the Hittite with the sword, and hast taken his wife to be thy wife" (12:9), "the sword shall never depart from thine house; because thou hast despised me, and hast taken the wife of Uriah the Hittite to be thy wife" (12:10), and "I will raise up evil against thee out of thine own house" (12:11). The child "that Uriah's wife bare unto David" is struck (12:15) and dies. "David comforted Bathsheba his wife, and went in unto her, and lay with her: and she bare a son, and he called his name Solomon: and the LORD loved him" (12:24).

**Winning the throne for Solomon.** When David is very old and Adonijah has declared himself king, Nathan goes to "Bathsheba the mother of Solomon" with a plan: "Hast thou not heard that Adonijah the son of Haggith doth reign, and David our lord knoweth it not? Now therefore come, let me, I pray thee, give thee counsel, that thou mayest save thine own life, and the life of thy son Solomon" (1 Kings 1:11–12). She goes in to the aged king (Abishag the Shunammite is ministering to him), bows, and says "My lord, thou swarest by the LORD thy God unto thine handmaid, saying, Assuredly Solomon thy son shall reign after me" (1:15–17). Adonijah reigns now, "and thou, my lord, O king, the eyes of all Israel are upon thee… I and my son Solomon shall be counted offenders" (1:18–21). Nathan comes in on cue (1:22). David: "Call me Bathsheba" (1:28), and he swears "Assuredly Solomon thy son shall reign after me… even so will I certainly do this day" (1:30). She "bowed with her face to the earth… and said, Let my lord king David live for ever" (1:31).

**Queen mother.** Adonijah comes to her: "Comest thou peaceably?" (2:13) and asks her to speak to Solomon to give him Abishag as wife (2:15–17). She agrees "Well; I will speak for thee unto the king" (2:18). Solomon "rose up to meet her, and bowed himself unto her, and sat down on his throne, and caused a seat to be set for the king's mother; and she sat on his right hand" (2:19). Her request: "Let Abishag the Shunammite be given to Adonijah thy brother to wife" (2:21). Solomon: "why dost thou ask Abishag the Shunammite for Adonijah? ask for him the kingdom also" (2:22); Adonijah is put to death (2:23–25).

**Named elsewhere.** 1 Chronicles 3:5 lists the four sons "born unto him in Jerusalem; Shimea, and Shobab, and Nathan, and Solomon, four, of Bathshua the daughter of Ammiel". Solomon's Song 3:11 speaks of "king Solomon with the crown wherewith his mother crowned him in the day of his espousals". In Matthew's genealogy she is unnamed: "David the king begat Solomon of her that had been the wife of Urias" (Matthew 1:6).

## Relationships

- **[David](david.md)**: husband (28 shared verses) and the man who wronged her husband. **Uriah the Hittite**: first husband, killed on David's order.
- **Eliam** (or **Ammiel** in Chronicles): her father.
- **[Solomon](solomon.md)**: her son and heir (19 shared verses); **Shammua/Shimea, Shobab and Nathan**: her other sons (TIPNR).
- **Nathan the prophet**: confronts David and later advises her; **Adonijah**: the rival son of David and Haggith.
- **[Jesus](jesus.md)** is a descendant, and Matthew 1:6 keeps her in the line without naming her.

## Reading the data

- Psalm 51:5 (0.51) and 2 Samuel 12:8 (0.52) are the weakest links: the psalm is David's confession, associated with Bathsheba by its traditional title, not by the verse text. Psalm 51:1 is named in TIPNR (through the title) but scored 0.09 and was correctly missed.
- Solomon's birth naming in 2 Samuel 12:25 ("Jedidiah") is not a Bathsheba verse in this data. The link to Song of Solomon 3:11 is about "his mother" and is the only one from Wisdom literature.
