## Who John the Baptist is

John, son of the priest Zacharias and Elisabeth, is the forerunner of Jesus. The four Gospels give him about 150 verses (Matthew 40, Mark 24, Luke 49, John 37), and Acts refers back to his baptism (6). He is not [John the apostle](john_apostle.md); in Matthew, Mark and Luke he is called "the Baptist", in John's Gospel just "John" (so the classifier keeps the two apart by context).

## What the verses tell us

**Promised and born.** The angel tells Zacharias "thy prayer is heard; and thy wife Elisabeth shall bear thee a son, and thou shalt call his name John" (Luke 1:13). He will "be great in the sight of the Lord, and shall drink neither wine nor strong drink; and he shall be filled with the Holy Ghost, even from his mother's womb" (1:15), and "go before him in the spirit and power of Elias, to turn the hearts of the fathers to the children… to make ready a people prepared for the Lord" (1:17). At his circumcision the neighbours would name him after his father, but "his mother answered and said, Not so; but he shall be called John" (1:59–60; the naming scene is only partly linked). His father's prophecy: "thou, child, shalt be called the prophet of the Highest: for thou shalt go before the face of the Lord to prepare his ways" (1:76). "The child grew, and waxed strong in spirit, and was in the deserts till the day of his shewing unto Israel" (1:80).

**The preaching.** "In the fifteenth year… Annas and Caiaphas being the high priests, the word of God came unto John the son of Zacharias in the wilderness" (Luke 3:2). "In those days came John the Baptist, preaching in the wilderness of Judaea, and saying, Repent ye: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand" (Matthew 3:1–2). Matthew and Luke tie him to Isaiah: "the voice of one crying in the wilderness, Prepare ye the way of the Lord, make his paths straight" (Matthew 3:3, Luke 3:4). "His raiment of camel's hair, and a leathern girdle about his loins; and his meat was locusts and wild honey" (Matthew 3:4; Mark 1:6). "There went out unto him all the land of Judaea, and they of Jerusalem, and were all baptized of him in the river of Jordan, confessing their sins" (Mark 1:5). To the Pharisees and Sadducees he says "O generation of vipers, who hath warned you to flee from the wrath to come? Bring forth therefore fruits meet for repentance" (Matthew 3:7–8). The crowd, publicans and soldiers each ask "What shall we do?" and he says "He that hath two coats, let him impart to him that hath none… Exact no more than that which is appointed you… Do violence to no man… be content with your wages" (Luke 3:10–14). He describes the one to come: "I indeed baptize you with water unto repentance: but he that cometh after me is mightier than I, whose shoes I am not worthy to bear: he shall baptize you with the Holy Ghost, and with fire" (Matthew 3:11; Luke 3:16).

**Baptizing Jesus.** "Then cometh Jesus from Galilee to Jordan unto John, to be baptized of him. But John forbad him, saying, I have need to be baptized of thee, and comest thou to me?" (Matthew 3:13–14). Jesus: "Suffer it to be so now: for thus it becometh us to fulfil all righteousness" (3:15).

**Witness in John's Gospel.** "John bare witness of him, and cried, saying, This was he of whom I spake, He that cometh after me is preferred before me: for he was before me" (John 1:15). Asked by priests and Levites from Jerusalem who he is, "he confessed, and denied not; but confessed, I am not the Christ" (1:20); asked if he is Elias, "I am not" (1:21); "I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness, Make straight the way of the Lord, as said the prophet Esaias" (1:23). "I baptize with water: but there standeth one among you, whom ye know not; he it is, who coming after me is preferred before me" (1:26–27). "The next day John seeth Jesus coming unto him, and saith, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world" (1:29). "I saw the Spirit descending from heaven like a dove, and it abode upon him… this is the Son of God" (1:32–34). Two of his disciples follow Jesus when he says "Behold the Lamb of God!" a second time (1:35–36). John was baptizing "in Aenon near to Salim, because there was much water there" (3:23), and answers his disciples' worry that Jesus is now baptizing more: "A man can receive nothing, except it be given him from heaven… He must increase, but I must decrease" (3:26–30). Jesus later says "Ye sent unto John, and he bare witness unto the truth… He was a burning and a shining light" (5:33–35). "John did no miracle: but all things that John spake of this man were true" (10:41).

**Imprisoned and killed.** He reproved Herod the tetrarch "for Herodias his brother Philip's wife" (Luke 3:19): "It is not lawful for thee to have her" (Matthew 14:3–4), and Herod "shut up John in prison" (Luke 3:20). Mark adds "Herod feared John, knowing that he was a just man and an holy, and observed him; and when he heard him, he did many things, and heard him gladly" (6:20), while Herodias "had a quarrel against him, and would have killed him; but she could not" (6:19). Herodias' daughter, told by her mother, asks for "the head of John the Baptist" (6:24–25); the king "sent an executioner, and commanded his head to be brought: and he went and beheaded him in the prison" and gave the head to the damsel and her mother (6:27–28; Matthew 14:10).

**From prison.** "When John had heard in the prison the works of Christ, he sent two of his disciples" (Matthew 11:2) to ask "Art thou he that should come, or do we look for another?" (11:3; Luke 7:19). Jesus answers: "Go and shew John again those things which ye do hear and see" (11:4), the blind seeing, the lepers cleansed and the dead raised (Luke 7:22). To the crowd about John: "What went ye out for to see? A prophet? yea, I say unto you, and more than a prophet. For this is he, of whom it is written, Behold, I send my messenger before thy face" (Matthew 11:9–10). "Among them that are born of women there hath not risen a greater than John the Baptist: notwithstanding he that is least in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he" (11:11). "The law and the prophets were until John" (Luke 16:16; Matthew 11:13). "If ye will receive it, this is Elias, which was for to come" (Matthew 11:14; also 17:12–13, Mark 9:13). Of the generation he criticised: "John came neither eating nor drinking, and they say, He hath a devil" (Matthew 11:18, Luke 7:33).

**His baptism as a test.** Jesus challenges the chief priests: "The baptism of John, whence was it? from heaven, or of men?" and they fear answering because "all hold John as a prophet" (Matthew 21:25–26; Mark 11:30–32; Luke 20:4–6). "John came unto you in the way of righteousness, and ye believed him not: but the publicans and the harlots believed him" (Matthew 21:32). John's disciples ask why they and the Pharisees fast but Jesus' disciples do not (Matthew 9:14, Mark 2:18, Luke 5:33). Herod hears of Jesus and is perplexed that some said "John was risen from the dead" (Luke 9:7); "John have I beheaded: but who is this?" (9:9).

**After his death.** Paul (Acts 13:24–25) recalls "When John had first preached before his coming the baptism of repentance", quoting "Whom think ye that I am? I am not he." Peter (Acts 10:37) speaks of "the baptism which John preached" and (11:16) "John indeed baptized with water; but ye shall be baptized with the Holy Ghost". At Ephesus, disciples say they were baptized "Unto John's baptism" and Paul explains that John "baptized with the baptism of repentance, saying unto the people, that they should believe on him which should come after him, that is, on Christ Jesus" (Acts 19:3–4).

**Old Testament links.** The classifier also links Isaiah 40:3 ("the voice of him that crieth in the wilderness", 0.67) and Malachi 3:1 ("I will send my messenger, and he shall prepare the way", 0.60): the verses the Gospels apply to him.

## Relationships

- **Zacharias** and **Elisabeth**: parents; Elisabeth was a relative of Mary (Luke 1:36, not in the link set). **[Mary](mary_mother.md)** visits Elisabeth while carrying Jesus and shares 3 verses.
- **[Jesus](jesus.md)**: the one he baptizes and announces (83 shared verses). **[Elijah](elijah.md)**: the prophet he is said to be like or to be (7 shared verses). **[Peter](peter.md)** shares John 1:40 and Acts 11:16, and **[John the apostle](john_apostle.md)** shares John 1:37 (the two disciples who leave the Baptist to follow Jesus).
- **Herod Antipas** and **Herodias**: the couple he denounced. **Herodias' daughter**: asks for his head. His **disciples** bury him (Matthew 14:12).
- **[Isaiah](isaiah.md)**: the prophet quoted about him (1 shared verse).

## Reading the data

- **Two Johns.** No John the apostle is confused with the Baptist in this list, apart from John 1:37 where both are named (Jesus' first disciples are two of the Baptist's followers).
- **The Old Testament links** are messianic readings applied by the Gospels, not passages about him in their own context.
- **Named but missed:** Mark 6:24 (0.48), Matthew 14:8 (0.42), John 10:40, 5:36 and 4:1, Luke 1:63 and Acts 1:22, 1:5, 18:25 (all "John's baptism" or the daughter asking for his head).
- The baptism verse itself (Matthew 3:16) is not linked (0.17), because the subject is Jesus and the Spirit.
