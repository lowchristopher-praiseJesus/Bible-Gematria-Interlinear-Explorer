## Who Cain is

Cain is the first person born to humans. He appears in Genesis 4 (16 linked verses) and is held up in the New Testament as the example of the wicked brother (Hebrews 11:4, 1 John 3:12).

## What the verses tell us

**Birth and work.** Eve "bare Cain, and said, I have gotten a man from the LORD" (Genesis 4:1). Abel "was a keeper of sheep, but Cain was a tiller of the ground" (4:2).

**The rejected offering.** "In process of time… Cain brought of the fruit of the ground an offering unto the LORD" (4:3). Abel brought firstlings of his flock and "the LORD had respect unto Abel and to his offering: But unto Cain and to his offering he had not respect" (4:4–5). Cain "was very wroth, and his countenance fell."

**God's warning.** The LORD asks "Why art thou wroth?" and says "If thou doest well, shalt thou not be accepted? and if thou doest not well, sin lieth at the door. And unto thee shall be his desire, and thou shalt rule over him" (4:6–7).

**The murder.** "Cain talked with Abel his brother: and it came to pass, when they were in the field, that Cain rose up against Abel his brother, and slew him" (4:8). The LORD's question, "Where is Abel thy brother?", gets a lie and a retort: "I know not: Am I my brother's keeper?" (4:9). God answers "the voice of thy brother's blood crieth unto me from the ground" (4:10).

**Curse and mark.** Cain is "cursed from the earth", the ground will no longer yield to him, and he will be "a fugitive and a vagabond" (4:11–12). His response is "My punishment is greater than I can bear" and he fears "every one that findeth me shall slay me" (4:13–14). God promises sevenfold vengeance on anyone who kills him and "set a mark upon Cain, lest any finding him should kill him" (4:15).

**Exile and family.** "Cain went out from the presence of the LORD, and dwelt in the land of Nod, on the east of Eden" (4:16). There "Cain knew his wife; and she conceived, and bare Enoch: and he builded a city, and called the name of the city, after the name of his son" (4:17). The text does not name his wife or say where she came from. Verses 4:18–24 (not linked to Cain by the classifier) trace his line down to Lamech, whose "if Cain shall be avenged sevenfold, truly Lamech seventy and sevenfold" (4:24) shows Cain's story had become a byword.

**In the New Testament.** 1 John 3:12 is the plainest comment: "Not as Cain, who was of that wicked one, and slew his brother. And wherefore slew he him? Because his own works were evil, and his brother's righteous." Hebrews 11:4 sets Cain's offering against Abel's: "By faith Abel offered unto God a more excellent sacrifice than Cain". (Jude 11 names Cain in a warning; the classifier did not link it.)

## Relationships

- **[Adam](adam.md) and [Eve](eve.md)**: parents (Genesis 4:1).
- **[Abel](abel.md)**: younger brother, murdered by him (4:8). They share 7 verses in this index. **Seth**: a later brother, born "instead of Abel, whom Cain slew" (4:25).
- **Enoch**: his son, for whom Cain named the first city (4:17). This is Enoch the Cainite, not the Enoch who "walked with God" in Genesis 5.
- **His wife**: unnamed in the text.

## Reading the data
