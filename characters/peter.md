## Who Peter is

Peter (also Simon, Simeon, Cephas and "Simon Barjona") is the most frequently named disciple in the Gospels and the central figure of Acts 1–12 (167 of his 438 links). Two letters carry his name (1 Peter 12, 2 Peter 21). Paul mentions him as Cephas. In the data this is the fisherman-apostle; Simon the Zealot, Simon the tanner and Simon Magus are separate men, and the classifier keeps them out.

## What the verses tell us

**Called.** "Jesus, walking by the sea of Galilee, saw two brethren, Simon called Peter, and Andrew his brother, casting a net into the sea: for they were fishers" (Matthew 4:18). In John 1:41 Andrew "first findeth his own brother Simon, and saith unto him, We have found the Messias". In Luke 5:5 Simon says "Master, we have toiled all the night, and have taken nothing: nevertheless at thy word I will let down the net", and after the catch "Simon Peter… fell down at Jesus' knees, saying, Depart from me; for I am a sinful man, O Lord" (5:8). The lists of the twelve begin with him: "The first, Simon, who is called Peter, and Andrew his brother" (Matthew 10:2; Mark 3:16; Luke 6:14). Jesus heals his wife's mother of a fever in "Peter's house" (Matthew 8:14; Luke 4:38). He is one of the inner three, with James and John, at the raising of Jairus' daughter (Mark 5:37; Luke 8:51), the Transfiguration (Mark 9:2), the Mount of Olives discourse (Mark 13:3) and Gethsemane (14:33).

**On the water.** "Lord, if it be thou, bid me come unto thee on the water" (Matthew 14:28). "When Peter was come down out of the ship, he walked on the water, to go to Jesus. But when he saw the wind boisterous, he was afraid; and beginning to sink, he cried, saying, Lord, save me. And immediately Jesus stretched forth his hand, and caught him, and said unto him, O thou of little faith, wherefore didst thou doubt?" (14:29–31).

**The confession.** "But whom say ye that I am? And Peter answereth and saith unto him, Thou art the Christ" (Mark 8:29); in Matthew, "Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God" (16:16), and in Luke, "The Christ of God" (9:20). Jesus: "Blessed art thou, Simon Barjona: for flesh and blood hath not revealed it unto thee, but my Father which is in heaven. And I say also unto thee, That thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my church; and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it. And I will give unto thee the keys of the kingdom of heaven" (Matthew 16:17–19). Moments later, when Jesus speaks of his death, "Peter took him, and began to rebuke him, saying, Be it far from thee, Lord", and is told "Get thee behind me, Satan: thou art an offence unto me" (16:22–23).

**Questions from Peter.** He asks for the parable to be explained (Matthew 15:15), "how oft shall my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? till seven times?" (18:21), "Behold, we have forsaken all, and followed thee; what shall we have therefore?" (19:27; Mark 10:28; Luke 18:28) and "speakest thou this parable unto us, or even to all?" (Luke 12:41). At the Transfiguration he proposes three tabernacles, "not knowing what he said" (Matthew 17:4; Luke 9:33). In John 6:68, when many leave, "Simon Peter answered him, Lord, to whom shall we go? thou hast the words of eternal life."

**Foot-washing and Gethsemane.** At the last supper "Peter saith unto him, Thou shalt never wash my feet. Jesus answered him, If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me. Simon Peter saith unto him, Lord, not my feet only, but also my hands and my head" (John 13:8–9). Jesus sends "Peter and John" to prepare the passover (Luke 22:8). "Simon, Simon, behold, Satan hath desired to have you, that he may sift you as wheat: but I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren" (Luke 22:31–32). He answers "Though all men shall be offended because of thee, yet will I never be offended" (Matthew 26:33), and Jesus foretells that "the cock shall not crow this day, before that thou shalt thrice deny that thou knowest me" (Luke 22:34). In Gethsemane Jesus finds the three asleep: "Simon, sleepest thou? couldest not thou watch one hour?" (Mark 14:37). Peter "having a sword drew it, and smote the high priest's servant, and cut off his right ear. The servant's name was Malchus" (John 18:10).

**The denial and restoration.** In the high priest's courtyard a maid says "Thou also wast with Jesus of Galilee", and he "denied before them all, saying, I know not what thou sayest" (Matthew 26:69–70). He denies again "with an oath, I do not know the man" (26:72), then "began he to curse and to swear, saying, I know not the man. And immediately the cock crew" (26:74). "The Lord turned, and looked upon Peter. And Peter remembered the word of the Lord… And Peter went out, and wept bitterly" (Luke 22:61–62). On the Sunday the angel says "tell his disciples and Peter that he goeth before you into Galilee" (Mark 16:7). "Then arose Peter, and ran unto the sepulchre" and went away wondering (Luke 24:12; John 20:3). By the lake Jesus asks three times "Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me?" and answers "Feed my lambs… Feed my sheep" (John 21:15–17); Peter "was grieved because he said unto him the third time" and answers "Lord, thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee" (21:17). Jesus adds that when he is old "another shall gird thee, and carry thee whither thou wouldest not" (21:18).

**Leader in Acts.** After the ascension "Peter stood up in the midst of the disciples" (Acts 1:15) and the lot falls on Matthias. At Pentecost "Peter, standing up with the eleven, lifted up his voice" (2:14) and tells the hearers "Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and ye shall receive the gift of the Holy Ghost" (2:38); "about three thousand souls" are added (2:41). With John at the temple gate: "Silver and gold have I none; but such as I have give I thee: In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth rise up and walk" (3:6). Before the council, "filled with the Holy Ghost" (4:8), "when they saw the boldness of Peter and John… they took knowledge of them, that they had been with Jesus" (4:13). He tells Ananias "why hath Satan filled thine heart to lie to the Holy Ghost?" (5:3), and people bring the sick so "the shadow of Peter passing by might overshadow some of them" (5:15). In Samaria he rebukes Simon the sorcerer: "Thy money perish with thee, because thou hast thought that the gift of God may be purchased with money" (8:20). He heals Aeneas at Lydda: "Jesus Christ maketh thee whole: arise, and make thy bed" (9:34).

**Cornelius.** On a housetop at Joppa "about the sixth hour" he prays and sees the sheet of animals: "Not so, Lord; for I have never eaten any thing that is common or unclean" (10:9–14). At Cornelius' house he says "Of a truth I perceive that God is no respecter of persons" (10:34). Back in Jerusalem "they that were of the circumcision contended with him" (11:2). At the Jerusalem council he reminds them "a good while ago God made choice among us, that the Gentiles by my mouth should hear the word of the gospel, and believe" (15:7).

**Prison.** Herod imprisons him: "Peter therefore was kept in prison: but prayer was made without ceasing of the church unto God for him" (12:5). An angel "smote Peter on the side, and raised him up, saying, Arise up quickly. And his chains fell off from his hands" (12:7), and afterwards "Now I know of a surety, that the LORD hath sent his angel, and hath delivered me out of the hand of Herod" (12:11).

**In Paul's letters.** "Every one of you saith, I am of Paul; and I of Apollos; and I of Cephas; and I of Christ" (1 Corinthians 1:12). Christ "was seen of Cephas, then of the twelve" (15:5). Paul went up to Jerusalem "to see Peter, and abode with him fifteen days" (Galatians 1:18), notes that the gospel of the circumcision was committed to Peter (2:7–8), and "when Peter was come to Antioch, I withstood him to the face, because he was to be blamed" (2:11).

**His letters.** "Peter, an apostle of Jesus Christ, to the strangers scattered throughout Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia" (1 Peter 1:1). "The elders which are among you I exhort, who am also an elder, and a witness of the sufferings of Christ" (5:1); "the church that is at Babylon… saluteth you; and so doth Marcus my son" (5:13). "Simon Peter, a servant and an apostle of Jesus Christ" (2 Peter 1:1); "shortly I must put off this my tabernacle, even as our Lord Jesus Christ hath shewed me" (1:14); "our beloved brother Paul… hath written unto you" (3:15).
