## Who David is

David is the second most-linked person in the index (1,857 verses) after Jesus. His link count comes from three sources: the narrative in 1 Samuel 16 to 1 Kings 2 and 1 Chronicles (1,119 verses), the Psalms (663), and a scatter of later references, including the New Testament's use of him as the ancestor and prophet-king (about 40 verses).

The 663 Psalms links are a special case: they come from the classifier reading first-person psalms as David speaking. The KJV text used here has no psalm titles ("A Psalm of David"), so this is an inference (usually a sound one, since most of them are traditionally his), not something stated in the verse text. See "Reading the data".

## What the verses tell us

**Chosen.** God tells Samuel to go to "Jesse the Bethlehemite: for I have provided me a king among his sons" (1 Samuel 16:1). When seven sons have passed, Samuel asks "Are here all thy children?" and Jesse says "There remaineth yet the youngest, and, behold, he keepeth the sheep" (16:11). "Now he was ruddy, and withal of a beautiful countenance, and goodly to look to. And the LORD said, Arise, anoint him: for this is he" (16:12). "Samuel took the horn of oil, and anointed him in the midst of his brethren: and the Spirit of the LORD came upon David from that day forward" (16:13). He goes to Saul as a harpist and becomes his armour-bearer: "he loved him greatly" (16:21), while still going home "to feed his father's sheep at Bethlehem" (17:15).

**Goliath.** To the giant he says "Thou comest to me with a sword, and with a spear, and with a shield: but I come to thee in the name of the LORD of hosts… the battle is the LORD's" (17:45, 17:47). "David put his hand in his bag, and took thence a stone, and slang it, and smote the Philistine in his forehead… So David prevailed over the Philistine with a sling and with a stone… but there was no sword in the hand of David" (17:49–50; see [Goliath](goliath.md) and [Saul](saul.md)).

**Jonathan and Saul's jealousy.** "The soul of Jonathan was knit with the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul" (18:1). Saul is enraged by the women's song about "his thousands… ten thousands" (18:7–8) and twice throws a javelin (18:10–11). Jonathan warns David (19:2). David asks him "What have I done? what is mine iniquity?" (20:1), and they part in tears, having sworn "The LORD be between me and thee, and between my seed and thy seed for ever" (20:41–42; see [Jonathan](jonathan.md)).

**Outlaw.** At Nob he eats the priests' bread and takes Goliath's sword (21:1–2). At the cave of Adullam his family and every kind of distressed man gather to him (22:1). He asks God before every move ("David enquired of the LORD, saying, Shall I go and smite these Philistines?", 23:2), saves Keilah, and twice spares the sleeping Saul: "the LORD forbid that I should do this thing unto my master, the LORD's anointed" (24:6; 26:4). He tells himself "I shall now perish one day by the hand of Saul" and flees to the Philistine king Achish (27:1), who lets him live at Ziklag; the Philistine lords refuse to let him fight against Saul at Gilboa (29:2–4). Returning, he finds Ziklag burned and "their wives, and their sons, and their daughters… taken captives" (30:3).

**King.** After Saul's death David asks the LORD where to go and is told "Unto Hebron" (2 Samuel 2:1). His lament for Saul and Jonathan says of Jonathan "thy love to me was wonderful, passing the love of women" (1:26). The elders come to Hebron: "they anointed David king over Israel" (5:3). "David was thirty years old when he began to reign, and he reigned forty years… In Hebron he reigned over Judah seven years and six months: and in Jerusalem he reigned thirty and three years" (5:4–5). He takes "the strong hold of Zion: the same is the city of David" (5:7). He brings the ark to Jerusalem: "David danced before the LORD with all his might", and Michal "despised him in her heart" (6:14–16). He wins wars (8:1, 1 Chronicles 18:3) and shows kindness to Jonathan's crippled son Mephibosheth (2 Samuel 9:1–7).

**The covenant.** David wants to build a house for the ark (1 Chronicles 17:1); God says his son will build it and promises "thine house and thy kingdom shall be established for ever before thee: thy throne shall be established for ever" (2 Samuel 7:12–16). David responds "Who am I, O Lord GOD?" (7:18). In 1 Chronicles 22:8 he tells Solomon that God said "Thou hast shed blood abundantly… thou shalt not build an house unto my name".

**Bathsheba and Uriah.** "In an eveningtide, that David arose from off his bed, and walked upon the roof of the king's house: and from the roof he saw a woman washing herself" (11:2). He asks who she is: "Is not this Bathsheba, the daughter of Eliam, the wife of Uriah the Hittite?" (11:3). He sends for her, "and he lay with her" (11:4), and she tells him "I am with child" (11:5). He writes to Joab to set Uriah "in the forefront of the hottest battle, and retire ye from him, that he may be smitten, and die" (11:15). Nathan's parable ends "Thou art the man" (12:7). David: "I have sinned against the LORD"; Nathan: "The LORD also hath put away thy sin; thou shalt not die. Howbeit… the child also that is born unto thee shall surely die" (12:13–14). He fasts while the child is alive and stops when it dies: "I shall go to him, but he shall not return to me" (12:22–23). He then comforts Bathsheba and their next son is Solomon, "and the LORD loved him" (12:24). Psalm 51 is the psalm of confession: "Have mercy upon me, O God… Wash me throughly from mine iniquity… my sin is ever before me" (51:1–3).

**Family collapse.** Amnon wrongs his half-sister Tamar; David sends Tamar to him with food (13:7). Absalom "stole the hearts of the men of Israel" (15:6) and David has to flee Jerusalem: "Arise, and let us flee; for we shall not else escape from Absalom" (15:14). He is cursed by a Benjamite and tells Abishai "let him alone, and let him curse; for the LORD hath bidden him" (16:11). He crosses the Jordan (17:22), organises captains of thousands and hundreds (18:1), and on hearing of Absalom's death weeps: "O my son Absalom, my son, my son Absalom! would God I had died for thee" (18:33). He rebukes the sons of Zeruiah after the return (19:22).

**Census and old age.** "David's heart smote him after that he had numbered the people. And David said unto the LORD, I have sinned greatly" (24:10). He chooses "let us fall now into the hand of the LORD; for his mercies are great" (24:14) and pleads "these sheep, what have they done? let thine hand, I pray thee, be against me" (24:17). In 1 Kings 1:32 he commands the anointing of Solomon by Zadok, Nathan and Benaiah, and in 2:1 he charges Solomon before dying. "David slept with his fathers, and was buried in the city of David" (2:10–11). 1 Chronicles 28–29 records his final gathering: "Blessed be thou, LORD God of Israel our father, for ever and ever" (29:10).

**Psalms.** Verses such as "I have set the LORD always before me: because he is at my right hand, I shall not be moved" (Psalm 16:8), "I laid me down and slept; I awaked; for the LORD sustained me" (3:5), "My soul shall be satisfied as with marrow and fatness" (63:5) and "Why art thou cast down, O my soul?" (42:11) are the personal prayers the classifier ties to David. The Psalms also speak of him in the third person: "to his anointed, to David, and to his seed for evermore" (18:50), "I have found David my servant; with my holy oil have I anointed him" (89:20), "Lord, remember David, and all his afflictions" (132:1).

**Later writers.** Kings and Chronicles refer to the "city of David" and "David thy father" (1 Kings 8:18, 2 Chronicles 6:8). Jesus in Mark 2:25 asks "Have ye never read what David did, when he had need, and was an hungred?" and in Matthew 22:43–45 asks how David can call the Messiah "Lord" if he is his son. Peter in Acts 2:29–31 says David is "both dead and buried, and his sepulchre is with us unto this day", but was "a prophet" who "seeing this before spake of the resurrection of Christ". Paul in Acts 13:22 quotes God: "I have found David the son of Jesse, a man after mine own heart, which shall fulfil all my will", and 13:36: "David, after he had served his own generation by the will of God, fell on sleep". Hebrews 11:32 lists him among the men of faith.

## Relationships

- **Jesse**: father, of Bethlehem, and David's seven older brothers (**Eliab**, **Abinadab**, **Shimeah** and others, TIPNR).
- **[Saul](saul.md)**: the king who anointed him as a court musician, then hunted him (212 shared verses). **[Samuel](samuel.md)**: who anointed him (12). **[Jonathan](jonathan.md)**: his closest friend (59). **[Goliath](goliath.md)**: the giant he killed (20).
- **Wives:** **Michal** (Saul's daughter), **Abigail**, **Ahinoam**, **Maacah**, **Haggith**, **Abital**, **Eglah** and **[Bathsheba](bathsheba.md)** (28 shared verses).
- **Sons:** Amnon, Absalom, Adonijah, **[Solomon](solomon.md)** (107 shared verses), Nathan and many others; **Tamar** is a daughter (TIPNR lists over twenty children).
- **Nathan** and **Gad** (prophets), **Joab**, **Abishai** and **Abner** (commanders), **Uriah**, **Mephibosheth**, **Absalom**.
- **[Jesus](jesus.md)**: descendant, in 37 shared verses; **[Peter](peter.md)** and **[Jacob](jacob.md)** appear beside him in speeches (Acts 2; Psalm passages).

## Reading the data

- **Psalms (663 links).** A first-person psalm is linked to David even when the KJV text does not name him. Links fall in 76 of the 150 psalms, which roughly matches the psalms traditionally called his; the classifier had only the verse and its neighbours to go on, so it is inferring this from the tone of the prayer. Psalm 23:1 ("The LORD is my shepherd") is 0.35 and not linked; Psalm 110:1 is 0.55. This is the biggest single soft area in his profile.
- **Titles and epithets are missed.** Matthew 1:1 ("son of David", 0.11), Romans 1:3 (0.19), Luke 1:32 (0.40), and "the house of David" verses (Zechariah 12:12, 13:1, Isaiah 55:3, Acts 13:34) are named in TIPNR but fall below 0.5: 214 named verses in total.
- **Long narrative runs are well covered:** 1 Samuel 16–2 Samuel 24 is dense and generally above 0.9. The census plague (2 Samuel 24:15–16) is the kind of verse that drops when David is not the grammatical subject.
- Sampled links between 0.5 and 0.7 (for example 2 Samuel 7:13–15, 15:6) are verses about David's heir or about Absalom where David is only the implied "the king".
