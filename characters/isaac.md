## Who Isaac is

Isaac is the middle patriarch and the one whose story is shortest and quietest: he is mostly acted upon (offered, married by arrangement, tricked into a blessing). 130 of his 156 links are in Genesis 17–35. The rest are the formula "the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob" and New Testament arguments about the child of promise.

## What the verses tell us

**Promised and born.** "Sarah thy wife shall bear thee a son indeed; and thou shalt call his name Isaac: and I will establish my covenant with him for an everlasting covenant" (Genesis 17:19, 17:21). Abraham circumcises him at eight days (21:4) and "was an hundred years old, when his son Isaac was born unto him" (21:5). Sarah says "who would have said unto Abraham, that Sarah should have given children suck?" (21:7). At the weaning feast (21:8) Sarah demands that Hagar and Ishmael be sent away "for the son of this bondwoman shall not be heir with my son, even with Isaac" (21:10); God confirms "in Isaac shall thy seed be called" (21:12).

**Offered.** God says "Take now thy son, thine only son Isaac, whom thou lovest… and offer him there for a burnt offering" (22:2). Abraham lays the wood "upon Isaac his son" (22:6). Isaac's one question: "Behold the fire and the wood: but where is the lamb for a burnt offering?" and Abraham's answer "God will provide himself a lamb" (22:7–8). He is bound on the altar (22:9), the angel stops the knife (22:12) and a ram is offered "in the stead of his son" (22:13). Hebrews 11:17–19 says Abraham was "Accounting that God was able to raise him up, even from the dead; from whence also he received him in a figure".

**A wife from the family.** Abraham sends his servant to his own kindred, "take a wife unto my son Isaac" (24:4) and not from the Canaanites (24:3, 24:37–38). When the servant meets Rebekah (24:51) Isaac is "out to meditate in the field at the eventide" (24:63). She sees him and covers herself with her veil (24:64–65). "Isaac brought her into his mother Sarah's tent, and took Rebekah, and she became his wife; and he loved her: and Isaac was comforted after his mother's death" (24:67).

**Heir and twin sons.** "Abraham gave all that he had unto Isaac" (25:5). He is forty when he marries (25:20), and "intreated the LORD for his wife, because she was barren" (25:21). He is sixty when the twins are born (25:26). "Isaac loved Esau, because he did eat of his venison: but Rebekah loved Jacob" (25:28).

**Gerar and the wells.** In a famine God tells him "Go not down into Egypt" and repeats to him the promise sworn to Abraham (26:1–3). Like his father he says of his wife "She is my sister", and Abimelech sees "Isaac was sporting with Rebekah his wife" (26:7–9). Isaac sows and reaps "an hundredfold" and grows very rich (26:12–14); the Philistines envy him, and Abimelech sends him away (26:16). He re-digs Abraham's wells (26:18), and the herdsmen quarrel over Esek and Sitnah before Rehoboth ("the LORD hath made room for us", 26:20–22). At Beersheba he builds an altar and calls on the LORD (26:25) and makes peace with Abimelech (26:27–31).

**The stolen blessing.** Old and blind, he asks Esau for venison "that my soul may bless thee before I die" (27:1–4). Rebekah and Jacob substitute goat-meat and skins. Isaac is uneasy ("The voice is Jacob's voice, but the hands are the hands of Esau", 27:22) but blesses Jacob (27:23–27). When Esau arrives "Isaac trembled very exceedingly" (27:33); he can only say "I have made him thy lord" (27:37) and give Esau a lesser word (27:39).

**Sending Jacob away.** He calls Jacob, blesses him and orders him not to take a Canaanite wife but to go to Padanaram, to Laban (28:1–2, 28:5). He sees Esau's wives "grief of mind unto Isaac and to Rebekah" (26:35).

**Death.** Jacob comes to him at Mamre/Hebron; "the days of Isaac were an hundred and fourscore years" and he "gave up the ghost… old and full of days", buried by Esau and Jacob (35:27–29). He lies in the cave of Machpelah with Rebekah (49:31).

**In later texts.**
- The covenant formula: Exodus 6:3, 32:13; Leviticus 26:42; Deuteronomy 9:27; Psalm 105:9 ("his oath unto Isaac").
- *Genealogies:* 1 Chronicles 1:28, 1:34; Matthew 1:2; Acts 7:8.
- *Romans 9:7–10:* "In Isaac shall thy seed be called"; Rebecca "had conceived by one, even by our father Isaac".
- *Galatians 4:23–29:* the son of the freewoman born "by promise", persecuted by the one born after the flesh.
- *Hebrews 11:9, 11:18, 11:20:* he lived in tents as an heir of the same promise, and "by faith Isaac blessed Jacob and Esau concerning things to come".

## Relationships

- **[Abraham](abraham.md)** and **[Sarah](sarah.md)**: father and mother. Isaac shares 61 verses with Abraham and 11 with Sarah. **Ishmael** (by Hagar) is his older half-brother and the other son at Abraham's burial (Genesis 25:9).
- **Rebekah**: wife; from Padanaram, sister to Laban (25:20). Not one of the tracked characters.
- **Esau** and **[Jacob](jacob.md)**: twin sons; 53 shared verses with Jacob.
- **Abimelech**, king of the Philistines at Gerar, is the neighbour he deals with in Genesis 26.

## Reading the data

- Seven wrong links were removed from the database: Genesis 21:14–19 (Hagar and the boy in the wilderness, where the child is **Ishmael**) and Genesis 31:7 ("your father hath deceived me", which is Laban). Genesis 31:5 ("the God of my father hath been with me", 0.50) was left in, since "my father" there most likely means Isaac.
- The NT links at 0.50–0.65 (Romans 9:7, Galatians 4:22, 4:29) are about the "children of promise" idea; Isaac is the type, not the subject.
- Named but missed (below 0.5): the "God of Abraham, of Isaac, and of Jacob" verses in Exodus 3, Deuteronomy 6 and 30, Matthew 8:11 and 22:32, Acts 3:13, plus Amos 7:9, 7:16 ("the house of Isaac") where Isaac is the nation.
