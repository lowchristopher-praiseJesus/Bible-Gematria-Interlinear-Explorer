## Who Nehemiah is

The whole biography is in the book of Nehemiah (118 links, all in it). Most of the book is his memoir in the first person ("I"), so most links are "I" verses. He is named in only five verses, and the book calls him "Nehemiah, which is the Tirshatha" (Nehemiah 8:9), the Persian word for governor. He is not the Nehemiah in the lists of returned exiles (Ezra 2:2, Nehemiah 7:7).

## What the verses tell us

**The report.** "The words of Nehemiah the son of Hachaliah. And it came to pass in the month Chisleu, in the twentieth year, as I was in Shushan the palace" (Nehemiah 1:1). His brother "Hanani, one of my brethren, came, he and certain men of Judah" and he asked about the Jews and Jerusalem (1:2). Their answer: "the remnant that are left of the captivity there in the province are in great affliction and reproach: the wall of Jerusalem also is broken down, and the gates thereof are burned with fire" (1:3). He "sat down and wept, and mourned certain days, and fasted, and prayed before the God of heaven" (1:4). His prayer begins "I beseech thee, O LORD God of heaven, the great and terrible God, that keepeth covenant and mercy" and ends "prosper, I pray thee, thy servant this day, and grant him mercy in the sight of this man. For I was the king's cupbearer" (1:5, 1:11).

**Before the king.** In Nisan in the twentieth year of Artaxerxes "wine was before him: and I took up the wine, and gave it unto the king. Now I had not been beforetime sad in his presence" (2:1). The king asks "Why is thy countenance sad, seeing thou art not sick? this is nothing else but sorrow of heart. Then I was very sore afraid" (2:2). When asked "For what dost thou make request?", he writes "So I prayed to the God of heaven" and asks to be sent "unto Judah, unto the city of my fathers' sepulchres, that I may build it" (2:4–5). The king gives him a time-limited leave, letters to the governors beyond the river and to Asaph, keeper of the king's forest, for timber (2:6–8).

**The night ride and the call.** He reaches Jerusalem and after three days rides out by night with a few men, telling no one "what my God had put in my heart to do at Jerusalem" (2:11–12). To the people: "Ye see the distress that we are in, how Jerusalem lieth waste… come, and let us build up the wall of Jerusalem, that we be no more a reproach" (2:17). He tells them of "the hand of my God which was good upon me" and the king's words, and "they strengthened their hands for this good work" (2:18). Sanballat the Horonite, Tobiah the Ammonite and Geshem the Arabian "laughed us to scorn… What is this thing that ye do? will ye rebel against the king?" (2:19). His reply: "The God of heaven, he will prosper us; therefore we his servants will arise and build: but ye have no portion, nor right, nor memorial, in Jerusalem" (2:20).

**Building under threat.** "So built we the wall; and all the wall was joined together unto the half thereof: for the people had a mind to work" (4:6). "We made our prayer unto our God, and set a watch against them day and night" (4:9). He posts families "with their swords, their spears, and their bows" behind the wall (4:13) and tells them "Be not ye afraid of them: remember the LORD, which is great and terrible, and fight for your brethren, your sons, and your daughters, your wives, and your houses" (4:14). Builders work with a sword at their side, and the trumpeter stays beside him (4:18–19); "neither I, nor my brethren, nor my servants, nor the men of the guard which followed me, none of us put off our clothes, saving that every one put them off for washing" (4:23).

**Justice for the poor.** He is "very angry" at the cry of Jews in debt bondage (5:6), rebukes the nobles: "Ye exact usury, every one of his brother" and sets "a great assembly against them" (5:7); "It is not good that ye do: ought ye not to walk in the fear of our God?" (5:9). He and his household do not claim the governor's food allowance, though a hundred and fifty Jews and rulers eat at his table, "because the bondage was heavy upon this people" (5:14–18).

**Plots.** When Sanballat, Tobiah and Geshem hear the wall is done, "Sanballat and Geshem sent unto me, saying, Come, let us meet together in some one of the villages in the plain of Ono. But they thought to do me mischief" (6:1–2). "I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down: why should the work cease, whilst I leave it, and come down to you?" (6:3). To a false report of rebellion: "There are no such things done as thou sayest, but thou feignest them out of thine own heart… Now therefore, O God, strengthen my hands" (6:8–9). When Shemaiah urges him to hide in the temple: "Should such a man as I flee? and who is there, that, being as I am, would go into the temple to save his life? I will not go in" (6:11). "So the wall was finished in the twenty and fifth day of the month Elul, in fifty and two days" (6:15), and the enemies "were much cast down in their own eyes: for they perceived that this work was wrought of our God" (6:16).

**Order in the city.** He sets up doors and appoints porters, singers and Levites, and gives "my brother Hanani, and Hananiah the ruler of the palace, charge over Jerusalem: for he was a faithful man, and feared God above many" (7:1–2), ordering "Let not the gates of Jerusalem be opened until the sun be hot" (7:3). God "put into mine heart to gather together the nobles, and the rulers, and the people, that they might be reckoned by genealogy" (7:5).

**Law and covenant.** At the reading of the law he and Ezra tell the people, who weep: "This day is holy unto the LORD your God; mourn not, nor weep… Go your way, eat the fat, and drink the sweet, and send portions unto them for whom nothing is prepared… the joy of the LORD is your strength" (8:9–10). He is the first to seal the covenant: "Now those that sealed were, Nehemiah, the Tirshatha, the son of Hachaliah" (10:1), whose terms include no intermarriage, no Sabbath trading and a third of a shekel yearly for the temple (10:30–32).

**Dedication.** For the dedication of the wall he brings the princes of Judah up on it and appoints two great companies of thanksgiving; he follows the second company, with "the half of the people upon the wall" (12:31, 12:38, 12:40).

**Second term.** "In the two and thirtieth year of Artaxerxes king of Babylon came I unto the king, and after certain days obtained I leave of the king" (13:6). Back in Jerusalem he finds that Eliashib the priest gave Tobiah a chamber in the temple courts (13:7). "It grieved me sore: therefore I cast forth all the household stuff of Tobiah out of the chamber. Then I commanded, and they cleansed the chambers" (13:8–9). He acts against Sabbath-breaking traders, telling them "Why lodge ye about the wall? if ye do so again, I will lay hands on you" (13:21), and closes with two prayers: "Remember me, O my God, concerning this, and wipe not out my good deeds" (13:14) and "Remember me, O my God, for good" (13:31).

## Relationships

- **Hacaliah**: father. **Hanani**: brother, who brings the report and later governs Jerusalem (1:2, 7:2). He has no recorded wife or children.
- **Artaxerxes**: the king he served. **Ezra the scribe**: appears with him at the reading of the law (8:9, 12:36).
- **Sanballat, Tobiah, Geshem**: his opponents. **Eliashib** (priest), **Shemaiah**: those who cause him trouble.
- **[Moses](moses.md)** (2 shared verses, Nehemiah 1:7–8, where his prayer recalls the commandments given to "thy servant Moses").

## Reading the data

- Nehemiah 8:1–2 (the people ask Ezra to bring the law) are correctly not linked to him. Nehemiah 9:38, 10:31–32, 12:37 and 12:47 (0.53–0.67) are covenant and procession verses where "I" or "we" includes him.
- Nehemiah 3 (who built which section of the wall) is not linked because the verses list other builders.
- **Namesake error removed:** Nehemiah 7:7 (0.77) names the Nehemiah who returned with Zerubbabel, a different man, and has been removed from the database. The other lookalikes (Ezra 2:2 at 0.12, Nehemiah 3:16 "Nehemiah the son of Azbuk" at 0.16) were correctly kept out.
