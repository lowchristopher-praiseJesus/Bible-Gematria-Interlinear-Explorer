## Who Judas is

Judas Iscariot ("Judas of Kerioth") is always named in the lists of the twelve with the note that he "also betrayed him". He is in 102 of the linked verses: Matthew 27, Mark 26, Luke 18, John 24, Acts 6, and one in the Psalms. About half of these are the betrayal story (Matthew 26–27, Mark 14, Luke 22, John 12–18) and the other half are verses about "the twelve" in which he is one of the group. Judas the brother of James (Luke 6:16, Acts 1:13) and "Judas, not Iscariot" (John 14:22, 0.09) are different men and are kept out.

## What the verses tell us

**Chosen.** He appears last in every list of the apostles: "Simon the Canaanite, and Judas Iscariot, who also betrayed him" (Matthew 10:4); "Judas Iscariot, which also betrayed him" (Mark 3:19); "Judas the brother of James, and Judas Iscariot, which also was the traitor" (Luke 6:16). He is sent out with the others "to cast them out, and to heal all manner of sickness" (Matthew 10:1, 10:5; Mark 6:7; Luke 9:1–2) and is among the twelve at the miracles and teaching (Mark 6:42, 9:35, 10:32, Luke 18:31). John records Jesus' warning: "Have not I chosen you twelve, and one of you is a devil? He spake of Judas Iscariot the son of Simon: for he it was that should betray him, being one of the twelve" (6:70–71), and "Jesus knew from the beginning who they were that believed not, and who should betray him" (6:64).

**The thief.** At Bethany, when Mary anoints Jesus, "Then saith one of his disciples, Judas Iscariot, Simon's son, which should betray him, Why was not this ointment sold for three hundred pence, and given to the poor? This he said, not that he cared for the poor; but because he was a thief, and had the bag, and bare what was put therein" (John 12:4–6).

**The bargain.** "Then one of the twelve, called Judas Iscariot, went unto the chief priests, and said unto them, What will ye give me, and I will deliver him unto you? And they covenanted with him for thirty pieces of silver" (Matthew 26:14–15). Mark: "when they heard it, they were glad, and promised to give him money. And he sought how he might conveniently betray him" (14:10–11). Luke: "Then entered Satan into Judas surnamed Iscariot, being of the number of the twelve. And he went his way, and communed with the chief priests and captains, how he might betray him unto them… and sought opportunity to betray him unto them in the absence of the multitude" (22:3–6).

**The supper.** "The devil having now put into the heart of Judas Iscariot, Simon's son, to betray him" (John 13:2). At the table "Then Judas, which betrayed him, answered and said, Master, is it I? He said unto him, Thou hast said" (Matthew 26:25). Jesus says "He it is, to whom I shall give a sop, when I have dipped it. And when he had dipped the sop, he gave it to Judas Iscariot, the son of Simon. And after the sop Satan entered into him. Then said Jesus unto him, That thou doest, do quickly" (John 13:26–27). The others thought Jesus had told him to buy for the feast or give to the poor (13:29). "He then having received the sop went immediately out: and it was night" (13:30). In his prayer Jesus says "none of them is lost, but the son of perdition; that the scripture might be fulfilled" (17:12).

**The arrest.** "Judas also, which betrayed him, knew the place: for Jesus ofttimes resorted thither with his disciples" (John 18:2). "Judas then, having received a band of men and officers from the chief priests and Pharisees, cometh thither with lanterns and torches and weapons" (18:3; Matthew 26:47: "Judas, one of the twelve, came, and with him a great multitude with swords and staves"). The signal: "Whomsoever I shall kiss, that same is he: hold him fast" (Matthew 26:48; Mark 14:44). "And as soon as he was come, he goeth straightway to him, and saith, Master, master; and kissed him" (Mark 14:45). Jesus: "Judas, betrayest thou the Son of man with a kiss?" (Luke 22:48). John adds that "Judas also, which betrayed him, stood with them" when Jesus said "I am he" (18:5).

**Remorse and death.** "Then Judas, which had betrayed him, when he saw that he was condemned, repented himself, and brought again the thirty pieces of silver to the chief priests and elders, saying, I have sinned in that I have betrayed the innocent blood. And they said, What is that to us? see thou to that. And he cast down the pieces of silver in the temple, and departed, and went and hanged himself" (Matthew 27:3–5). The priests use the money for the potter's field, "The field of blood" (27:8), and Matthew reads this as "that which was spoken by Jeremy the prophet" (27:9–10; see [Jeremiah](jeremiah.md)).

**In Acts.** Peter says the scripture about "Judas, which was guide to them that took Jesus" had to be fulfilled: "he was numbered with us, and had obtained part of this ministry. Now this man purchased a field with the reward of iniquity; and falling headlong, he burst asunder in the midst, and all his bowels gushed out. And it was known unto all the dwellers at Jerusalem; insomuch as that field is called in their proper tongue, Aceldama, that is to say, The field of blood" (Acts 1:16–19). He quotes Psalm 69 and 109: "Let his habitation be desolate… and his bishoprick let another take" (1:20; Psalm 109:8, "Let his days be few; and let another take his office"). Matthias replaces him, "that he may take part of this ministry and apostleship, from which Judas by transgression fell, that he might go to his own place" (1:25). The two accounts of his death, in Matthew and Acts, differ in detail (hanging; falling and bursting open).

## Relationships

- **[Jesus](jesus.md)**: the master he betrays (82 shared verses). **Simon**: his father (John 6:71, 12:4, 13:2, 13:26).
- **[Peter](peter.md)** (25 shared verses) and **[John the apostle](john_apostle.md)** (31) are fellow apostles in the lists, the last supper and Acts 1. **[Thomas](thomas.md)** shares 3 verses.
- **The chief priests and elders** (and **Caiaphas** by implication) pay him; **Matthias** takes his place; **the apostle Judas (Thaddaeus/Lebbaeus)**, brother of James, is a different Judas.
- **[Jeremiah](jeremiah.md)** shares Matthew 27:9 (the "Jeremy" prophecy).

## Reading the data

- **Group verses.** About 40 links are 0.5–0.7 verses about "the twelve" where Judas is among them but is not named (Matthew 10:1, 17:6, Mark 3:14, 4:10, 6:7–8, 6:42, 9:10, 9:35, 10:32, 11:11, Luke 8:1, 9:2, 9:10, 9:12, 18:31, John 6:67, 16:31). Treat them as "the twelve", not as evidence about Judas himself.
- **Psalm 109:8** (0.58) is linked because Acts 1:20 applies it to him.
- **Namesake handling.** The link set cleanly excludes "Judas, not Iscariot" (John 14:22) and Judas the brother of James, except for the mention of Judas Iscariot in Luke 6:16.
