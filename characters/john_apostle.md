## Who John is

This profile is **John son of Zebedee, the apostle**, not [John the Baptist](john_baptist.md). John appears by name in the Synoptic Gospels and Acts (about 136 verses: Matthew 29, Mark 42, Luke 30, Acts 35) and is also, by long tradition, the unnamed "disciple whom Jesus loved" (John 13:23, 19:26, 20:2, 21:7, 21:20–24) and the "I" of 1 John (27 links), 2 John (6), 3 John (11) and Revelation (72). Those last groups are *not stated by the texts as John's own name* except in Revelation; they are the classifier's reading, supported by the traditional attribution (see "Reading the data").

## What the verses tell us

**Called.** "Going on from thence, he saw other two brethren, James the son of Zebedee, and John his brother, in a ship with Zebedee their father, mending their nets; and he called them" (Matthew 4:21; Mark 1:19). Luke says James and John "were partners with Simon" (5:10). In every list of the twelve he is paired with James (Matthew 10:2; Luke 6:14). "James the son of Zebedee, and John the brother of James; and he surnamed them Boanerges, which is, The sons of thunder" (Mark 3:17).

**Inner circle.** With Peter and James he is one of the three taken to the raising of Jairus' daughter (Luke 8:51), to the Transfiguration ("after six days Jesus taketh Peter, James, and John his brother, and bringeth them up into an high mountain apart", Matthew 17:1; Mark 9:2; Luke 9:28), to the Mount of Olives with Andrew (Mark 13:3) and to Gethsemane: "he took with him Peter and the two sons of Zebedee, and began to be sorrowful and very heavy" (Matthew 26:37; Mark 14:33). Jesus sends "Peter and John" to prepare the passover (Luke 22:8).

**The sons of thunder.** John says "Master, we saw one casting out devils in thy name, and he followeth not us: and we forbad him" (Mark 9:38; Luke 9:49), and Jesus replies "Forbid him not: for he that is not against us is for us" (Luke 9:50). When a Samaritan village refuses Jesus, "his disciples James and John saw this, they said, Lord, wilt thou that we command fire to come down from heaven, and consume them, even as Elias did?" (9:54). Their request: "Master, we would that thou shouldest do for us whatsoever we shall desire… Grant unto us that we may sit, one on thy right hand, and the other on thy left hand, in thy glory" (Mark 10:35–37); Matthew has "the mother of Zebedee's children with her sons, worshipping him, and desiring a certain thing of him" (20:20). Jesus: "Ye know not what ye ask: can ye drink of the cup that I drink of? and be baptized with the baptism that I am baptized with?" — "We can" (Mark 10:38–39; Matthew 20:22).

**In John's Gospel.** The "disciple whom Jesus loved" is at the last supper "leaning on Jesus' bosom" (John 13:23), asks "Lord, who is it?" when Peter beckons (13:25), follows Jesus into the high priest's palace and brings Peter in (18:15–16), stands by the cross where Jesus says to his mother "Woman, behold thy son!" and to the disciple "Behold thy mother! And from that hour that disciple took her unto his own home" (19:26–27). On Easter morning Mary Magdalene runs "to Simon Peter, and to the other disciple, whom Jesus loved" (20:2); "that other disciple, which came first to the sepulchre… saw, and believed" (20:8). When the risen Jesus appears at the Sea of Galilee, "that disciple whom Jesus loved saith unto Peter, It is the Lord" (21:7). Peter asks "Lord, and what shall this man do?" and Jesus says "If I will that he tarry till I come, what is that to thee?", which gave rise to the saying "that that disciple should not die" (21:20–23). The Gospel closes: "This is the disciple which testifieth of these things, and wrote these things: and we know that his testimony is true" (21:24). The two disciples who follow Jesus after the Baptist's testimony (John 1:37–40) are Andrew and an unnamed one, often taken as John.

**In Acts.** In the upper room "abode both Peter, and James, and John, and Andrew, Philip, and Thomas, Bartholomew, and Matthew, James the son of Alphaeus, and Simon Zelotes, and Judas the brother of James" (Acts 1:13). John accompanies Peter to the temple: "Peter and John went up together into the temple at the hour of prayer" (3:1), where Peter says "Look on us" (3:4). Before the council: "Whether it be right in the sight of God to hearken unto you more than unto God, judge ye" (4:19), and the rulers note "the boldness of Peter and John" (4:13). "When the apostles which were at Jerusalem heard that Samaria had received the word of God, they sent unto them Peter and John" (8:14–15). Herod "killed James the brother of John with the sword" (12:2, not linked, 0.46). Paul names him with Peter and James as one of the "pillars" who gave him "the right hands of fellowship" (Galatians 2:9).

**The letters.** 1 John begins "That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked upon, and our hands have handled, of the Word of life" (1:1), and the "we" and "I write" verses throughout are linked to him, e.g. "My little children, let us not love in word, neither in tongue; but in deed and in truth" (3:18) and "These things have I written unto you that believe on the name of the Son of God; that ye may know that ye have eternal life" (5:13). 2 John is "The elder unto the elect lady and her children, whom I love in the truth" (1:1) and 3 John "The elder unto the wellbeloved Gaius" (1:1), with the complaint "I wrote unto the church: but Diotrephes, who loveth to have the preeminence among them, receiveth us not" (1:9). The letters name their author only as "the elder".

**Revelation.** "The Revelation of Jesus Christ… he sent and signified it by his angel unto his servant John" (Revelation 1:1); "I John, who also am your brother, and companion in tribulation, and in the kingdom and patience of Jesus Christ, was in the isle that is called Patmos, for the word of God, and for the testimony of Jesus Christ" (1:9). He hears "a great voice, as of a trumpet" saying "What thou seest, write in a book, and send it unto the seven churches" (1:10–11). The book's visions ("a door was opened in heaven", 4:1; "I wept much, because no man was found worthy to open and to read the book", 5:4; "I saw when the Lamb opened one of the seals", 6:1; "there was given me a reed like unto a rod", 11:1) are the first-person voice. "I John saw the holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down from God out of heaven" (21:2) and "I John saw these things, and heard them… I fell down to worship before the feet of the angel which shewed me these things" (22:8).

## Reading the data

- **Three different kinds of link.** (1) *Synoptics and Acts* (136 links, of which only 35 name John in TIPNR): where John is named, or is one of the pair "James and John" or "Peter and John", the classifier is sure. (2) *Beloved disciple*: the 47 links in John's Gospel (chiefly chapters 13, 18–21), where "the disciple whom Jesus loved" is read as John. (3) *Epistles and Revelation* (116 verses): the author's first-person "I", including many verses of 0.5–0.9 with no name in the verse. Only Revelation 1:1, 1:4, 1:9, 21:2 and 22:8 give the name "John", and the letters say "the elder".
- Because of (3), John has a much lower share of very high scores (59 at ≥0.9 out of 300) than Peter or Matthew's disciples.
- Group verses that include him among "the eleven" or "the twelve" are linked at 0.5–0.6 (Matthew 16:20, 28:16, Mark 16:14, Luke 24:48, John 6:67, 16:29).
- Acts 12:2 ("James the brother of John") was missed.
