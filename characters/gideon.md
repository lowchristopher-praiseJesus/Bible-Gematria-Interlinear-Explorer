## Who Gideon is

Gideon (also called Jerubbaal, "let Baal plead") is a judge from the tribe of Manasseh. Judges 6–8 tell his story, and 88 of his 92 links are there. He is remembered in 1 Samuel 12:11 and Hebrews 11:32 as one of Israel's deliverers.

## What the verses tell us

**The call.** "There came an angel of the LORD, and sat under an oak which was in Ophrah… and his son Gideon threshed wheat by the winepress, to hide it from the Midianites" (Judges 6:11). The angel greets him "The LORD is with thee, thou mighty man of valour" (6:12). Gideon's reply is a complaint: "if the LORD be with us, why then is all this befallen us? and where be all his miracles…?" (6:13). The LORD: "Go in this thy might, and thou shalt save Israel from the hand of the Midianites: have not I sent thee?" (6:14). He objects "my family is poor in Manasseh, and I am the least in my father's house" (6:15); God answers "Surely I will be with thee" (6:16).

**Sign and altar.** He asks for a sign, prepares a kid and unleavened cakes, and the angel has him lay them on a rock and pour out the broth (6:17–20). When he realises "I have seen an angel of the LORD face to face" he is afraid, but the LORD says "Peace be unto thee; fear not: thou shalt not die" (6:22–23). He builds an altar named Jehovahshalom (6:24).

**Baal's altar.** That night God tells him to throw down his father's altar of Baal and cut down the grove, using his father's second bullock (6:25). He takes ten servants and does it by night "because he feared his father's household, and the men of the city" (6:27). The town asks "Who hath done this thing?" and names "Gideon the son of Joash" (6:29); Judges 6:32 (not in this sample) gives him the name Jerubbaal for it.

**Gathering and the fleece.** "The Spirit of the LORD came upon Gideon, and he blew a trumpet" and Abiezer, Manasseh, Asher, Zebulun and Naphtali come (6:34–35). He still asks for proof: a wool fleece wet with dew while the ground is dry, then the reverse (6:36–40). "I will speak but this once" (6:39).

**300 men.** God says the army of 32,000 is "too many for me to give the Midianites into their hands, lest Israel vaunt themselves against me" (7:2). 22,000 leave when he says "Whosoever is fearful and afraid, let him return" (7:3). Ten thousand are brought to the water and the LORD picks "every one that lappeth of the water with his tongue, as a dog lappeth" (7:4–5): "By the three hundred men that lapped will I save you" (7:7). Told to go down to the camp "if thou fear", with Phurah his servant (7:10–11), he overhears a Midianite's dream of a loaf of barley bread that flattened a tent, and the answer "This is nothing else save the sword of Gideon the son of Joash… for into his hand hath God delivered Midian" (7:13–14). He worships and gives the command: "Arise; for the LORD hath delivered into your hand the host of Midian" (7:15).

**The night attack.** He divides the 300 into three companies, each man with a trumpet, "empty pitchers, and lamps within the pitchers" (7:16). At the middle watch they blow the trumpets, break the pitchers and cry "The sword of the LORD, and of Gideon" (7:18–20). The Midianites flee, and "the LORD set every man's sword against his fellow" (7:22). Ephraim is called to cut off the fords and captures the princes Oreb and Zeeb (7:24–25). Ephraim then "did chide with him sharply", and he answers "What have I done now in comparison of you? Is not the gleaning of the grapes of Ephraim better than the vintage of Abiezer?" (8:1–3).

**Pursuit and reprisals.** "Faint, yet pursuing", he asks Succoth and Penuel for bread; both refuse, and he vows to tear the flesh of the Succoth elders with thorns and briers (8:4–7). He catches Zebah and Zalmunna, the two kings of Midian (8:12), returns and punishes Succoth with thorns and briers and destroys the tower of Penuel and kills its men (8:16–17). He kills the two kings (8:21).

**Refuses a crown, makes an ephod.** The men of Israel say "Rule thou over us, both thou, and thy son, and thy son's son also" (8:22). He says "I will not rule over you, neither shall my son rule over you: the LORD shall rule over you" (8:23). Then he asks for the gold earrings of their spoil (8:24), and "made an ephod thereof, and put it in his city, even in Ophrah: and all Israel went thither a whoring after it: which thing became a snare unto Gideon, and to his house" (8:27).

**Family and death.** "Gideon had threescore and ten sons of his body begotten: for he had many wives" (8:30), and a concubine in Shechem bore him Abimelech (8:31). He "died in a good old age" and was buried in Ophrah (8:32). In Judges 9 Abimelech kills his seventy brothers on one stone (9:18) and the youngest, Jotham, denounces the Shechemites' treatment of "Jerubbaal and his house" (9:16–19).

**Later references.** 1 Samuel 12:11: "the LORD sent Jerubbaal, and Bedan, and Jephthah, and Samuel". Hebrews 11:32–34: "the time would fail me to tell of Gedeon, and of Barak, and of Samson, and of Jephthae; of David also, and Samuel… out of weakness were made strong, waxed valiant in fight".

## Relationships

- **Joash the Abiezrite**: his father, who owned the altar of Baal (6:11, 6:25). Gideon's own clan is Abiezer, in Manasseh.
- **Abimelech** (son by the Shechem concubine), **Jotham** (youngest son) and **Jether** (eldest, Judges 8:20): three of his children by name; seventy sons in all.
- **Phurah**: his servant. **Zebah and Zalmunna**: the Midianite kings; **Oreb and Zeeb**: the Midianite princes.
- **[Samson](samson.md)** (2 shared verses) and **[Samuel](samuel.md)** (3) are named alongside him in Hebrews 11:32 and 1 Samuel 12:11, as is **[David](david.md)**.

## Reading the data

The Gideon links are dense and reliable. Judges 9 (Abimelech's rise) is the weak zone: 9:16–19 (0.67–0.69) speak *of* "Jerubbaal and his house", and the other Jerubbaal verses (9:1, 9:2, 9:5, 9:24, 9:28, 9:57 and 2 Samuel 11:21, where the KJV calls him "Jerubbesheth") scored below the threshold.
