## Who Moses is

Moses is the second most-linked Old Testament character in the index (1,384 verses), and Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers and Deuteronomy are built around him: 1,238 of his links are in those four books. Leviticus and much of Numbers are mostly "the LORD spake unto Moses" followed by law, which is why he appears in so many verses. The New Testament treats him as the lawgiver, the prophet Jesus is compared to, and a witness at the Transfiguration.

## What the verses tell us

**Birth, adoption, flight.** A Levite couple's son is hidden three months, then placed in "an ark of bulrushes" by the river; his sister watches "afar off" (Exodus 2:1–4). Pharaoh's daughter finds the baby, and his sister offers to fetch "a nurse of the Hebrew women", so his own mother nurses him (2:5–8). "She called his name Moses: and she said, Because I drew him out of the water" (2:10). Acts 7:22 adds that he "was learned in all the wisdom of the Egyptians, and was mighty in words and in deeds". Grown, he sees an Egyptian beating a Hebrew and "slew the Egyptian, and hid him in the sand" (2:11–12). A Hebrew asks "Who made thee a prince and a judge over us?" (2:14); Pharaoh seeks to kill him and Moses flees to Midian (2:15). Hebrews 11:24–25 reads the earlier choice: he "refused to be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter; choosing rather to suffer affliction with the people of God, than to enjoy the pleasures of sin for a season".

**The bush and the call.** He is keeping the flock of Jethro, "priest of Midian", at Horeb (3:1) when a bush burns "and the bush was not consumed" (3:2). God says "I will send thee unto Pharaoh, that thou mayest bring forth my people" (3:10). Moses: "Who am I…?" (3:11). Asked what to say when Israel asks the name of the God who sent him, God answers "I AM THAT I AM… I AM hath sent me unto you" (3:13–14). Moses objects "I am not eloquent… I am slow of speech" (4:10) and asks God to send someone else (4:13). The LORD's anger is kindled, and he says Aaron, "the Levite… can speak well… he shall be thy spokesman… and thou shalt be to him instead of God" (4:14–16). Later: "See, I have made thee a god to Pharaoh: and Aaron thy brother shall be thy prophet" (7:1).

**Pharaoh and the plagues.** Moses and Aaron go to Pharaoh with "Let my people go, that they may hold a feast unto me in the wilderness" (5:1). Exodus 7–11 repeats the pattern of the LORD sending Moses "unto Pharaoh" with a demand and a plague, each time with the refrain that God "hardened Pharaoh's heart" (10:1, 11:10).

**Red Sea.** At the sea Moses tells the people "Fear ye not, stand still, and see the salvation of the LORD" (14:13); he stretches his hand and the sea becomes "dry land, and the waters were divided" (14:21–22). Then "sang Moses and the children of Israel this song unto the LORD" (15:1), the song that Revelation 15:3 later calls "the song of Moses the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb".

**Wilderness leader.** He is the person the people bring their grievances to, from thirst ("the people did chide with Moses… Why chide ye with me? wherefore do ye tempt the LORD?", 17:2) to fire (Numbers 11:2, "when Moses prayed unto the LORD, the fire was quenched") to serpents (21:7). At the battle with Amalek he holds up his hand: "when Moses held up his hand, that Israel prevailed", and Aaron and Hur prop up his arms until sunset (17:11–12). His father-in-law Jethro brings Moses' wife and two sons to him (18:6).

**Sinai.** Moses goes up to God (19:3); God speaks "all these words" and the Ten Commandments begin "I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt" (20:1–2). Moses tells the fearful people "Fear not: for God is come to prove you" (20:20). He receives the tabernacle pattern ("as it was shewed thee in the mount", 27:8) and "two tables of testimony, tables of stone, written with the finger of God" (31:18); he calls the craftsmen Bezaleel and Aholiab (36:2), inspects the finished work and blesses the workers (39:43).

**The golden calf.** God tells him "thy people, which thou broughtest out of the land of Egypt, have corrupted themselves" (32:7). Moses comes down, "Moses' anger waxed hot", smashes the tablets, burns the calf, grinds it to powder and makes Israel drink it (32:19–20). He pleads: "if thou wilt forgive their sin—; and if not, blot me, I pray thee, out of thy book" (32:32). New tablets are cut (34:1). "The LORD spake unto Moses face to face, as a man speaketh unto his friend" (33:11), and after the mountain "Moses wist not that the skin of his face shone" (34:29).

**Priesthood and law.** In Leviticus 8–10 Moses is the one who consecrates Aaron and his sons and speaks for God when Nadab and Abihu die: "This is it that the LORD spake, saying, I will be sanctified in them that come nigh me" (Leviticus 10:3). Numbers 12:3 says "the man Moses was very meek, above all the men which were upon the face of the earth", and God defends him against Miriam and Aaron: "My servant Moses is not so, who is faithful in all mine house. With him will I speak mouth to mouth" (12:7–8). Numbers 16 is Korah's rebellion: "Ye take too much upon you" (16:3). Numbers 13:16 records that Moses "called Oshea the son of Nun Jehoshua" ([Joshua](joshua.md)).

**The rock and the ban.** At Meribah God tells him to speak to the rock; instead "he said unto them, Hear now, ye rebels; must we fetch you water out of this rock?" and struck it twice (Numbers 20:8–11). The LORD: "Because ye believed me not, to sanctify me… therefore ye shall not bring this congregation into the land" (20:12). In Deuteronomy 3:25 he begs to see it: "I pray thee, let me go over".

**Deuteronomy.** The whole book is presented as speeches to Israel "on this side Jordan" (1:1): "I set before you this day a blessing and a curse" (11:26), "I have set before you life and death… therefore choose life" (30:19). Deuteronomy 18:15 promises "a Prophet from the midst of thee, of thy brethren, like unto me". Chapter 33 is "the blessing, wherewith Moses the man of God blessed the children of Israel before his death" (33:1).

**Death.** From Nebo the LORD shows him the land: "I have caused thee to see it with thine eyes, but thou shalt not go over thither" (34:4). "So Moses the servant of the LORD died there in the land of Moab… And he buried him in a valley… but no man knoweth of his sepulchre unto this day. And Moses was an hundred and twenty years old when he died: his eye was not dim, nor his natural force abated" (34:5–7). Israel weeps thirty days (34:8). "There arose not a prophet since in Israel like unto Moses, whom the LORD knew face to face" (34:10). Joshua 1:13 begins the next book with "the word which Moses the servant of the LORD commanded you". Jude 9 tells of Michael and the devil disputing "about the body of Moses".

**Later Old Testament.** Psalm 106:23: God "would destroy them, had not Moses his chosen stood before him in the breach". Psalm 77:20: "Thou leddest thy people like a flock by the hand of Moses and Aaron". Micah 6:4: "I sent before thee Moses, Aaron, and Miriam."

**New Testament.**
- *Matthew 17:3, Mark 9:4, Luke 9:30:* at the Transfiguration "there appeared unto them Moses and Elias talking with him" (Jesus).
- *John 5:45–47:* "there is one that accuseth you, even Moses… had ye believed Moses, ye would have believed me: for he wrote of me."
- *Acts 7:20–44:* Stephen's long retelling of his life, including "This Moses whom they refused, saying, Who made thee a ruler and a judge? the same did God send to be a ruler and a deliverer" (7:35).
- *Romans 10:5, 2 Corinthians 3:7:* Moses as the writer of the law, and the fading glory on his face.
- *Hebrews 3:5, 8:5, 9:19, 11:23–28:* "Moses verily was faithful in all his house, as a servant, for a testimony of those things which were to be spoken after"; the tabernacle pattern; his hidden birth; and his choices.

## Relationships

- **[Aaron](aaron.md)**: older brother, spokesman and first high priest; 227 shared verses. **[Miriam](miriam.md)**: sister, who watches over him at the river (Exodus 2:4) and later disputes with him (Numbers 12).
- **Amram and Jochebed**: parents, of the tribe of Levi (Exodus 6:18–20).
- **Zipporah**: wife, from Midian; **Gershom** and **Eliezer**: sons. **Jethro**: father-in-law and priest of Midian (Exodus 3:1, 18:6); Numbers 10:29 names the father-in-law as Raguel, with Hobab as his son.
- **[Joshua](joshua.md)**: assistant and successor (46 shared verses; Exodus 33:11, Numbers 13:16).
- **Pharaoh** and **Pharaoh's daughter**; Korah; Nadab and Abihu.
- **[Jesus](jesus.md)**: 29 shared verses, in the comparison of the law and grace, the Transfiguration and the "he wrote of me" claim. **[Elijah](elijah.md)** appears with him at the Transfiguration (7 shared verses).
- **[Abraham](abraham.md)**, **[Isaac](isaac.md)** and **[Jacob](jacob.md)**: the covenant fathers God keeps citing to him (Exodus 3:15, 32:13, 33:1).

## Reading the data

- More than a third of the Moses links (Leviticus 126, Numbers 366) are "the LORD spake unto Moses" openings and the laws that follow; treat them as *God addressing Moses*, not narrative about him. Many are near-certain (0.9+), so they are reliable but repetitive.
- Around 80 named verses (mostly "commanded by Moses" in Joshua, Kings and Chronicles, and "law of Moses" in Acts and the Epistles) sit just under the threshold: e.g. Joshua 1:5, Jeremiah 15:1, John 9:28, Luke 16:29 and Acts 6:14.
- TIPNR lists "Manasseh" (KJV) as a name form of Moses in Judges 18:30, the well-known scribal quirk.
