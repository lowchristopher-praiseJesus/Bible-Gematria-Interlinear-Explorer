## Who Elijah is

Elijah (Elias in the KJV New Testament) appears in 1 Kings 17–2 Kings 2 (about 110 links, plus some Elisha-related passages in 2 Kings), and then in later passages where he is the model of a prophet or the figure who must "come first": Malachi 4, the Gospels, Romans 11 and James 5. He shares 30 verses with [Elisha](elisha.md), 19 with [Jesus](jesus.md) and 7 with [John the Baptist](john_baptist.md).

## What the verses tell us

**The drought.** "Elijah the Tishbite, who was of the inhabitants of Gilead, said unto Ahab, As the LORD God of Israel liveth, before whom I stand, there shall not be dew nor rain these years, but according to my word" (1 Kings 17:1). He is told to hide "by the brook Cherith, that is before Jordan", where "I have commanded the ravens to feed thee" (17:3–4). "The ravens brought him bread and flesh in the morning, and bread and flesh in the evening; and he drank of the brook" (17:6).

**The widow of Zarephath.** God sends him to Zarephath "which belongeth to Zidon", where "I have commanded a widow woman there to sustain thee" (17:9). She is gathering two sticks and says she has only "an handful of meal in a barrel, and a little oil in a cruse… that we may eat it, and die" (17:12). Elijah: "Fear not; go and do as thou hast said: but make me thereof a little cake first" (17:13); the barrel and cruse do not fail (17:14–16). When her son dies she asks "art thou come unto me to call my sin to remembrance, and to slay my son?" (17:18). He "stretched himself upon the child three times, and cried unto the LORD… let this child's soul come into him again" (17:21), the child revives, and Elijah says "See, thy son liveth" (17:22–23). Jesus recalls this in Luke 4:25–26: many widows were in Israel "in the days of Elias… but unto none of them was Elias sent, save unto Sarepta, a city of Sidon, unto a woman that was a widow."

**Carmel.** "In the third year" God says "Go, shew thyself unto Ahab; and I will send rain" (18:1). Ahab's steward Obadiah, who hid a hundred prophets in caves from Jezebel (18:4, 18:13), meets him and says "Art thou that my lord Elijah?" (18:7). Elijah gathers the people and the prophets of Baal on the mountain: "How long halt ye between two opinions? if the LORD be God, follow him: but if Baal, then follow him. And the people answered him not a word" (18:21). At the evening sacrifice he prays "LORD God of Abraham, Isaac, and of Israel, let it be known this day that thou art God in Israel, and that I am thy servant" (18:36–37). "The fire of the LORD fell, and consumed the burnt sacrifice, and the wood, and the stones, and the dust, and licked up the water that was in the trench" (18:38). "The LORD, he is the God" (18:39). Elijah has the prophets of Baal killed at the brook Kishon (18:40). Then he climbs Carmel, puts "his face between his knees", and sends his servant seven times to look at the sea until "there ariseth a little cloud out of the sea, like a man's hand" (18:42–44). "The heaven was black with clouds and wind, and there was a great rain" and Elijah runs ahead of Ahab's chariot to Jezreel (18:45–46).

**Flight and Horeb.** Jezebel sends "So let the gods do to me, and more also, if I make not thy life as the life of one of them by to morrow about this time" (19:2). He goes a day's journey into the wilderness and sits under a juniper tree: "It is enough; now, O LORD, take away my life; for I am not better than my fathers" (19:4). Strengthened, "went in the strength of that meat forty days and forty nights unto Horeb the mount of God" (19:8). God asks twice "What doest thou here, Elijah?" (19:9, 19:13). Elijah: "I have been very jealous for the LORD God of hosts… I, even I only, am left; and they seek my life" (19:14). God passes by: not in the wind, the earthquake or the fire, but "a still small voice" (19:11–12). He is sent to anoint Hazael as king of Syria, Jehu as king of Israel and Elisha "to be prophet in thy room" (19:15–16). Romans 11:2–4 uses the scene: "I am left alone… But what saith the answer of God unto him? I have reserved to myself seven thousand men, who have not bowed the knee to the image of Baal."

**Naboth's vineyard.** After Ahab takes Naboth's vineyard, "the word of the LORD came to Elijah the Tishbite" (21:17). Ahab: "Hast thou found me, O mine enemy?" Elijah: "I have found thee: because thou hast sold thyself to work evil in the sight of the LORD" (21:20). The judgement: "In the place where dogs licked the blood of Naboth shall dogs lick thy blood, even thine" (21:19), and on Jezebel "The dogs shall eat Jezebel by the wall of Jezreel" (21:23; see [Jezebel](jezebel.md)). Ahab humbles himself and the word comes again (21:28). Jehu later remembers "the word of the LORD, which he spake by his servant Elijah" (2 Kings 9:36; 10:10).

**Fire on the captains.** When King Ahaziah sends for him, "the angel of the LORD said to Elijah the Tishbite, Arise, go up to meet the messengers of the king of Samaria, and say unto them, Is it not because there is not a God in Israel, that ye go to enquire of Baalzebub the god of Ekron?" (2 Kings 1:3). To the captain of fifty: "If I be a man of God, then let fire come down from heaven, and consume thee and thy fifty. And there came down fire from heaven" (1:10, 1:12).

**Taken up.** "when the LORD would take up Elijah into heaven by a whirlwind, that Elijah went with Elisha from Gilgal" (2:1). At Bethel, Jericho and the Jordan he says "Tarry here" and Elisha answers "As the LORD liveth, and as thy soul liveth, I will not leave thee" (2:2, 2:4, 2:6). He strikes the river with his mantle and it divides (2:8). "Ask what I shall do for thee, before I be taken away" — "let a double portion of thy spirit be upon me" — "Thou hast asked a hard thing" (2:9–10). Then "there appeared a chariot of fire, and horses of fire, and parted them both asunder; and Elijah went up by a whirlwind into heaven" (2:11); Elisha cries "My father, my father, the chariot of Israel, and the horsemen thereof" (2:12). 2 Chronicles 21:12 records a written message from Elijah to King Jehoram.

**Promised return.** Malachi 4:5–6: "Behold, I will send you Elijah the prophet before the coming of the great and dreadful day of the LORD: And he shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children."

**In the Gospels.**
- Jesus says of [John the Baptist](john_baptist.md): "And if ye will receive it, this is Elias, which was for to come" (Matthew 11:14), and "Elias verily cometh first, and restoreth all things… Elias is indeed come" (Matthew 17:11; Mark 9:12–13). John himself denies it: "Art thou Elias? And he saith, I am not" (John 1:21).
- The crowds guess that Jesus might be Elias (Matthew 16:14); at the cross some say "Let be, let us see whether Elias will come to save him" (Matthew 27:49).
- At the Transfiguration "there appeared unto them Moses and Elias talking with him", and Peter offers three tabernacles (Matthew 17:3–4; Mark 9:4–5; Luke 9:30–33).
- James 5:17–18: "Elias was a man subject to like passions as we are, and he prayed earnestly that it might not rain: and it rained not on the earth by the space of three years and six months."

## Relationships

- **[Elisha](elisha.md)**: his successor and servant (30 shared verses). **Obadiah**, the steward of Ahab's house.
- **Ahab** and **[Jezebel](jezebel.md)**: king and queen he opposes (5 shared verses with Jezebel). **Ahaziah**: the king he confronts. **Jehu** and **Hazael**: the kings he is to anoint.
- **[Moses](moses.md)**: 7 shared verses (Transfiguration, Malachi's pairing of the Law and the prophets). **[Jesus](jesus.md)**, **[John the Baptist](john_baptist.md)** and **[Peter](peter.md)** (4) appear with him in the Gospels.
- No family is recorded.
