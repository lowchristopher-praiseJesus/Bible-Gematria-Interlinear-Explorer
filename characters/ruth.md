## Who Ruth is

The book of Ruth is entirely about her (62 of her 63 links), set "in the days when the judges ruled" (Ruth 1:1). The one other link is the genealogy in Matthew 1:5. She is one of the two women in Matthew's genealogy who came from outside Israel (with [Rahab](rahab.md)).

## What the verses tell us

**Moab.** A famine drives Elimelech, his wife Naomi and their two sons Mahlon and Chilion from Bethlehem to Moab (1:1–2). Elimelech dies (1:3). The sons "took them wives of the women of Moab; the name of the one was Orpah, and the name of the other Ruth" (1:4). After about ten years both sons die too, and Naomi is "left of her two sons and her husband" (1:4–5).

**The road back.** Naomi hears "the LORD had visited his people in giving them bread" and sets out with her daughters-in-law (1:6–7). She urges them to go home: "the LORD deal kindly with you, as ye have dealt with the dead, and with me… The LORD grant you that ye may find rest, each of you in the house of her husband" (1:8–9). They weep. "Orpah kissed her mother in law; but Ruth clave unto her" (1:14). Naomi says "thy sister in law is gone back unto her people, and unto her gods: return thou after thy sister in law" (1:15). Ruth's answer is one of the most quoted in the Bible: "Intreat me not to leave thee… for whither thou goest, I will go; and where thou lodgest, I will lodge: thy people shall be my people, and thy God my God: Where thou diest, will I die, and there will I be buried: the LORD do so to me, and more also, if ought but death part thee and me" (1:16–17). They come to Bethlehem "in the beginning of barley harvest", and the town says "Is this Naomi?" (1:19, 1:22).

**Gleaning.** Ruth asks to "glean ears of corn after him in whose sight I shall find grace" and by chance lands in the field of Boaz, "of the kindred of Elimelech" (2:2–3). Boaz asks whose damsel she is, and is told "the Moabitish damsel that came back with Naomi" (2:5–6). He tells her to stay by his maidens, warns the young men not to touch her, and lets her drink from their vessels (2:8–9). She "fell on her face… seeing I am a stranger" (2:10). He answers that he knows all she has done for her mother-in-law: "how thou hast left thy father and thy mother, and the land of thy nativity… The LORD recompense thy work… under whose wings thou art come to trust" (2:11–12). He feeds her, has the young men leave handfuls on purpose (2:14–16), and she takes an ephah of barley home (2:17). Naomi says "The man is near of kin unto us, one of our next kinsmen" (2:20). Ruth stays there through both harvests (2:23).

**The threshing floor.** Naomi plans: "shall I not seek rest for thee?" and sends her to wash, anoint, dress, and lie at Boaz's feet after he has eaten (3:1–4). Ruth: "All that thou sayest unto me I will do" (3:5). At midnight Boaz wakes to find a woman at his feet: "Who art thou? And she answered, I am Ruth thine handmaid: spread therefore thy skirt over thine handmaid; for thou art a near kinsman" (3:8–9). His response: "Blessed be thou of the LORD, my daughter… thou followedst not young men, whether poor or rich… all the city of my people doth know that thou art a virtuous woman" (3:10–11). There is a nearer kinsman; if he declines, "as the LORD liveth" Boaz will do it (3:12–13). She leaves before dawn with six measures of barley (3:14–15). Naomi: "the man will not be in rest, until he have finished the thing this day" (3:18).

**Redemption.** At the gate Boaz tells the nearer kinsman that buying Naomi's field means buying "Ruth the Moabitess, the wife of the dead, to raise up the name of the dead upon his inheritance" (4:5). The kinsman gives way. Boaz declares before the elders: "Ruth the Moabitess, the wife of Mahlon, have I purchased to be my wife" (4:10). The people bless the marriage: "The LORD make the woman that is come into thine house like Rachel and like Leah, which two did build the house of Israel… let thy house be like the house of Pharez, whom Tamar bare unto Judah" (4:11–12).

**Obed.** "Boaz took Ruth, and she was his wife: and… the LORD gave her conception, and she bare a son" (4:13). The women tell Naomi that Ruth, "which loveth thee, which is better to thee than seven sons", has given her a "restorer of thy life" (4:14–15). Naomi takes the child to her bosom (4:16). "They called his name Obed: he is the father of Jesse, the father of David" (4:17), and the book ends with the line from Pharez to David (4:18–22).

**Matthew 1:5.** "Salmon begat Booz of Rachab; and Booz begat Obed of Ruth; and Obed begat Jesse", which places Ruth in the line of [David](david.md) and [Jesus](jesus.md).

## Relationships

- **Naomi**: mother-in-law, the woman Ruth stays with. **Orpah**: the sister-in-law who went back.
- **Mahlon**: first husband, who died in Moab (1:4–5, 4:10). **Boaz**: second husband and her kinsman-redeemer.
- **Obed**: her son, father of **Jesse**, grandfather of **[David](david.md)**.
- **[Rahab](rahab.md)**: Boaz's mother in Matthew 1:5 (TIPNR marks it uncertain); the two share that one verse.
