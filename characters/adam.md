## Who Adam is

Adam is the first human being in the Bible. His story is told almost entirely in Genesis 1–5 (37 of his 53 linked verses), and then he is used as a reference point by Paul (Romans, 1 Corinthians, 1 Timothy). "Adam" is also the ordinary Hebrew word for "man", which is why TIPNR records his name as "the man", "mankind" and "men" in places where the KJV does not print a name at all.

## What the verses tell us

**Made and placed in the garden.** "The LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul" (Genesis 2:7). God put him in Eden "to dress it and to keep it" (2:15), gave him the one prohibition: the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, "in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die" (2:17). Male and female are blessed together and told to "be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth, and subdue it" (1:28).

**Namer of creatures, and the need for a partner.** God says "It is not good that the man should be alone" (2:18) and brings the animals to Adam, who names them, "but for Adam there was not found an help meet for him" (2:19–20). God then takes a rib while Adam sleeps and builds a woman (2:21–22). Adam's response is the first recorded human speech: "This is now bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh: she shall be called Woman" (2:23). Verse 2:24 draws the marriage moral: a man leaves his parents and cleaves to his wife.

**The fall.** Eve takes the fruit and "gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat" (3:6). Both realise they are naked and sew fig-leaf aprons (3:7), then hide when they hear God in the garden (3:8). God's question "Where art thou?" (3:9) gets a frightened answer, and then Adam shifts the blame: "The woman whom thou gavest to be with me, she gave me of the tree, and I did eat" (3:12). God's sentence on Adam is specific: because he "hearkened unto the voice of thy wife", the ground is cursed, he will eat in sorrow and by "the sweat of thy face" until he returns to dust (3:17–19).

**Named his wife, clothed and expelled.** Right after the sentence Adam calls his wife Eve, "because she was the mother of all living" (3:20). God makes them coats of skins (3:21), then drives the man out and places cherubim and a flaming sword at the east of the garden to guard the tree of life (3:22–24).

**Father of three named sons and more.** "Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived, and bare Cain" (4:1). After Abel's murder by Cain, Adam has another son, Seth, "instead of Abel, whom Cain slew" (4:25). Genesis 5 repeats the creation ("This is the book of the generations of Adam", 5:1), says Seth was born when Adam was 130 (5:3), that he had "sons and daughters" after that (5:4), and that "all the days that Adam lived were nine hundred and thirty years: and he died" (5:5).

**Adam in the New Testament.** Paul uses him as the head of the old humanity:
- *Romans 5:12–19:* "by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin" (5:12); "death reigned from Adam to Moses" (5:14); Adam "is the figure of him that was to come", i.e. Jesus. The pattern is set as a contrast: "by one man's disobedience many were made sinners, so by the obedience of one shall many be made righteous" (5:19).
- *1 Corinthians 15:22, 45–47:* "as in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive"; "The first man Adam was made a living soul; the last Adam was made a quickening spirit"; "The first man is of the earth, earthy: the second man is the Lord from heaven".
- *1 Corinthians 11:8–9:* used to argue the order of creation between man and woman.
- *1 Timothy 2:13–14:* "Adam was first formed, then Eve", and "Adam was not deceived, but the woman being deceived was in the transgression."

## Relationships

- **[Eve](eve.md)**: his wife, formed from his rib (Genesis 2:22) and named by him (3:20). They share 18 of the verses linked here, the whole of Genesis 2–4.
- **[Cain](cain.md) and [Abel](abel.md)**: sons (Genesis 4:1–2). **Seth**: a third son, born after Abel's death (4:25, 5:3). TIPNR lists these three by name and adds no others; Genesis 5:4 only says there were more "sons and daughters".
- **God**: he speaks to Adam directly in Eden (2:16–17, 3:9–19); the relationship is one of commands, blame and expulsion, but God also clothes him (3:21).
- **[Jesus](jesus.md)**: the "last Adam" and "second man" of Paul's comparison (1 Corinthians 15:45–47; Romans 5:14).

## Reading the data

- Adam is linked to Genesis 1:28–29, which speak of "them" (man and woman together), and to Genesis 5:1–2, where "Adam" is the name given to both.
- The lower-probability links (about 0.5–0.67) are the ones where "man" or "one" is a theological type rather than a named person: Psalm 8:6 ("thou madest him to have dominion", mankind in general), Romans 5:15–17 and 1 Corinthians 11:8–9, 15:22, 15:48. Read them as Adam as the head of humanity, not as narrative about the individual.
- TIPNR's sibling field lists Eve as Adam's "sibling" as well as his partner. That is a quirk of the dataset (she was formed from him, not born to his parents), not a claim of the text.
