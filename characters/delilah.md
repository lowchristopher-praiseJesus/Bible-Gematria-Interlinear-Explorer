## Who Delilah is

Delilah is named six times, all in Judges 16, and every one of her 17 linked verses is in that scene. The text calls her only "a woman in the valley of Sorek, whose name was Delilah" (16:4). It does not say she is a Philistine, though the Philistine lords come to her and she works for them; nor that she married Samson, though TIPNR lists her as his wife.

## What the verses tell us

**The offer.** "The lords of the Philistines came up unto her, and said unto her, Entice him, and see wherein his great strength lieth, and by what means we may prevail against him, that we may bind him to afflict him: and we will give thee every one of us eleven hundred pieces of silver" (Judges 16:5). Since each lord pays that amount, the total is very large.

**Three failed tests.** She asks him directly: "Tell me, I pray thee, wherein thy great strength lieth, and wherewith thou mightest be bound to afflict thee" (16:6). Samson names seven green withs (16:7); she has the lords bring them and ties him up, with men "lying in wait, abiding with her in the chamber", and cries "The Philistines be upon thee, Samson" (16:8–9). He snaps them. Then new ropes (16:11–12), and then the seven locks woven into a web and pinned (16:13–14). After each: "Behold, thou hast mocked me, and told me lies" (16:10, 16:13).

**The argument.** "How canst thou say, I love thee, when thine heart is not with me? thou hast mocked me these three times, and hast not told me wherein thy great strength lieth" (16:15). She "pressed him daily with her words, and urged him, so that his soul was vexed unto death" (16:16). He tells her "all his heart", including the Nazirite vow (16:17).

**The betrayal.** "When Delilah saw that he had told her all his heart, she sent and called for the lords of the Philistines, saying, Come up this once, for he hath shewed me all his heart. Then the lords of the Philistines came up unto her, and brought money in their hand" (16:18). "She made him sleep upon her knees; and she called for a man, and she caused him to shave off the seven locks of his head; and she began to afflict him, and his strength went from him" (16:19). She cries again "The Philistines be upon thee, Samson", and "he wist not that the LORD was departed from him" (16:20). The narrative then follows Samson alone: the Philistines take him, blind him, and set him to grind (16:21). Delilah is not mentioned again.

## Relationships

- **[Samson](samson.md)**: the man she betrays. All 17 of her links are shared with his.
- **The lords of the Philistines**: her paymasters.
- No parents, spouse in the text, or children are given for her; TIPNR lists Samson as her "partner" by inference from "he loved" her.
