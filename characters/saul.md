## Who Saul is

This profile is **King Saul son of Kish**, not Saul of Tarsus (Paul). The classifier keeps them apart: 372 of the 441 links are in 1 Samuel, 40 in 2 Samuel, 24 in 1 Chronicles, only three in the Psalms and two in Acts (13:21, where Paul's sermon recalls the king). Most Saul verses are also David verses (212 shared) and Samuel verses (79), because the story is a triangle.

## What the verses tell us

**A goodly young man.** "Saul, a choice young man, and a goodly: and there was not among the children of Israel a goodlier person than he: from his shoulders and upward he was higher than any of the people" (1 Samuel 9:2). Looking for his father's lost donkeys, he says to his servant "let us return; lest my father leave caring for the asses" (9:5) and wonders what they can bring the "man of God" (9:7). Samuel anoints him with oil (10:1). "God gave him another heart" and "the Spirit of God came upon him, and he prophesied among them" (10:9–10), which gave the proverb "Is Saul also among the prophets?" (10:11). At the public choosing "Saul the son of Kish was taken: and when they sought him, he could not be found" (10:21). When he tells his uncle, he leaves out "the matter of the kingdom, whereof Samuel spake" (10:16).

**First victory.** When Jabesh-gilead is threatened, "Saul came after the herd out of the field… And the Spirit of God came upon Saul… and his anger was kindled greatly" (11:5–6). He cuts a yoke of oxen into pieces and sends them round Israel (11:7). Samuel calls the people to Gilgal to "renew the kingdom" (11:14).

**Loss of the kingdom.** With the Philistines at Michmash he waits seven days for Samuel; the people begin to scatter and he offers the burnt offering himself (13:8–10). Samuel: "Thou hast done foolishly… now thy kingdom shall not continue" (13:13–14). In the Amalek campaign (15:4, 200,000 footmen and 10,000 of Judah) "Saul and the people spared Agag, and the best of the sheep, and of the oxen… and would not utterly destroy them" (15:9). Samuel: "to obey is better than sacrifice… Because thou hast rejected the word of the LORD, he hath also rejected thee from being king" (15:22–23). Samuel never sees him again (15:35).

**The evil spirit and the harp.** "An evil spirit from God troubleth thee", his servants say (16:15), and he asks for a man "that can play well" (16:17); David is brought.

**David and Goliath.** Saul and Israel are at the valley of Elah (17:2). He hears David say "thy servant will go and fight with this Philistine" (17:32) and arms him in his own armour, which David cannot walk in (17:38–39). He sets David "over the men of war" and David is accepted by all (18:5).

**Jealousy.** As they return, the women sing "Saul hath slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands" (18:7), and Saul is "very wroth… and what can he have more but the kingdom?" (18:8). The next day the evil spirit comes on him and he hurls his javelin at David twice (18:10–11). He gives his daughter Michal, "David's wife", because "the thing pleased him" (18:20). He tells Jonathan and his servants to kill David (19:1); Jonathan pleads and Saul swears "As the LORD liveth, he shall not be slain" (19:6), and then tries again. At the new-moon feast, when David's place is empty, "Saul spake not any thing that day" (20:26).

**The hunt.** David flees "for fear of Saul" (21:10). Saul, in Gibeah with his spear, accuses his Benjamite servants of conspiring: "will the son of Jesse give every one of you fields and vineyards?" (22:6–7). When the footmen refuse to kill the priests who helped David, he tells Doeg, who kills "fourscore and five persons that did wear a linen ephod" (22:17–18). He pursues David to Keilah (23:7–8), Ziph (26:2) and En-gedi. In the cave "Saul went in to cover his feet" while David and his men hid at the back (24:3). David refuses to touch him: "the LORD forbid that I should do this thing unto my master, the LORD's anointed" (24:6). In 26:17 Saul recognises his voice: "Is this thy voice, my son David?". He gives Michal to Phalti (25:44). Eventually David is in Philistine service and Saul "sought no more again for him" (27:4).

**Endor and Gilboa.** Facing the Philistines at Shunem/Gilboa, Saul "was afraid, and his heart greatly trembled" (28:4–5). With God silent, he orders "Seek me a woman that hath a familiar spirit" (28:7). She raises Samuel, who tells him the LORD has become his enemy (28:14–16). In the battle "the archers hit him… then said Saul unto his armourbearer, Draw thy sword, and thrust me through… But his armourbearer would not… Therefore Saul took a sword, and fell upon it" (31:3–4). "Saul died, and his three sons, and his armourbearer, and all his men, that same day together" (31:6). The men of Jabesh-gilead remember his first rescue: they take the bodies from the wall of Bethshan, burn them and bury the bones under a tree (31:11–13).

**After him.** David's lament: "Saul and Jonathan were lovely and pleasant in their lives, and in their death they were not divided: they were swifter than eagles, they were stronger than lions" (2 Samuel 1:23). David blesses Jabesh for burying "your lord, even unto Saul" (2:5). Abner backs Saul's son Ishbosheth (3:8), and the young man who claimed to have killed Saul is himself executed (4:10). At Hebron the tribes say "when Saul was king over us, thou wast he that leddest out and broughtest in Israel" (5:2). Nathan reminds David "I delivered thee out of the hand of Saul" (12:7). David makes the promise to Solomon "my mercy shall not depart away from him, as I took it from Saul, whom I put away before thee" (7:15). Saul's bones and Jonathan's are re-buried in Zelah in his father Kish's tomb (21:13–14). Chronicles reads the genealogy "Ner begat Kish, and Kish begat Saul, and Saul begat Jonathan, and Malchishua, and Abinadab, and Eshbaal" (1 Chronicles 8:33) and notes his death (10:3–4). Acts 13:21: "God gave unto them Saul the son of Cis, a man of the tribe of Benjamin, by the space of forty years".

## Relationships

- **Kish**: father, of the tribe of Benjamin. **Ahinoam**: wife; **Rizpah**: concubine (2 Samuel 3:7, 21:8–11).
- **[Jonathan](jonathan.md)**: eldest son and David's friend (72 shared verses); other sons **Abinadab**, **Malchi-shua** and **Ish-bosheth**, and daughters **Merab** and **Michal**.
- **[Samuel](samuel.md)**: kingmaker and critic (79 shared verses). **[David](david.md)**: rival and successor (212 shared verses); **[Goliath](goliath.md)** shares 8.
- **Abner** (cousin and commander), **Doeg the Edomite** (informer), **Achish**, and the Philistines.
- **[Solomon](solomon.md)** (2 shared verses) and **[Bathsheba](bathsheba.md)** (1) appear where later writers contrast him with David.

## Reading the data

- Some links are to *the king* in 1 Samuel 8 (8:15 at 0.59: "he will take the tenth of your seed") and 2:10, which describe kings in general or God's "anointed" and only later fit Saul.
- Psalms 7:14, 59:12 and 89:46 (0.52–0.55) are weak: they are Psalm verses where scholars connect a psalm to Saul only through its title, which is not in this text.
- The classifier misses 44 named verses (mostly "the house of Saul", "Saul's son", "Saul's daughter": 2 Samuel 2:8, 2:10, 3:1, 4:2, 6:20, 21:6–11, Isaiah 10:29 "Gibeah of Saul").
