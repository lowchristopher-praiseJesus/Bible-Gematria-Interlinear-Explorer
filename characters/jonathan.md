## Who Jonathan is

This profile is **Jonathan son of Saul**. Jonathan is a common name; the classifier's description ties this entry to Saul's son. 88 of his 110 links are in 1 Samuel, and he is remembered in 2 Samuel through David's lament and his son Mephibosheth.

## What the verses tell us

**Warrior prince.** Saul keeps three thousand men: "two thousand were with Saul in Michmash and in mount Bethel, and a thousand were with Jonathan in Gibeah of Benjamin" (1 Samuel 13:2). "Jonathan smote the garrison of the Philistines that was in Geba" (13:3). In the disarmament under Philistine control, only Saul and Jonathan have a sword or spear in the day of battle (13:22).

**The climb at Michmash.** "Jonathan the son of Saul said unto the young man that bare his armour, Come, and let us go over to the Philistines' garrison… But he told not his father" (14:1). His reason: "it may be that the LORD will work for us: for there is no restraint to the LORD to save by many or by few" (14:6). The armourbearer: "Do all that is in thine heart… I am with thee according to thy heart" (14:7). They climb "upon his hands and upon his feet" and kill about twenty men (14:13–14).

**The honey.** He does not hear his father's oath that no one may eat, and "put forth the end of the rod that was in his hand, and dipped it in an honeycomb… and his eyes were enlightened" (14:27). "My father hath troubled the land" (14:29). When the lot falls on him and Saul says "thou shalt surely die, Jonathan" (14:42–44), the people say "Shall Jonathan die, who hath wrought this great salvation in Israel? God forbid: as the LORD liveth, there shall not one hair of his head fall to the ground" and rescue him (14:45).

**David.** "The soul of Jonathan was knit with the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul" (18:1). They make a covenant, and Jonathan "stripped himself of the robe that was upon him, and gave it to David, and his garments, even to his sword, and to his bow, and to his girdle" (18:3–4). When Saul orders David killed, Jonathan warns him: "Saul my father seeketh to kill thee… take heed to thyself until the morning" (19:1–2), speaks up for him ("he did put his life in his hand, and slew the Philistine… wherefore then wilt thou sin against innocent blood, to slay David without a cause?", 19:4–5), and brings him back to court (19:7).

**The test of the new moon.** David asks "What have I done? what is mine iniquity?" (20:1) and Jonathan answers "Whatsoever thy soul desireth, I will even do it for thee" (20:4). He explains the arrow signal and makes a covenant "with the house of David" (20:16–17). At the feast David's place is empty (20:25). Saul explodes "Thou son of the perverse rebellious woman, do not I know that thou hast chosen the son of Jesse to thine own confusion?" (20:30); Jonathan asks "Wherefore shall he be slain? what hath he done?" (20:32) and leaves "in fierce anger… for he was grieved for David" (20:34). The next morning he shoots arrows with a boy, sends him back to the city and meets David for a farewell in which both weep (20:35–42).

**In the wood.** Later "Jonathan Saul's son arose, and went to David into the wood, and strengthened his hand in God": "Fear not: for the hand of Saul my father shall not find thee; and thou shalt be king over Israel, and I shall be next unto thee; and that also Saul my father knoweth" (23:16–17). They renew their covenant, "and David abode in the wood, and Jonathan went to his house" (23:18). It is the last time they meet.

**Gilboa.** "The Philistines followed hard upon Saul and upon his sons; and the Philistines slew Jonathan, and Abinadab, and Melchishua, Saul's sons" (31:2). The bodies are hung on the wall of Bethshan (31:8–10); the men of Jabesh-gilead bury their bones (31:12–13). David's lament: "Saul and Jonathan were lovely and pleasant in their lives, and in their death they were not divided… O Jonathan, thou wast slain in thine high places. I am distressed for thee, my brother Jonathan: very pleasant hast thou been unto me: thy love to me was wonderful, passing the love of women" (2 Samuel 1:23–26). David later collects the bones of Saul and Jonathan and buries them in Kish's tomb at Zelah (21:12–14).

**His son.** "Jonathan, Saul's son, had a son that was lame of his feet… And his name was Mephibosheth" (4:4): his nurse dropped him when fleeing the news of Gilboa. David seeks him out "for Jonathan thy father's sake" (9:1–7, not linked here). 1 Chronicles 8:33–34 and 9:39–40 trace his line: "the son of Jonathan was Meribbaal; and Meribbaal begat Micah."

## Relationships

- **[Saul](saul.md)** (father; 72 shared verses) and **Ahinoam** (mother). Siblings: **Abinadab**, **Malchi-shua**, **Merab**, **Michal** (David's first wife), and half-brothers **Ish-bosheth**, **Armoni** and **Mephibosheth** (TIPNR).

## Reading the data

- Three namesake links were removed from the database: **2 Samuel 17:17–18** ("Jonathan and Ahimaaz stayed by Enrogel", Jonathan son of Abiathar) and **Jeremiah 38:26** ("Jonathan's house", another man). Every remaining link belongs to Saul's son.
- Chronicles verses about Saul's death (10:2, 10:6, 10:8, 10:12) are correctly linked, with the same events as 1 Samuel 31.
- Named but not linked (0.16–0.48): 2 Samuel 9:1, 9:3, 9:6–7, 21:7, which are about kindness "for Jonathan's sake" (Mephibosheth).
