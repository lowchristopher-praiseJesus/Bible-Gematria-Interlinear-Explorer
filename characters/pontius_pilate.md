## Who Pilate is

Pontius Pilate was governor (prefect) of Judaea under Tiberius Caesar (Luke 3:1). All but a handful of his links are in the four trial narratives and the burial and guard scenes (Matthew 18, Mark 14, Luke 22, John 28); the other three are in Acts, where the apostles look back at what he did. Nearly every verse is at 0.90 or above (74 of 85).

## What the verses tell us

**Governor.** "In the fifteenth year of the reign of Tiberius Caesar, Pontius Pilate being governor of Judaea" (Luke 3:1). Some report to Jesus "of the Galilaeans, whose blood Pilate had mingled with their sacrifices" (13:1).

**Handed over.** "When they had bound him, they led him away, and delivered him to Pontius Pilate the governor" (Matthew 27:2). Luke: "the whole multitude of them arose, and led him unto Pilate" (23:1). John's account begins outside the judgment hall: "Pilate then went out unto them, and said, What accusation bring ye against this man? They answered… If he were not a malefactor, we would not have delivered him up unto thee. Then said Pilate unto them, Take ye him, and judge him according to your law. The Jews therefore said unto him, It is not lawful for us to put any man to death" (John 18:29–31).

**The question.** "Art thou the King of the Jews?" is Pilate's question in all four Gospels. Jesus answers "Thou sayest" (Matthew 27:11; Mark 15:2; Luke 23:3). In John he asks, "Sayest thou this thing of thyself, or did others tell it thee of me?" and Pilate replies "Am I a Jew? Thine own nation and the chief priests have delivered thee unto me: what hast thou done?" (18:34–35). "Art thou a king then? Jesus answered, Thou sayest that I am a king. To this end was I born, and for this cause came I into the world, that I should bear witness unto the truth. Every one that is of the truth heareth my voice. Pilate saith unto him, What is truth?" (18:37–38). He then goes out and says "I find in him no fault at all" (18:38).

**Silence and wonder.** "Hearest thou not how many things they witness against thee? And he answered him to never a word; insomuch that the governor marvelled greatly" (Matthew 27:13–14; Mark 15:4–5). Later in John, "Whence art thou? But Jesus gave him no answer. Then saith Pilate unto him, Speakest thou not unto me? knowest thou not that I have power to crucify thee, and have power to release thee?" and Jesus replies "Thou couldest have no power at all against me, except it were given thee from above: therefore he that delivered me unto thee hath the greater sin" (19:9–11).

**Herod.** "When Pilate heard of Galilee, he asked whether the man were a Galilaean. And as soon as he knew that he belonged unto Herod's jurisdiction, he sent him to Herod" (Luke 23:6–7). "The same day Pilate and Herod were made friends together: for before they were at enmity between themselves" (23:12).

**Innocent, three times.** "I find no fault in this man" (Luke 23:4); "I, having examined him before you, have found no fault in this man touching those things whereof ye accuse him" (23:14); "I have found no cause of death in him: I will therefore chastise him and let him go" (23:22). "Pilate therefore, willing to release Jesus, spake again to them" (23:20). His wife sends: "Have thou nothing to do with that just man: for I have suffered many things this day in a dream because of him" (Matthew 27:19). He knew "that the chief priests had delivered him for envy" (Mark 15:10).

**Barabbas.** "Ye have a custom, that I should release unto you one at the passover: will ye therefore that I release unto you the King of the Jews?" (John 18:39; Mark 15:6–9). "Whom will ye that I release unto you? Barabbas, or Jesus which is called Christ?" (Matthew 27:17). The crowd: "Barabbas" (27:21). "What shall I do then with Jesus which is called Christ? They all say unto him, Let him be crucified" (27:22). "Why, what evil hath he done? But they cried out the more" (27:23; Mark 15:14).

**Scourged and shown.** "Pilate therefore took Jesus, and scourged him" (John 19:1). "Behold, I bring him forth to you, that ye may know that I find no fault in him" (19:4). "Then came Jesus forth, wearing the crown of thorns, and the purple robe. And Pilate saith unto them, Behold the man!" (19:5). To "Crucify him", he says "Take ye him, and crucify him: for I find no fault in him" (19:6). "When Pilate therefore heard that saying, he was the more afraid" (19:8).

**The verdict.** The crowd: "If thou let this man go, thou art not Caesar's friend: whosoever maketh himself a king speaketh against Caesar" (19:12). "He brought Jesus forth, and sat down in the judgment seat in a place that is called the Pavement, but in the Hebrew, Gabbatha" (19:13). "Shall I crucify your King? The chief priests answered, We have no king but Caesar" (19:15). Pilate "took water, and washed his hands before the multitude, saying, I am innocent of the blood of this just person: see ye to it" (Matthew 27:24). "So Pilate, willing to content the people, released Barabbas unto them, and delivered Jesus, when he had scourged him, to be crucified" (Mark 15:15). "Pilate gave sentence that it should be as they required" (Luke 23:24), and "then delivered he him therefore unto them to be crucified" (John 19:16).

**The title.** "Pilate wrote a title, and put it on the cross. And the writing was, JESUS OF NAZARETH THE KING OF THE JEWS" (John 19:19). When the chief priests object, "Pilate answered, What I have written I have written" (19:22).

**Burial and the guard.** The Jews ask that the legs be broken (John 19:31). "Joseph of Arimathaea, an honourable counsellor… came, and went in boldly unto Pilate, and craved the body of Jesus. And Pilate marvelled if he were already dead: and calling unto him the centurion, he asked him whether he had been any while dead. And when he knew it of the centurion, he gave the body to Joseph" (Mark 15:43–45; Matthew 27:58; John 19:38). The next day the chief priests and Pharisees come to him about the tomb: "Ye have a watch: go your way, make it as sure as ye can" (Matthew 27:62, 27:65). The soldiers' priests promise "if this come to the governor's ears, we will persuade him, and secure you" (28:14).

**Afterwards.** Peter says "ye delivered up, and denied him in the presence of Pilate, when he was determined to let him go" (Acts 3:13). The believers pray that "against thy holy child Jesus… both Herod, and Pontius Pilate, with the Gentiles, and the people of Israel, were gathered together" (4:27). Paul: "though they found no cause of death in him, yet desired they Pilate that he should be slain" (13:28). 1 Timothy 6:13 ("Christ Jesus, who before Pontius Pilate witnessed a good confession") scored 0.30.

## Relationships

- **[Jesus](jesus.md)**: the prisoner he judges (69 shared verses).
- **Herod Antipas**: the tetrarch he sends Jesus to; **Caiaphas** and the chief priests: the accusers. **Barabbas**, released in Jesus' place.
- **Pilate's wife**: sends the warning about her dream (Matthew 27:19).
- **Joseph of Arimathaea**: asks him for the body. **Tiberius Caesar** is the emperor he serves.
