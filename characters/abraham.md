## Who Abraham is

Abraham (called Abram until Genesis 17:5) is the most-cited person in the Bible after Moses, David and Jesus. Genesis 11–25 tells his life (249 of his 351 linked verses); the rest of the Old Testament recalls him as the source of Israel's covenant, and the New Testament argues from him about faith, works, circumcision and who counts as his children (Romans 4, Galatians 3–4, Hebrews 6–11, James 2, John 8).

## What the verses tell us

**Origins and call.** "Terah lived seventy years, and begat Abram, Nahor, and Haran" (Genesis 11:26). Terah takes Abram, his grandson Lot and Sarai out of Ur of the Chaldees "to go into the land of Canaan" but they settle in Haran (11:31). Stephen later says "The God of glory appeared unto our father Abraham, when he was in Mesopotamia" and told him "Get thee out of thy country" (Acts 7:2–3). Abram leaves Haran at seventy-five (Genesis 12:4). Joshua 24:3 sums up: "I took your father Abraham from the other side of the flood, and led him throughout all the land of Canaan, and multiplied his seed, and gave him Isaac."

**A worshipper who moves.** He builds altars and "called upon the name of the LORD" at Bethel and elsewhere (12:8, 13:4). In Egypt he passes Sarai off as his sister (12:11–13, see [Sarah](sarah.md)); the LORD plagues Pharaoh (12:17). Later at Gerar he does the same (20:2).

**Generosity and courage.** He gives Lot first choice of land (13:8–9). When Lot is captured he arms "three hundred and eighteen" trained servants, pursues "unto Dan" and by night beats the kings (14:14–15). Melchizedek, king of Salem, blesses him "Blessed be Abram of the most high God" (14:19), and Abram refuses the king of Sodom's goods with an oath (14:22). Hebrews 7:1–6 reads this scene: Abraham "gave a tenth part of all" to Melchizedek, who "blessed him that had the promises".

**Promise and doubt.** "Abram said, LORD God, what wilt thou give me, seeing I go childless, and the steward of my house is this Eliezer of Damascus?" (15:2). God answers that his heir will come "out of thine own bowels" (15:4) and foretells four hundred years of affliction for his seed in a land not theirs (15:13). Paul's core text is here: "Abraham believed God, and it was counted unto him for righteousness" (Romans 4:3, Galatians 3:6, James 2:23, quoting Genesis 15).

**Hagar.** After ten years in Canaan Sarai gives Abram her Egyptian maid Hagar "to be his wife" (16:2–3). Hagar conceives and Sarai's jealousy follows (16:4–5).

**Covenant, name and circumcision.** God: "Thou shalt keep my covenant therefore, thou, and thy seed after thee" (17:9); Sarai becomes Sarah (17:15). Abraham "fell upon his face, and laughed" at the promise of a son at a hundred (17:17), and asks "O that Ishmael might live before thee!" (17:18). He circumcises Ishmael and every male of his house "in the selfsame day" (17:23). Paul reads this as a seal: he "received the sign of circumcision, a seal of the righteousness of the faith which he had yet being uncircumcised" (Romans 4:11), and God's words "I have made thee a father of many nations" (4:17).

**Hospitality and intercession.** He runs to fetch a calf and meal for three visitors (18:6–7). As they turn toward Sodom he "stood yet before the LORD" and pleads "Wilt thou also destroy the righteous with the wicked?" (18:22–23). Afterwards he watches "the smoke of the country went up as the smoke of a furnace" (19:28), and "God remembered Abraham" when he saved Lot (19:29).

**Isaac, Ishmael and the test.** Isaac is born when Abraham is a hundred (21:5); Abraham circumcises him on the eighth day (21:4) and feasts at the weaning (21:8). Sarah demands the expulsion of Hagar and Ishmael; God says "hearken unto her voice: for in Isaac shall thy seed be called" (21:12). Then "God did tempt Abraham": "Take now thy son, thine only son Isaac, whom thou lovest… offer him there for a burnt offering" (22:1–2). He rises early, saddles the ass, splits the wood and goes (22:3), and the ram replaces the boy (22:13). New Testament readings: "By faith Abraham, when he was tried, offered up Isaac" (Hebrews 11:17); "justified by works, when he had offered Isaac his son upon the altar" (James 2:21).

**Death of Sarah, a wife for Isaac.** He weeps for Sarah (23:2), bows to the sons of Heth, and buys the cave and field of Machpelah for a burying place (23:7, 23:13, 49:30). "Abraham was old, and well stricken in age: and the LORD had blessed Abraham in all things" (24:1). He makes his chief servant swear not to take a wife for Isaac from Canaanites and not to bring Isaac back to Mesopotamia (24:2–8). He later marries Keturah (25:1), gives "all that he had unto Isaac" and only gifts to the sons of his concubines (25:5–6), and dies "in a good old age, an old man, and full of years" (25:8). Isaac and Ishmael bury him at Machpelah (25:9).

**God's reason.** To Isaac God says "Abraham obeyed my voice, and kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws" (26:5).

**A name in every generation.** Israel's prayers and covenant language keep returning to him: "the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob" (Exodus 3:15–16, Mark 12:26, Luke 13:28); Moses's plea "Remember Abraham, Isaac, and Israel" (Exodus 32:13, Deuteronomy 9:27); Jehoshaphat calls him God's "friend" (2 Chronicles 20:7, Isaiah 41:8, James 2:23); Isaiah 51:2 "Look unto Abraham your father, and unto Sarah that bare you"; Micah 7:20 "the mercy to Abraham"; Nehemiah 9:7–8 recalls how God "didst choose Abram… and gavest him the name of Abraham".

**In the Gospels and Acts.** Matthew 1:2 starts the genealogy with "Abraham begat Isaac". Luke 16:24–30 has the rich man call "Father Abraham" from torment and Abraham answer "They have Moses and the prophets". In John 8:39–56 Jesus argues with those who say "Abraham is our father": "If ye were Abraham's children, ye would do the works of Abraham… Your father Abraham rejoiced to see my day: and he saw it, and was glad." Acts 3:25 quotes the promise "in thy seed shall all the kindreds of the earth be blessed", which Paul explains as pointing to one seed "which is Christ" (Galatians 3:8, 3:16).

- **[Lot](lot.md)**: nephew, rescued in Genesis 14 and remembered at Sodom (15 shared verses).
- **Terah** (father), **Nahor** and **Haran** (brothers).
- **Melchizedek**, **Pharaoh**, **Abimelech**, and the sons of Heth are the other named figures he deals with.
- **[Jesus](jesus.md)**: shares 9 verses (e.g. John 8, Luke 13, Matthew 1).

## Reading the data

- Most of the 351 links are secure: the Genesis narrative is explicit and the New Testament arguments name him.
- The 0.50–0.65 links are mostly formulas like "the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob" or verses about Abraham's seed (Exodus 3, Deuteronomy 1:8 and 9:5, Luke 1:55, Romans 9:7, Acts 13:26); read those as *invoking* him, not narrating him.
- Around 60 TIPNR-named verses fell just under the threshold: Genesis 14:12, Matthew 22:32 and 3:9, John 8:53 and 8:58 ("Before Abraham was, I am") and 1 Chronicles 1:28.
