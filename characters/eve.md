## Who Eve is

Eve appears in 26 verses of Genesis 1–5 and in three verses of 1 Timothy 2. Before Genesis 3:20 she is simply "the woman" or "his wife"; the name Eve is given to her by Adam only after the fall.

## What the verses tell us

**Made as a partner.** God says of Adam "I will make him an help meet for him" (Genesis 2:18). He builds a woman from Adam's rib and brings her to him (2:22). Adam's first words on seeing her: "bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh: she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man" (2:23). The passage closes with the pair "both naked… and were not ashamed" (2:25). Genesis 1:27–28 (linked at a lower probability) has male and female blessed together and told to "be fruitful, and multiply".

**The conversation with the serpent.** The serpent "was more subtil than any beast of the field" and speaks to the woman, not the man: "Yea, hath God said, Ye shall not eat of every tree of the garden?" (3:1). She answers with the rule and adds a detail of her own: "neither shall ye touch it, lest ye die" (3:3). The serpent contradicts God ("Ye shall not surely die", 3:4) and tempts her with being "as gods, knowing good and evil" (3:5). She sees that the tree is "good for food… pleasant to the eyes… to be desired to make one wise", eats, and gives some to her husband (3:6). Both then realise they are naked and make aprons of fig leaves (3:7).

**Confronted.** When God asks what she has done, her answer is "The serpent beguiled me, and I did eat" (3:13), so both humans shift blame downwards (Adam blames her in 3:12).

**Judgement.** God's word to the serpent includes "I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel" (3:15). To Eve: "I will greatly multiply thy sorrow and thy conception; in sorrow thou shalt bring forth children; and thy desire shall be to thy husband, and he shall rule over thee" (3:16).

**Named and clothed.** "Adam called his wife's name Eve; because she was the mother of all living" (3:20), and God makes coats of skins for both (3:21).

**Mother.** "Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived, and bare Cain, and said, I have gotten a man from the LORD" (4:1). She then bears Abel, "a keeper of sheep" (4:2). After Cain kills Abel she bears Seth and names him herself: "God, said she, hath appointed me another seed instead of Abel, whom Cain slew" (4:25). Genesis 5:2 records that God "called their name Adam" for the pair.

**Eve in the New Testament.** Only 1 Timothy 2:13–15 is linked here: "Adam was first formed, then Eve", "the woman being deceived was in the transgression", and "she shall be saved in childbearing, if they continue in faith and charity and holiness with sobriety". Paul also refers to her in 2 Corinthians 11:3 ("the serpent beguiled Eve through his subtilty"), which the classifier scored just under the threshold (0.30).

## Relationships

- **[Adam](adam.md)**: husband; they share 18 of the verses in this index.
- **[Cain](cain.md), [Abel](abel.md) and Seth**: her three named sons (Genesis 4:1–2, 4:25).
- **The serpent**: the tempter in Genesis 3; not a person tracked in TIPNR but the counterpart of the "seed of the woman" promise in 3:15.

## Reading the data

- Verses 1:27–29 and 5:2 are borderline (0.55–0.59) because they speak of "them" (male and female) without naming her.
- Everything in Genesis 2–4 that says "the woman", "his wife" or "she" is correctly attached to Eve; none of these are guesses from a name.
- TIPNR gives Adam and Eve as each other's "sibling" as well as partner: a dataset quirk, since Eve was formed from Adam, not born to his parents.
