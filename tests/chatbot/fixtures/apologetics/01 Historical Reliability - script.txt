A professor told his class the Gospels can't be trusted. Too late, he said. Written decades after Jesus died. Basically legends.

Then he held up his favorite book: a biography of Alexander the Great. He tapped the cover. "This is real history."

Alexander died in 323 BC. And the whole lecture hall nodded along.

One student raised her hand. "Sir, when was that written?" He laughed. "Doesn't matter." And the class laughed with him.

That night, she checked.

The earliest surviving history of Alexander was written nearly three hundred years after he died. The one historians trust most, by Arrian, came more than four hundred years later.

Now, Jesus. Paul's letters were written within twenty to thirty years of the cross. He even quotes a resurrection creed many scholars date to within a few years. Mark's Gospel follows within about forty years. All four, within one lifetime.

You see, that early, the eyewitnesses were still alive. Fishermen. Neighbors. Enemies. Invent a miracle, and someone would stand up and say: that never happened.

Next morning, she put both books on his desk. Four hundred years. Forty.

If four hundred years is good enough for Alexander... forty is good enough for Jesus.
