TITLE:
What If the Law Itself Is Wrong? ⚖️ #Shorts

DESCRIPTION:
A city councilman cuts the ribbon on a new civil rights museum. "We don't need God to know what's right," he tells the cameras. "We have laws." 🎤

The old night guard clears his throat. "In 1963, segregation was the law."

He leads him to a jail cell exhibit. On the bunk lies a newspaper, its margins crammed with handwriting. 📰

✍️ April 1963: Martin Luther King Jr. sits in a Birmingham jail.
⛪ Eight local clergymen publish a statement calling the demonstrations "unwise and untimely." Wait for the courts. Respect law and order.
📝 King begins his reply in the margins of that newspaper: the Letter from Birmingham Jail.
📜 "There are two types of laws: just and unjust… I would agree with St. Augustine that 'an unjust law is no law at all.'"
⚖️ "A just law is a man-made code that squares with the moral law or the law of God."

Take that away, and Alabama's segregation laws were just… the laws. Legally passed. Enforced by police. Nothing higher to appeal to.

"So the law isn't the top," the councilman says quietly.
"Never was."

Human rights need a court above the courthouse. 🏛️

Can human rights exist without something higher than government? 👇

📚 Further reading: Martin Luther King Jr., "Letter from Birmingham Jail" (April 16, 1963) · "A Call for Unity" (statement by eight Alabama clergymen, April 12, 1963) · Augustine, On Free Choice of the Will, Book I · Thomas Aquinas, Summa Theologiae I–II, Q. 95–96
(The councilman and the night guard are fictional characters. Dr. King is not depicted.)
